## Setup and goal
Ages 10–15; trio in a straight 2×8 m lane. Teacher marks endpoints 4–6 m apart without revealing distance. Two flat markers, flexible measuring tape and record sheet. Goal: connect estimates, steps and measurement, explaining why step counts differ between people.
## Procedure and timing
12 minutes: 2 explanation, 6 for three 2-minute turns, 2 measurement and comparison, 2 review and collection. Each turn one estimates metres then walks normally between endpoints; one counts steps and one records estimate and count. Rotate so everyone walks. Only after all walking, lay tape straight on floor, measure together and compare estimates. Note a partial final step separately.
## Coaching and safety
No deliberately changing steps to reach a number, jumping or longest-step contest. Tape goes down only when lane is inactive; never stretch at height or leave across route. No ranking by stride length. Teacher confirms units, metres or centimetres, before comparison.
## Adaptations and assessment
Easier: choose among three teacher-written estimates. Harder: calculate average step length as distance divided by count, explaining partial-step limitations. Mobility-aid users may estimate and measure without step counting; do not artificially equate pushes with steps. Success means consistent records and explaining limitations, not a perfect estimate.
