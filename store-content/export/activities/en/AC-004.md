## Setup and goal
Ages 10–15; four pupils in a marked 8×8 m area. Equipment: six flat markers, one soft ball and a sheet with three symbols drawn by the teacher. Goal: design a short route everyone can perform, identify a difficulty and improve it. A standalone design-and-test activity.
## Procedure
15 minutes: 2 explanation, 3 design, 5 testing, 3 revision and retesting, 2 review. Three points: travel between two markers 2 m apart; stop and reach comfortably toward a marker without touching the floor; roll a ball from 2 m through a 1 m gate formed by two markers. The sixth marker indicates the rolling line. Group draws an order and direction arrows without crossing paths. Teacher approves layout before movement starts.
## Roles and safety
Each turn has a route tester, a clear-path checker, a difficulty recorder and a ball collector who retrieves after the ball stops while the tester stays still. The three waiting pupils use a teacher-designated side area outside the ball path. Rotate so everyone tests an accessible version. No racing, raised obstacles, blindfolds, pulling partners or moving area boundaries. If the ball leaves, stop; ask the teacher if retrieval would cross another group.
## Adaptations and assessment
Adapt passage width and turning space to mobility aids, rolling distance and reaching height. Reaching and rolling can be seated; do not require getting down to the floor. Adapt participation rather than always assigning recording to the same person. Change one detail after testing and check whether it helped. Success: a participation route for everyone, no crossing paths, and an observation-based explanation of the change.
