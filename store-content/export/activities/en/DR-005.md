## Identity and setup
Ages 9–13; beginners. Pairs stand an arm's length apart in a 2×3 m area. One light baton per pair; 15 batons for 30 pupils. No running.
## Observable goal
Pass and receive the baton without dropping it or striking the hand in 4 of 5 exchanges.
## Procedure and timing
5 minutes: 1 demonstration, 3 practice, 1 checking. Receiver turns slightly sideways to see the passer and offers an open hand at a comfortable height. Passer asks 'Ready?', waits for the reply and places one end in the palm. Receiver grips and says 'Got it'; only then does the passer release. Swap roles after five exchanges, then change hands.
## Coaching and error correction
Both participants see the baton. For early release, repeat ask–grip–release. If fingers crowd one end, separate the grips. This is a visual introductory stage, not a blind race exchange.
## Safety
No throwing, poking or swinging the baton. No backward movement without looking. If dropped, both stop and only one picks it up.
## Adaptations and assessment
Easier: thicker baton or foam tube. Harder: one coordinated walking step, then stop and exchange. Stable seated participation or an agreed visual signal can replace speech. Check five exchanges in each role; 4/5 with full coordination supports progression to walking.
