## Identity and setup
Ages 9–12; beginners who can catch. Trios in a 4×5 m rectangle: performer, receiver and observer outside the passing lane. One ball and 4 flat markers per trio. For 30 pupils: 10 balls and 40 markers.
## Observable goal
Keep the pivot foot in place during a turn and pass to the receiver's chest in 4 of 5 attempts.
## Procedure and timing
6 minutes: 1 for demonstration, 4 for practice, 1 for feedback and collection. Start stationary holding the ball; choose a pivot foot before moving. Receiver stands about 3 m ahead. Turn roughly a quarter-turn and back without moving the pivot foot, then make a two-handed pass. Rotate roles after five attempts; use the other pivot foot next round. No defender.
## Coaching and error correction
Soft knee; the pivot foot may rotate in place but must not hop to a new position. If it slides, reduce the turn and rehearse without a ball. For an overpowered pass, shorten the distance and target the chest after eye contact.
## Safety
Receiver signals readiness. No face-level passes, steals or entry into another trio's area. Observer stands to the side. Retrieve a loose ball only when the route is clear.
## Adaptations and assessment
Easier: smaller turn and 2 m pass. Harder: receiver moves between two markers before the turn and stops to receive. From a stable seated position, practise trunk rotation and the passing line as a separate accessible goal, not a pivot-foot test. Observer records stable foot and accurate pass on each attempt; below 4/5, return to the easier version.
