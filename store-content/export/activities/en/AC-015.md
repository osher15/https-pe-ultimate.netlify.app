## Setup and goal
Ages 11–17; 4 participants, two stopwatches, sheet and flat markers. Lane 2 m wide and 14 m long: 10 m between timing lines plus 2 m clear before and after. Goal: notice measurement differences and define consistent start/finish, not test speed.
## Procedure, rules and ending
Roles: walker, two independent timers and recorder giving the signal. Walker waits behind start; timers beside finish can see the signal. At recorder's hand drop, timers start and walker sets off comfortably. Stop when torso crosses finish. Each reports before hearing the other; recorder notes both and absolute difference. Return outside lane. Up to 2 journeys in each 2-minute segment; rotate through four segments so all walk and time. Mark missed-signal measurements invalid; never invent a time.
## Safety and adaptations
No race or attempt to beat a time. Start only with clear lane and continue beyond finish before stopping. With wheelchair propulsion, agree a visible crossing reference beforehand and record it; do not compare with walking times. Shorter route for everyone may be recorded in advance. Easier: one journey per turn. Harder: suggest whether difference arose at start or finish without claiming certainty.
## Timing and assessment
12 minutes: 2 explanation/demo, 8 for four segments including changes, 2 reflection. Output: paired times and a common measurement rule. Difference does not prove which timer is right; matching times do not guarantee absolute accuracy. No student ranking.
