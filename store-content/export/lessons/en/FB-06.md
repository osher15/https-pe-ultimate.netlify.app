# 1. Lesson identity
**Name/code:** No-Contact Defence and Closing a Passing Lane; FB-06-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Football; stance, distance, lane interception
**Age/experience:** 11–14; developing
**Duration/students:** 45 minutes; 20–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 8–12 balls, 28 cones, bibs
**Space/complexity:** field or gym; intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Develop defence based on position, distance, and reading a pass, delaying attack without contact or unsafe tackling.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Keep the body between attacker and gate in 6/8.
- Maintain 1.5–2 metres without contact.
- Use short uncrossed steps.
- Identify and close one passing lane in 5/7.
- Intercept only a ball travelling through space.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Changing direction, off-ball movement, and passing-lane understanding from Lessons 03 and 05. Students needing support mirror without a ball.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Football 05—create a passing lane.
- **Current:** It reverses the previous lesson's viewpoint — having learned to open a passing lane in Football 05, students now shut one using stance, distance, and the timing of an interception, entirely without contact; it withholds any second defender and all pressing as a unit, since one defender works alone against a pass, which the 2v1 of Football 07 opens up.
- **Next:** Football 07—2v1 attacking advantage.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** lateral movement and stopping.
- **Social:** self-control and respect.
- **Cognitive:** read body and ball.
- **Tactical:** delay and close lane.
- **Emotional:** safe confidence without aggression.
# 7. Equipment and space
Five or six 6×9 metre rectangles with a rear gate and side passer point. **Setup:** 7 minutes; **pack-down:** 4. One active trio per area; return outside.
# 8. Safety
- No tackling, sliding, pushing, or pulling.
- No foot between the attacker’s feet.
- Work up to 60% during base practice — 65% is allowed only in the advanced adaptation, for students who have already shown control and safety.
- Intercept only a free-moving ball.
- One trio per area; stop at the boundary.
- Retrieve after scan; check surface and laces.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes. “Good defence closes a route before chasing the ball.” Purpose: body–gate–passing lane.
## Warm-up
7 minutes. Mirror pairs: the leader moves side to side; the defender keeps two metres and faces them. After 4 minutes add a gate to protect. **Variations:** walking, colour cue.
## Main activity A
10 minutes. The attacker without ball moves between cones; the defender stays between attacker and rear gate. Switch every 45 seconds. **Cues:** bent knees, short steps, do not chase, watch body centre. **Errors:** too close, crossed feet, back to gate, pushing hands.
## Main activity B
10 minutes. Passer and receiver seek a pass through either of two gates; the defender closes one and may intercept only the pass. Rotate every four balls. **Cues:** see passer and receiver, half-open body, react to release. **Errors:** charge the passer, touch a player, guess early, square stance.
## Applied game
10 minutes. 2v1: attackers score for passing through a gate; the defender scores for closing or a clean interception. Initially no dribble. **Variations:** 2v0; two-touch; one dribble.
## Closure
5 minutes. **Message:** “See ball and player; protect the important route.” Demonstration, brief feedback, collection.
# 10. Common errors
- **Too close:** the attacker escapes; mark the distance.
- **Eyes only on ball:** the receiver disappears; half-open body.
- **Charge the passer:** the gate opens; remain between lane and gate.
- **Early intercept:** the defender leaves before the pass; react to release.
- **Extend foot:** contact risk; intercept the ball path only.
- **Cross feet:** loss of balance; short parallel steps.
# 11. Teaching points
- Body between attacker and gate.
- Safe distance.
- Half-open body.
- See ball and receiver.
- Short steps.
- Close before intercept.
- React to release.
- No contact.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** no ball, leader at 40%. **Intermediate:** pass and two gates. Advanced (once control and safety are shown): 65%, two-touch. **Large group:** six areas. **Small group:** 2v1. **Limited space:** 4×7. **Limited equipment:** ball per trio. **Different needs:** wide gate, walking, visual mark, fixed partner, gate-only role.
# 13. Assessment
Position, distance, movement, lane, safety. **Success:** at least four of the five objectives achieved, each by its own stated measure. **Partial:** three objectives, or achieved without ball. **Not yet:** contact, chasing, open gate. Return to Main A.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were distance and speed controlled? Student: What helped select the lane? Improve: area width and pass speed.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Football 07—2v1. **If not:** repeat Main B slowly; return to Football 03 for control.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it defines defending as reading space rather than tackling, which is both the more effective habit and the one that makes football safe to teach in a mixed-ability class.
- **Develops:** a balanced stance with short uncrossed steps, judgement of the distance that delays without inviting a skill move, the half-open body that tracks ball and receiver at once, and the patience to react to the release rather than guess.
- **Why not skip:** students who are never taught positional defending resort to sliding in and grabbing, which produces the kind of injuries and disputes that lead some teachers to avoid competitive football altogether.
