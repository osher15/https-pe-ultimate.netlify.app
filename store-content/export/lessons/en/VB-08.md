# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 08 — Net Defence and Backcourt Coverage
**Lesson code:** VB-08-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** shadow block and coverage
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Coordinate net hand position and backcourt coverage without contact or crossing.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Presses both hands high above the net with fingers spread and thumbs up, holding the shape throughout the shadow block, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Lands on two feet on the same side of the net, without stepping forward into or under it, in 3 of 4 attempts.
- Travels to the block with side steps rather than crossing the feet, arriving square to the net before the attack.
- Covers the backcourt space beside a blocking team-mate and plays the ball that passes the block, in 3 of 5 situations.
- Takes blocker, backcourt, and observer roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's hand position or landing.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-07 — controlled attack.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-07 — controlled attack.  
**Current lesson:** It answers VB-07 from the other side of the net: while one student attacks, a front player shows a shadow block with high hands and a back player covers the space behind. It withholds jumping to block a genuine spike and all contact at the net — blocks are shadowed and attacks stay at controlled force — so shape is learned before speed. VB-09 puts it into continuous rotation.  
**Next lesson:** VB-09 — 3v3 play and transition.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “The block is not about stopping the ball — it is about taking away one direction so your partner behind knows where to stand. Hands high, land where you took off, and never reach over the net.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — hands high, land on the same side, back player covers — plus the absolute rules about never touching the net and never crossing under it.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle beside a low net.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare shoulders, ankles, and knees for repeated overhead reaching and soft landings.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then partner mirrors at the net.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add overhead arm reaches, ankle bounces, and low half-squats; then face a partner across the net and mirror their side steps along it, both pressing the hands high on a signal without jumping.  
**Teaching points:** side step, never cross the feet; hands stay in front of the face, fingers spread and firm.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — no jump at all, hands high from standing; advanced — add a small controlled jump and landing.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** build the blocking shape and a safe landing before any ball is involved.  
**Procedure:** students side-step along the net to a called position, press both hands high in a shadow block, and land balanced on two feet on their own side. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs at the net, one calling positions and one moving, alternating; a clear no-go strip marked under the net.  
**Teaching points:** square the shoulders to the net before pressing the hands up; fingers spread and thumbs up so the ball cannot pass between the hands; land on the spot you took off from, bending the knees; never reach over or touch the net.  
**Common errors:** crossing the feet while travelling; drifting forward and landing under the net; hands apart so the ball passes straight through; landing stiff-legged.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** connect the block to the coverage behind it, so defence becomes a pair of linked decisions.  
**Procedure:** a front player calls and shows the block on one side; the back player reads that call and moves to cover the space the block leaves open, playing the easy ball sent past it. Roles rotate every minute.  
**Organisation:** groups of four — attacker, blocker, backcourt player, feeder — with attacks at controlled force only.  
**Teaching points:** the blocker calls which side they are taking before the attack; the back player stands behind and to the open side, never directly in the blocker's shadow; stay low and watch the attacker's hand, not the ball.  
**Common errors:** the back player hiding behind the blocker where no ball can arrive; no call, so both cover the same space; the blocker turning to watch the ball after landing instead of recovering.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** defend a live attack with the shape intact and no contact at the net.  
**Rules:** 3v3 over a low net with attacks from the floor only; the defending team scores a point for keeping the ball alive after an attack, plus a bonus point when the blocker called a side and the back player covered accordingly. Any net contact or reaching over stops the rally immediately.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a defence that keeps the ball up has already achieved something, even in a lost rally; call before every attack.  
**Common errors:** everyone watching the attacker and nobody covering; silent defence; reaching over the net as the game tightens.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A block that takes away one side is worth more than a block that tries to take away everything — tell your partner which half you have, and cover the rest together.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their hand position, landing, or coverage, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Jumping forward into the net:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per trio and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** hand position at the block, balanced landing, side-step footwork, backcourt coverage, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-09 — 3v3 play and transition. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is the first organised answer to an attack, and it teaches the principle that blocking and backcourt defence are one coordinated act: the block decides where the ball can go, and the cover waits there.  
**What develops?** A safe blocking shape with high spread hands, side-step footwork along the net, a balanced landing on the same side, reading the attacker's hand, and the calling that pairs a blocker with a coverer.  
**Why not skip?** The net is where the main collision risks in school volleyball sit — landing under it, reaching over it, or colliding with a team-mate — and the habits taught here address all three; without coverage, meanwhile, a ball the block deflects simply wins the point for the other team.
