# 1. Lesson identity
**Name:** Receive on the Move: Pass or Dribble
**Code:** BB-06-EN
**Sport:** Basketball
**Subtopic:** reception, scanning, and decision-making
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** beginner–developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Students:** 20–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 8–12 balls, 28 cones, bibs
**Space:** full or half court
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Connect off-ball movement to a simple decision after reception so students act from available space rather than a preselected action.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Receive while moving and stop in balance or continue a controlled dribble in 6/8 attempts.
- Scan before reception and identify a colour cue or open lane in 6/8.
- Pass when the forward lane is closed and dribble when open in 5/7 situations.
- Decide within three seconds without contact.
- State the cue that led to the choice.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Basic passing, receiving, dribbling on the move, and movement into a passing lane. Students missing a component begin walking with an early cue.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Basketball 05—move after passing and create a lane.
- **Current:** It adds the choice Basketball 05 withheld: having made themselves available, students now scan before the ball arrives and pick between dribbling an open lane and passing a closed one. It withholds finishing at the basket — an open lane here is dribbled through a gate, never converted into a shot — which is Basketball 07's subject.
- **Next:** Basketball 07—lay-up after a controlled drive.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** moving reception, stop, dribble exit, quick pass.
- **Social:** trust, communication, role rotation.
- **Cognitive:** cue perception, response inhibition, choice.
- **Tactical:** attack an open lane or transfer against closure.
- **Emotional:** confidence to decide and learn from error.
# 7. Equipment and space
One ball per pair/trio, two-colour cone gates, one shadow-defender bib. **Setup:** 7 minutes before. **Pack-down:** 4 minutes after. Mark five 5×12-metre lanes with a side gate, forward gate, and outside return route.
# 8. Safety
- One active receiver per lane.
- No contact or taking from hands.
- Shadow defender stays at 50% and avoids late cuts.
- Pass below head height to a watching receiver.
- Stop and scan before retrieving a loose ball.
- Return outside the lane.
- Check dry floor, laces, and cones.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Time:** 3 minutes  
**Teacher says:** “Before the ball arrives, look for a clue: open lane means dribble; closed lane means pass.”  
**Purpose:** introduce scan–receive–decide.
## Warm-up
**Time:** 7 minutes  
**Purpose:** movement, reaction, balanced stop.  
**Structure:** pairs in lanes, initially no defender.  
**Instructions:** move to receive; the teacher shows right/left colour and the student turns through that gate.  
**Transitions:** 3 minutes without ball, 4 with a soft pass.  
**Variations:** walk; sound cue; closer gates.
## Main activity A
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** scan before reception.  
**Process:** the receiver moves to a central cone. Before the pass, the partner shows a colour. The receiver calls it, catches, and balances facing the matching gate.  
**Organisation:** trios—passer, receiver, cue giver; rotate every four balls.  
**Teaching points:** eyes early, ready hands, catch in front, wide base.  
**Errors:** late look, trapping at body, narrow stop, leaving before pass.
## Main activity B
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** choose pass or dribble.  
**Process:** after reception, the defender either closes the forward gate or remains to the side. Open: three controlled bounces through. Closed: stop and pass to a partner moving into support.  
**Organisation:** groups of four; rotate every 90 seconds.  
**Teaching points:** head up, decide within three seconds, first step into space, pass to a ready target.  
**Errors:** predetermined action, dribbling into pressure, blind pass, defender too fast.
## Applied game
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** apply under moderate pressure.  
**Rules:** 3v2 in a square with two end gates. The first receiver scans, then dribbles through an open route or passes when closed. No shot and no contact.  
**Scoring:** one point for a correct choice completed under control; bonus for explaining the cue.  
**Variations:** 3v1 support; 3v2 standard; 3v3 at 70% challenge.
## Closure
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Message:** “I decide from what I see, not from what I planned.”  
**Review:** compare two demonstrations and their cues.  
**Feedback:** each student says, “I chose ___ because ___.”  
**Exit:** assigned collection, count, teacher release.
# 10. Common errors
- **No scan:** eyes only on the ball; place colour at eye height and require a call.
- **Early decision:** same action every time; change the cue just before reception.
- **Dribble into closure:** the student approaches the defender; stop, identify support, pass.
- **Pass without target:** the receiver is unready; require eye contact and hands.
- **Unbalanced catch:** feet together; mark two wide footprints.
- **Slow response:** over three seconds; reduce to two cues and guide repetitions.
# 11. Teaching points
- Scan before the catch.
- Receive in front.
- Open—dribble.
- Closed—pass.
- Decide within three seconds.
- Head up.
- Quality before speed.
# 12. Adaptations
- **Foundation:** walk, early cue, no defender.
- **Intermediate:** shadow defender at 50%.
- **Advanced:** defender at 70%, later cue.
- **Large group:** six lanes and cue-giver role.
- **Small group:** 2v1.
- **Limited space:** 4×8 lane, two bounces.
- **Limited equipment:** one ball per four; parallel scanning without ball.
- **Different needs:** visual or sound cue, short route, soft ball, fixed partner, approved individual plan.
# 13. Assessment
Check scan, reception, choice, execution, safety. **Success:** 4/5 in 6/8. **Partial:** three criteria or success only unopposed. **Not yet:** cannot distinguish open/closed or repeatedly loses control. Return to colour-cue choice.
# 14. Reflection
- **Teacher:** Was the cue visible? Was the defender predictable? Who needs slower timing?
- **Students:** What did you see? When was passing better?
- **Improve:** adjust cue timing and distance.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Basketball 07—lay-up from an open lane. **If not:** repeat Main A; return to Basketball 05 if moving reception is the barrier.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it is the hinge between technique and genuine play: until a student chooses an action from what the defence is actually doing, basketball is only a set of rehearsed movements performed in turn.
- **Develops:** visual scanning ahead of the ball, response inhibition — holding back the pre-planned action — decision speed under a three-second limit, and the vocabulary to explain a choice.
- **Why not skip:** students who never practise reading open and closed lanes default to a single habit, usually dribbling into traffic, and that habit hardens exactly when later lessons add defenders and speed.
