# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 01 — Ready Position and Forearm Pass
**Lesson code:** VB-01-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** ready stance, platform formation, and controlled forearm pass
**Age:** 10–13
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 10–15 lightweight volleyballs or beach balls, 24 cones, and a low net or rope
**Space:** gym or court divided into 4–6 small grids
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Build a stable ready position and controlled forearm platform so students can direct a gently delivered ball to a partner.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Reaches the ready position—knees bent, weight forward—before every contact.
- Forms a flat forearm platform with the thumbs together in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Directs the ball into the target zone above head height in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Moves to the ball with the feet and stops before contact rather than reaching.
- Takes both performer and observer roles in every rotation and names one factual cue seen in the partner's stance or platform, rather than a verdict.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No volleyball lesson required; students must respect personal space and stop on signal.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** No volleyball lesson required; students must respect personal space and stop on signal.  
**Current lesson:** As the opening volleyball lesson it builds the two things every later contact depends on: the low ready position and a flat, stable forearm platform aimed with the legs. It withholds the overhead set and all play across a net — every ball is delivered by a gentle underhand toss and ends in a catch — which VB-02 begins to open up.  
**Next lesson:** VB-02 — overhead setting and support movement.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- Use lightweight balls, keep grids separated, deliver by underhand toss, and stop balls rolling into another grid before restarting.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use a controllable force and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn the ready stance, the platform formation, and the controlled forearm pass. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Move in small grids, stop in ready position, mirror a partner, and catch a gentle self-toss at waist height before forming the platform.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** From a self-toss, join the forearms, extend the legs gently, and direct the ball to a wall target or partner catch. One contact followed by a catch.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** Feet apart and ready; move behind the ball; flat platform; lift with legs; point the platform to the target.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** In pairs, one underhand-tosses from 2–3 metres and the other passes toward a target hoop or partner. Rotate after five deliveries and record controlled direction.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** scan before acting, select free space, and reset after each attempt.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied area, late decision, and repeating without feedback.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Three-Contact Preparation: in groups of three, toss–forearm pass–catch earns one point when the receiver starts ready and the ball travels safely upward. No spikes or over-net competition. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—larger target and no defender; intermediate—smaller target; advanced—one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Your arms do not hit the ball in volleyball — your legs lift it. Get there with your feet, hold the platform still, and the ball goes where you point it.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- Contact with fists or separated palms: the ball leaves at a random angle; correct with a single forearm platform and thumbs held together.
- **Bent elbows:** there is no stable platform; correct with straight, locked arms before contact.
- **Swinging at the ball:** the ball flies high and backwards; correct by driving from the legs with no arm swing.
- **Contacting while reaching:** the student stretches at the ball instead of moving; correct by arriving with the feet and stopping before contact.
- **Standing upright between contacts:** reactions are slow; correct by returning to a low ready position immediately after every contact.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without adding contacts.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, ball size, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** ready position before every contact, a flat forearm platform with the thumbs together, moving to the ball with the feet, lifting with the legs, and the accuracy of direction into the target zone.  
**Success:** all five measures are shown consistently and the student explains one cue.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for preparation or decision.  
**Not yet successful:** spacing or force remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before VB-02 — overhead setting and support movement.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to VB-02 — overhead setting and support movement.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The forearm pass is the first contact in almost every volleyball rally, so a class that cannot control it never reaches a second or third touch and never experiences the game as a rally at all.  
**What develops?** A low athletic ready position, a stable platform of straight locked arms and joined thumbs, moving to the ball with the feet rather than reaching, and directing the ball by angling the platform instead of swinging at it.  
**Why not skip?** Students who begin by swinging their arms send every ball backwards and out; the habit forms quickly, is hard to remove, and makes any later net game collapse after a single contact.
