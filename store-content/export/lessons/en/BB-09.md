# 1. Lesson identity
**Name/code:** 2v1 Numerical Advantage; BB-09-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Basketball; width, committing a defender, pass-or-drive choice
**Age/experience:** 12–14; developing
**Duration/students:** 45 minutes; 18–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 8–12 balls, 24 cones, bibs, 2–4 baskets or end gates
**Space/complexity:** half or full court; intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Develop the ability to use a two-attacker advantage through width, timing, and reading one defender rather than following a fixed action.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Attackers retain 3–5 metres of width in 6/8 situations.
- Ball carrier scans the defender before the decision zone.
- Pass when the defender closes the lane or advance when the defender stays with the partner in 5/7.
- Off-ball player remains ahead of the ball and visible.
- Complete within eight seconds, under control and without contact.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Moving pass/catch, open-or-closed choice, basic lay-up, and no-contact defence. Students not ready use 2v0 with a cone defender.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Basketball 08—close a lane safely.
- **Current:** It adds the numerical advantage, asking two attackers to hold width and read one defender rather than follow a fixed action; it withholds any second defender and all defensive recovery, so the advantage never disappears mid-attack — which Basketball 10 removes.
- **Next:** Basketball 10—3v3 combining support, drive, and defence.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** running, moving pass, finish.
- **Social:** cooperation and trust.
- **Cognitive:** attend to defender and choose.
- **Tactical:** width, commit, timed pass.
- **Emotional:** patience under moderate pressure.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark 4–5 corridors 6–8 metres wide and 12–18 metres long with basket/end gate. **Setup:** 8 minutes; **pack-down:** 5. Three active per corridor; waiting/observer roles outside.
# 8. Safety
- No contact, screens, or taking from hands.
- Defender 60%, then maximum 75%.
- Clear finish zone before restart.
- Passes below head height to a watching partner.
- No corridor crossing.
- Rebound after players exit; retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Stop for pain, slipping, or crowding.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes. “In an advantage, keep width and wait for the defender to choose.” Purpose: width–scan–decide.
## Warm-up
7 minutes. Pairs move in parallel lanes and pass; at a decision cone the carrier advances or passes from an observer’s colour cue. 3 minutes walking, 2 light run, 2 finish through gate. **Variations:** no ball or sound cue.
## Main activity A
10 minutes. Two attackers advance wide; a cone/observer “closes” one side, prompting lane or pass choice. Rotate after four sequences. **Cues:** do not converge, pass before paths cross, show hands, continue in support. **Errors:** same line, late pass, eyes down, stopping.
## Main activity B
10 minutes. The defender begins centrally at 60%, no taking from hands. Attackers reach the end within eight seconds; rotate every two sequences. **Cues:** the carrier commits without collision; the partner holds width; pass when the defender commits. **Errors:** preselection, driving into the defender, partner behind the ball, aggressive defence.
## Applied game
10 minutes. Play 2v1 to a gate, then return against a new defender. Score one for correct decision, one for controlled execution, one safety point for no contact. **Variations:** shadow; 60%; 75% with two gates.
## Closure
5 minutes. **Message:** “Width makes the defender choose; that choice gives our answer.” Demonstration, “I saw–I chose” feedback, organised collection.
# 10. Common errors
- **Attackers converge:** one defender covers both; use coloured lanes.
- **Early pass:** the defender is uncommitted; reach the decision cone.
- **Late pass:** the lane is already closed; two-second limit in zone.
- **Partner trails:** the passing lane disappears; remain half a step ahead.
- **Eyes only on ball:** cannot read the defender; identify bib colour.
- **Drive into contact:** the lane is closed; balanced stop and outlet pass.
# 11. Teaching points
- Keep width.
- Scan early.
- Commit without collision.
- Pass when defender commits.
- Partner ahead and visible.
- Lead the pass.
- Decision before speed.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** 2v0/cone defender. **Intermediate:** 60%. **Advanced:** 75%, seven seconds, two gates. **Large group:** five corridors plus observers. **Small group:** trio rotation. **Limited space:** 5×10, no basket. **Limited equipment:** cone gates. **Different needs:** walking, soft ball, wider route, visual/sound cue, fixed support role.
# 13. Assessment
Width, scan, choice, timing, safety. **Success:** 4/5 in 6/8. **Partial:** three criteria, or success only against shadow. **Not yet:** crowding, random choice, repeated contact. Return to cued 2v0.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Was defender intensity fair and space wide enough? Student: What defender action caused the pass or drive? Improve: speed and decision zone.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Basketball 10—3v3. **If not:** slow Main B; return to Basketball 06 for open/closed reading.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it is the smallest situation in which tactics genuinely exist: with two attackers and one defender a student must read an opponent rather than execute a rehearsed move, which is the bridge from technique to understanding.
- **Develops:** attacking width, the timing of a pass released at the moment the defender commits, sustained attention on an opponent rather than the ball, and patience under moderate pressure.
- **Why not skip:** taken straight from 1v1 to 3v3, students converge on the ball and choose at random; the 2v1 is where width and timing can still be seen, coached, and corrected one decision at a time.
