# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 02 — Overhead Set and Support Movement
**Lesson code:** VB-02-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** finger contact and support
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Direct an overhead set to a partner and move immediately to support.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Contacts the ball with the finger pads above the forehead, elbows out and thumbs back, without catching or holding the ball, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Arrives under the ball with the feet and is stationary before contact, in 3 of 4 attempts.
- Sets the ball high enough to pass above head height and lands it inside a 2-metre target zone near the partner, in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Calls for the ball before entering its path and keeps two arm lengths from every other student.
- Moves to a free support position within 2 seconds of releasing the set, in 4 of 5 rallies, rather than watching the ball, and names one factual cue seen in a partner's hand shape.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-01 — ready position and forearm pass.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-01 — ready position and forearm pass.  
**Current lesson:** It adds the second of the two basic contacts: where VB-01 played the ball from below with a platform, students now play it from above with the finger pads, then move to support the next contact. It withholds serving and any play that starts a rally — every ball is fed by a self-toss or a partner — which VB-03 introduces.  
**Next lesson:** VB-03 — accurate underhand serve.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “The set is the softest contact in volleyball. Get your forehead under the ball, make a window with your hands, and push it up with your legs — if it makes a slapping sound, you hit it instead of setting it.”  
**Purpose:** state the three cues — forehead under the ball, open fingers, push from the legs — plus the calling rule and the stop signal.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle, balls held still.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise wrists, fingers, shoulders, and ankles, and rehearse the hand shape before any ball is set.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then self-contacts.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add wrist circles, finger stretches, and low half-squats; then hold the ball above the forehead to feel the window shape, drop and catch it in that same shape ten times, and finally set it to yourself at low height.  
**Teaching points:** thumbs point back towards the eyes, not forward; contact is with the pads, never the palms.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — catch and throw from the set position; advanced — continuous self-sets.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** build the contact under stable, predictable conditions.  
**Procedure:** from a self-toss, each student sets the ball to a wall target or to a partner who catches it. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs in each zone with performer and observer alternating; one ball per pair.  
**Teaching points:** toss straight up so you are not chasing your own toss; move the feet so the ball falls onto your forehead; bend the knees and extend legs, arms, and wrists in one flow; finish with the hands pointing where the ball went.  
**Common errors:** stiff flat palms, which slap the ball; letting the ball drop behind the head; arms doing all the work with no leg drive; catching and throwing, which is a held ball.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** join the contact to the movement that follows it.  
**Procedure:** in triads, one student sets to a partner and immediately moves to the open cone, so the three positions rotate continuously. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** three cones in a triangle 3–4 metres apart, one ball per triad, the waiting student observing one cue.  
**Teaching points:** call the receiver's name before setting; move the moment the ball leaves the fingers; arrive at the cone and return to the ready position before the next ball; set high, because height buys your team-mate time.  
**Common errors:** admiring the set instead of moving; drifting towards the ball rather than to the open cone; flat fast sets that arrive before anyone is ready; silent play.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** use the set inside a cooperative rally without the pressure of a full game.  
**Rules:** 2v2 over a low net or rope, with one catch permitted per side so rallies survive; a point is scored for a controlled set a partner can play, plus a bonus point when the setter then moved to support. No spiking and no competitive scoring over the net.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** the catch is a reset, not a failure; talk before every contact.  
**Common errors:** rushing to win the rally; two students going for the same ball; standing flat-footed after the set.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A good set is quiet and high — it gives your team-mate time. If your set is loud and flat, you hit the ball instead of guiding it.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their hand shape or leg drive, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Rigid palm contact:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per triad and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** posture, contact, direction, position, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-03 — accurate underhand serve. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The set is the contact that turns a dug ball into an attack; without it a team can only return the ball, never build a point, and the three-touch rally that defines volleyball is impossible.  
**What develops?** A soft finger-pad contact and a stable hand shape, moving the feet to get under a falling ball, extension through the legs rather than the arms, and the habit of supporting the next contact after making one.  
**Why not skip?** Students who never learn the hand shape set with flat palms or double-contact every ball, both of which are faults; jamming stiff fingers into a falling ball is also a real source of finger injuries, which a taught hand shape helps prevent.
