# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 05 — Three-Step Rhythm and Controlled Finish
**Lesson code:** HB-05-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** linking movement to throwing
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student links a catch, up to three steps, and a controlled throw without breaking movement rhythm.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Catches the ball in stride and releases within three steps in 4 of 5 attempts, without a travelling call.
- Counts the steps aloud during practice and lands balanced on the final step rather than stumbling into the throw.
- Matches the number of steps to the distance of the reception, choosing one, two, or three steps in 3 of 4 receptions.
- Keeps the throwing action intact at the end of the run, with the same side-on stance and high elbow taught in HB-04.
- Takes receiver, feeder, and observer roles in turn and names one observable cue about a partner's rhythm or balance.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-04 — accurate overarm throw.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-04 — accurate overarm throw.  
**Current lesson:** joins the two halves the pathway has so far kept apart: the moving reception of HB-03 and the standing throw of HB-04 become one continuous action inside the three-step rule. It withholds any defender in the finishing lane, so the rhythm is learned unopposed — which HB-06 introduces from the defending side.  
**Next lesson:** HB-06 — no-contact defence and lane control.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 metres clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Three steps are not a punishment; they are a rhythm. Catch while you are moving, count one–two–three in your head, and let the third step throw the ball for you.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — catch, count, balance, throw — plus the lane rules and the retrieval route.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle, balls held still.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the legs for repeated take-offs and landings and establish the three-step count before a ball is involved.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then floor markers, then paired passing.  
**Instructions:** jog with arm swings and gentle skips; walk through three floor markers counting one–two–three aloud and mime the throw on the third; then in pairs pass and catch while walking, taking exactly three steps between catch and release.  
**Teaching points:** count out loud at first — the rhythm is learned by ear before it is felt.  
**Transitions:** move to work zones by colour.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk and use two steps; advanced — vary the count to one, two, or three on call.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: practise three-step rhythm without pressure.  
**Procedure:** a feeder passes to a student running onto the ball. The receiver catches in stride, steps on three floor markers, and throws through a cone goal on the third step. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, in four groups; every student completes both sides.  
**Organisation:** one lane per group, three floor spots set at natural stride length, a feeder at the side and a retriever behind the goal, never in it.  
**Teaching points:** catch with the hands already up so no step is wasted; count one–two–three aloud; stay tall and balanced on the third step; hold the finish position for a moment before walking out.  
**Common errors:** catching flat-footed and only then starting to run, which wastes the count; taking a fourth step; rushing so the throw begins before the third foot lands; dropping the elbow because the legs are doing the work.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: match step count to distance.  
**Procedure:** the feeder passes from three marked distances, so the receiver must judge whether one, two, or three steps fit the space before the goal. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** trios — feeder, receiver, observer — with the observer counting the steps aloud and reporting the number.  
**Teaching points:** decide the step count as the ball arrives, not afterwards; a short reception needs one step, not a forced three; balance always comes before power.  
**Common errors:** using three steps regardless of distance; adding a shuffle to make the count fit; losing the throwing shape when the count is short; the observer counting after the throw rather than during it.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** keep the step rhythm legal when the pressure of a game makes students hurry.  
**Rules:** 3v3 in a marked area, no goalkeeper; a goal counts one point, with a bonus point when the finish was a legal catch-and-three-step sequence. Travelling simply cancels the attempt, without penalty. No contact and no stealing from the hands.  
**Teaching points:** the pass before the finish should arrive into the runner's path; slow down to stay legal rather than speed up to score.  
**Common errors:** travelling under pressure; stopping dead to be safe and losing all rhythm; defenders closing to contact.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Count your three steps out loud today and you will not have to count them at all next month — that rhythm is what lets you shoot while everyone else is still stopping.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their count or balance, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection and a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Rushing steps without balance:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “catch–count.”
- **Late transition:** seen in standing after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, gaze, decision, spacing, safety. **Success:** 4–5 criteria in most rounds. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompting. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-06 — no-contact defence and lane control. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** it connects static technique to a moving finish, and the three-step rule is the one law of handball that decides whether an attack is legal at all.  
**Develops:** step rhythm and the timing of a reception in stride, balance on a moving take-off, judgement of how many steps a distance allows, and the ability to hold throwing form while the legs are working.  
**Why not skip:** students who never rehearse the count either travel constantly, which kills every attack they build, or stop dead before throwing, which hands the defence all the time it needs.
