# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Basketball 01 — Controlled Dribbling on the Move
**Lesson code:** BB-01-EN
**Sport:** Basketball
**Subtopic:** dribble height, ball protection, and movement into free space
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 basketballs or soft balls, 30 cones, bibs, and 4 colour markers
**Space:** gym or court divided into 3–4 lanes with safety zones
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Build basic dribbling control while moving, scanning space, and keeping safe distance as preparation for stopping and changing direction.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Dribbles the full lane with the ball kept below waist height and beside the body, retaining possession in 4 of 5 trials.
- Lifts the eyes off the ball at least twice per lane run and names the gate chosen before entering it.
- Ends the dribble on the signal within two seconds by catching the ball in two hands with both feet settled, without crossing into a neighbouring lane.
- Changes dribbling hand on the teacher's cue at least once per round, keeping the ball on the side away from the shadow defender.
- Names for a partner one specific marker from the lesson's teaching points — finger push, ball height, soft knees, or forward look — and says where it was seen.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No previous lesson required; students must move safely and respond to a stop signal. No previous tactical knowledge is required.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** No previous lesson required; students must move safely and respond to a stop signal.  
**Current lesson:** As the entry point of the pathway it adds the first on-ball skill: holding a controlled dribble while walking and scanning for free space. It deliberately withholds the sharp stop and the change of direction under pressure — students stop only on a signal, in open space, with at most a passive shadow defender — because those belong to BB-02.  
**Next lesson:** BB-02 — controlled stop and change of direction.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** hand–eye control, rhythm, stopping, and direction change.  
**Social:** turn-taking, spacing, and respectful feedback.  
**Cognitive:** scanning, recognising space, and deciding.  
**Tactical:** ball protection and movement into a free lane.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after losing the ball.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark 3–4 one-way lanes, 2–3 metres wide, a waiting zone behind the start line, and a ball station outside the lanes.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs; one performs and one observes, 5–8 students per lane, one ball per pair.
# 8. Safety
- Check the surface, balls, and footwear before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and travel only in the marked direction.
- Enter a lane only after the previous student passes the second cone.
- No stealing, body contact, or chasing between lanes.
- Retrieve a loose ball by walking after checking the route.
- Start and stop only on signal; hold balls during instruction.
- Stop and report pain, dizziness, or a collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn dribble height, ball protection, and movement into free space. Success is control, scanning, and spacing—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, stop signal, boundaries, and loose-ball rule.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare ankles, knees, shoulders, and reactions.  
**Structure:** movement without a ball between cones, then self-bounce and catch.  
**Instructions:** brisk walk, lateral steps, soft stop, direction change, and catch after one bounce.  
**Transitions:** change on signal; return by walking outside the lane.  
**Variations:** foundation—no ball; intermediate—carry the ball; advanced—low stationary dribble.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** build the technical pattern in stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** In three lanes, progress from waist-height stationary dribble to walking in a straight line and ending the dribble by catching the ball in two hands. Each student completes three trials with each hand, with no speed demand.  
**Organisation:** pairs in three lanes; switch after each attempt.  
**Teaching points:** Push with the fingers, do not slap; keep the ball beside the body; soften the knees; take brief forward looks; place the body between a shadow defender and the ball.  
**Common errors:** ball too high, eyes fixed down, and upright posture.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** use the skill with a spatial choice.  
**Procedure:** Use a cone grid with coloured gates. Students dribble to an open gate, raise their eyes before entry, and stop in a safe area. On signal they change hand or direction and select a different gate each round.  
**Organisation:** up to six students in a zone; partner observes one marker.  
**Teaching points:** choose before entry, slow in crowded space, and keep the ball on the far side.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied gate, late change, and lost spacing.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect control and decision without a speed contest.  
**Rules:** Space Collector: one point for dribbling through a free gate under control, one for looking forward, and one for a safe stop. A collision, carrying the ball, or leaving the lane cancels only that attempt. No stealing or individual ranking.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 points per attempt for control, scanning, and safety.  
**Variations:** foundation—wide gate; intermediate—choose between two gates; advanced—change hand on cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “If you can see the whole court while the ball stays beside your hand, you are dribbling well — speed is the last thing we add, not the first.”  
**Summary:** each student names one success marker and one choice.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, balls to cart, cones by colour, and head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Slapping the ball:** loud contact and ball escapes; correct with a soft finger push.
- **Eyes down:** cones are not scanned; correct with a short forward look every two dribbles.
- **Ball in front of feet:** student kicks it; correct by dribbling beside the body.
- **Upright posture:** stopping is difficult; correct with soft knees and a lower centre of gravity.
- **Insufficient spacing:** student enters an active lane; correct by waiting for the second cone.
- **Rushing:** attempt becomes uncontrolled; correct with walking pace and one quality target.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate from the side and front.
- Teach one technical marker at a time.
- Reward scanning and control, not speed.
- Give equal practice time to both hands without demanding symmetry.
- Use a softer or smaller ball where needed.
- Stop the group before instruction; do not speak over bouncing balls.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walking, wider gates, and soft ball.  
**Intermediate:** narrower lane and planned hand change.  
**Advanced:** late visual cue or short weak-hand section.  
**Large group:** duplicate lanes and use performer–observer pairs.  
**Small group:** more gates and feedback after each attempt.  
**Limited space:** stationary dribble with turn and a short lane.  
**Partial equipment:** one ball per pair; observer practises footwork without a ball.  
**Different needs:** adapt ball size, bounce height, pace, distance, and role; allow rolling or two hands at the foundation stage.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** control, scanning, spacing, response to signal, and lane choice.  
**Success:** at least four of the five listed measures are met, each in 4 of 5 attempts.  
**Partial success:** safe control, but scanning or choice needs a reminder.  
**Not yet successful:** loss of control threatens spacing after adaptation; return to stationary or walking practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who looked forward? Was spacing sufficient? Which adaptation reduced loss of control?  
**Students:** When was the ball close? What helped you choose a lane? What will you try next?  
**Improvement:** adjust lane width, cue type, or group ratio before BB-02 — controlled stop and change of direction.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to BB-02 — controlled stop and change of direction.  
**If not:** return to stationary dribble, straight-line walking, and a single stop signal.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Dribbling with the eyes up is the gateway skill of basketball: a student who must watch the ball cannot pass, cut, or defend, because every later decision depends on having the eyes free.  
**What develops?** Fine hand control and a consistent bounce height, visual scanning and spatial awareness, the self-regulation to stop on a signal, and the early habit of shielding the ball with the body.  
**Why not skip?** Students who miss this stage carry a head-down dribble into every later lesson; under game pressure the habit becomes automatic and is far harder to unlearn than to teach correctly now.
