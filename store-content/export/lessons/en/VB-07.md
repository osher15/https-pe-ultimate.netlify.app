# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 07 — Controlled Standing Attack
**Lesson code:** VB-07-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** open-hand hit and tip
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Send a controlled standing attack into open space safely.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Contacts the ball with an open hand above shoulder height, elbow leading and wrist firm, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Arrives under the set and is balanced before contacting, rather than reaching or jumping, in 3 of 4 attempts.
- Places the attack into a marked open space rather than at an opponent, in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Chooses between a firm hit and a soft tip according to which target is open, holding force at about 60%.
- Takes setter, attacker, and observer roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's hand shape or placement.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-06 — movement and coverage.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-06 — movement and coverage.  
**Current lesson:** It gives the third contact of VB-05 a purpose: instead of simply sending the ball over, the student now attacks it with an open hand from a standing position, choosing between a firm hit and a tip. It withholds jumping, spiking downward, and any blocking — every contact stays at controlled force from the floor — which VB-08 answers from the defending side.  
**Next lesson:** VB-08 — net defence and backcourt coverage.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “An attack is not the hardest hit — it is the ball that lands where nobody is standing. Open hand, high elbow, about sixty per cent force, and look at the floor you are aiming for.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — high elbow, open hand, 60% force — plus the rules that nobody stands in a target zone and no attack is aimed at a person.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing a low net with two target zones marked on the far side.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise the shoulder thoroughly before repeated overhead striking and rehearse the hand shape.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then wall contacts.  
**Instructions:** jog with direction changes on a colour call; add full arm circles, shoulder rolls, and trunk rotations; then shadow the attacking action ten times with a high elbow and open hand; finally strike a ball gently against a wall from standing, catching it after one bounce.  
**Teaching points:** contact with the flat of the open hand, fingers spread and wrist firm — not a fist and not a slap.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — softer ball at shorter distance; advanced — alternate hit and tip against the wall.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** groove the contact on a predictable ball, with a target large enough that accuracy is achievable.  
**Procedure:** a partner tosses a high ball; the attacker steps under it and strikes it with an open hand into a large marked target on the far side. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs per court, the tosser standing to the side and never in front, a retriever collecting from outside the target zone.  
**Teaching points:** get under the ball and be balanced before you swing; lead with a high elbow and contact the ball in front of the head; keep the wrist firm and finish the hand towards the target; sixty per cent force is a rule, not a suggestion.  
**Common errors:** hitting with a fist or a closed slap; contacting behind the head, which sends the ball backwards; swinging hard and losing all direction; jumping when the lesson asks for a standing contact.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** turn the attack into a decision rather than a reflex.  
**Procedure:** two targets are marked, one deep and one short, with one of them covered by a stationary defender. The attacker reads which is open and chooses a firm hit to the deep target or a soft tip to the short one. Roles rotate every minute.  
**Organisation:** groups of four — setter, attacker, stationary defender, retriever — with the defender never moving once the set has been made.  
**Teaching points:** look at the open floor before the set arrives, not after; keep the same preparation for hit and tip so the choice stays disguised; a tip needs firm fingers, not a push.  
**Common errors:** deciding before looking; the preparation giving away the tip; tipping with soft fingers so the ball drops short; hitting straight at the defender because that is where the eyes are.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** attack against a real opponent while placement, not power, decides the reward.  
**Rules:** 2v2 over a low net; one point for winning the rally plus a bonus point for any attack that lands in open space having been preceded by a set. Attacks are made from the floor at controlled force, and any attack aimed at a player's body cancels the point.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** look at the gap, not at the opponent; a well-placed tip earns the same bonus as a firm hit.  
**Common errors:** power creeping up as the game tightens; attacking on the first contact without a set; aiming at the opponent rather than the space.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The ball that wins the point is the one that lands where nobody is — look at the empty floor, not at the player in front of you.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their hand shape or placement choice, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Hard hit without direction:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per trio and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** open-hand contact, positioning under the ball, placement into open space, force control, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-08 — net defence and backcourt coverage. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is the point at which a team can finally win a rally rather than wait for the other side to make a mistake, and it establishes from the outset that an attack is aimed at placement rather than maximum force.  
**What develops?** An open-hand striking pattern with a high elbow and firm wrist, moving under a set and attacking from balance, reading which space a defence has left, and disguising the choice between a hit and a tip.  
**Why not skip?** Students who meet attacking for the first time in a free game hit as hard as they can at whoever is nearest, which is both ineffective and a genuine source of injury and fear on a shared court; teaching placement first sets the norm before power is available.
