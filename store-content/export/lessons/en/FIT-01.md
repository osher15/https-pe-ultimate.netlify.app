# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Fitness 01 — Self-Regulated Strength and Endurance Circuit
**Lesson code:** FIT-01-EN
**Sport:** Physical fitness
**Subtopic:** foundation strength, aerobic endurance, and self-selected load
**Age:** 12–16
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, 6–10 mats, stable benches or wall space, timer, and level cards
**Space:** gym, court, or flat marked area with 4–6 stations
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Introduce a safe circuit in which students select a manageable level, maintain movement quality, and describe their effort.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Chooses an exercise version they can control at each station and justifies it with one body signal.
- Holds clean technique for the full work interval at 4 of 5 stations.
- Rates effort on a 1–5 scale and adjusts the load at least once in the lesson.
- Moves between stations along the marked route within the set transition time.
- Takes both performer and observer roles and gives one factual comment.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No previous fitness lesson required; students need the stop signal and 1–5 effort scale.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** No previous fitness lesson required; students need the stop signal and 1–5 effort scale.  
**Current lesson:** As the opening fitness lesson it establishes the two things the whole pathway depends on: choosing a level you can actually control, and rating your own effort honestly on a 1–5 scale. It withholds any teaching of the movement patterns themselves — the stations use only simple, self-limiting exercises — which FIT-02 addresses directly.  
**Next lesson:** FIT-02 — movement quality in squat, hinge, push, and brace.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- No maximal efforts or work to failure; water and rest are available without penalty; mats and benches must be stable.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use only force you can control and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn foundation strength, aerobic endurance, and self-selected load. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space — not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Walk, lateral step, partial squat, wall push, knee lift, and balance for 30 seconds each with gradual intensity.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** Teach four foundation stations: sit-to-stand, wall push, marching in place, and quadruped or standing brace. Work 25 seconds and recover for 35 seconds while a partner checks one marker.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** quality before quantity; breathe continuously; keep two repetitions in reserve; choose a manageable level; stop for warning signs.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** Complete two circuit rounds. Before each station choose a foundation or standard level; after it, rate effort 1–5 and change only time, range, or pace if needed.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** scan before acting, select free space, and reset after each attempt.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied area, late decision, and repeating without feedback.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Quality Circuit — earn one point for safe technique, one for an appropriate effort rating, and one for a sensible adjustment. Repetition totals are not compared. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation — larger target and no defender; intermediate — smaller target; advanced — one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The right level is the one where your technique holds for the whole interval and you could still do two more — nobody in this room is training to exhaustion.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- Starting at too high an intensity: a clear drop in quality already in the first round; correct by starting at an effort rating of 3 out of 5.
- **Breath holding under effort:** facial tension and inability to speak; correct by breathing out during the effort.
- **Uncontrolled range of movement:** momentum or knees falling inward; correct by reducing the range and setting a clear end point.
- **Cutting the recovery short:** the student starts a round before time is up; correct with a central transition signal and entry into the recovery area.
- **Quantity over quality:** counting repetitions instead of moving well; correct with one quality target per station.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment use before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without increasing contact.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, load or resistance, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** preparation, execution, target adjustment, spacing, and explanation.  
**Success:** all five measures are shown consistently and the student explains one cue.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for preparation or decision.  
**Not yet successful:** spacing or force remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before FIT-02 — movement quality in squat, hinge, push, and brace.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to FIT-02 — movement quality in squat, hinge, push, and brace.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It sets the norm for every fitness lesson that follows — that students choose their own load, judge their own effort, and are never asked to work to failure — which is what makes fitness training safe and repeatable in a school class.  
**What develops?** Self-selection of an appropriate level, interoceptive awareness through the 1–5 effort scale, movement quality sustained across a work interval, and the judgement to adjust load mid-session.  
**Why not skip?** Circuits run without self-regulation become competitions to complete the most repetitions, in which technique collapses and the least fit students work hardest and enjoy it least; establishing the effort scale first prevents that for the rest of the unit.
