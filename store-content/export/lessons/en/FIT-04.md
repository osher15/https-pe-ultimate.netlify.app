# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Fitness 04 — Self-Regulated Partner Circuit
**Lesson code:** FIT-04-EN
**Sport:** Fitness
**Subtopic:** strength, endurance, cooperation
**Age:** 12–16
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, mats, timer, level cards
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Choose an appropriate load and maintain technique and communication in a partner circuit.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Chooses the exercise version they can control at each station and states the body signal that justified the choice.
- Holds the named technical cue for the full work interval at 4 of 5 stations, stopping if the shape breaks.
- As observer, reports what was actually seen by naming the cue, rather than counting repetitions or offering encouragement alone.
- Respects a partner's chosen level without pressuring them to match their own, in every rotation.
- Records one measure across the two rounds and changes one variable in response to it.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
FIT-03 — aerobic intervals.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** FIT-03 — aerobic intervals.  
**Current lesson:** It adds the partner to the self-regulation of FIT-01 and FIT-03: students now work in pairs, one performing and one observing, which means learning to give useful feedback and, crucially, to respect a partner's level rather than impose their own. It withholds designing the circuit itself — the stations are still set by the teacher — which FIT-05 hands over to them.  
**Next lesson:** FIT-05 — designing a balanced personal circuit.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four to six stations; cones, mats, timer, and level cards. Setup 5 minutes; pack-down 3.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “You are your partner's coach today, not their competition. Your job is to tell them what you actually saw — and never to talk them into a level that is not theirs.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — choose your version, keep control, observe, give evidence, switch — and model the difference between “well done” and “your heels stayed down for all six reps”.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle where the level cards can be seen.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the whole body for a mixed strength and endurance circuit and rehearse the observer role before it matters.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then pairs.  
**Instructions:** walk and jog with direction changes on a colour call; add hip circles, arm circles, half-squats, and knee lifts; then in pairs take turns performing five slow squats while the partner watches one cue only and reports it aloud.  
**Teaching points:** the observer names one thing seen, not a verdict; practise the sentence “I saw… so try…” now.  
**Transitions:** move to stations on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — observer watches from one fixed angle; advanced — observer names the cue before the set begins.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** establish the two roles clearly before the circuit starts moving at pace.  
**Procedure:** at four stations pairs alternate performer and observer, the observer given one specific cue to watch at each station and required to report it as “I saw… so try…”. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback.  
**Organisation:** pairs at four stations with level cards showing foundation and standard versions; three to four pairs working side by side at each station, with a marked rotation route.  
**Teaching points:** the performer chooses their own level and announces it before starting; the observer watches the named cue and nothing else; feedback describes the movement, never judges the person.  
**Common errors:** the observer counting repetitions instead of watching form; feedback reduced to “good” or “keep going”; the performer choosing a level to match their partner; both students performing at once so nobody observes.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** run the full circuit twice, so a measure can be recorded and one variable deliberately changed.  
**Procedure:** two rounds of six stations, 40 seconds at each station (about 8 minutes of work plus signalled transitions), each student choosing their variation at every station, recording one measure in round one, and changing exactly one variable — time, range, or pace — in round two. Partners swap performer and observer roles at every station change, and each round starts with the same performer, so every student works at the same stations in both rounds.  
**Organisation:** six stations with cards and a central timer, pairs rotating one way, a recovery lane available at any moment.  
**Teaching points:** change one variable only, so its effect can actually be attributed; a level that felt right in round one may not in round two, and changing it is a good decision rather than a failure; recovery is available without asking.  
**Common errors:** changing three things at once; refusing to drop a level when technique breaks; skipping the recording so round two has no basis; partners pushing each other to keep up.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** close with a partner task that scores coaching quality as highly as performance.  
**Rules:** Partner challenge — a pair scores one point for each station where the performer held the named cue for the whole interval, and one point for each piece of observer feedback that named a specific cue rather than a generality. Repetition counts earn nothing, and no pair is ranked against another.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a pair can score full marks working entirely at foundation level, which is exactly the point; the feedback points make the observer's job as valuable as the performer's.  
**Common errors:** feedback slipping back into encouragement once the pair is tired; a performer raising their level to score more and losing the cue; observers awarding points generously to a friend.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The best partner here today was not the strongest — it was the one who told you exactly what they saw and let you pick your own level.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their level choice or the feedback they gave, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the heart rate, collect mats and cards by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- Partner imposing the other learner's load: control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with themselves.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** FIT-05 — designing a balanced personal circuit. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It turns fitness from a private struggle into a shared, coached activity, and it teaches the social skill that makes partner work safe: respecting somebody else's chosen level instead of imposing your own.  
**What develops?** Observation of a specific technical cue, descriptive rather than evaluative feedback, independent load selection while being watched, and evidence-based adjustment across rounds.  
**Why not skip?** Partner work introduced without these rules becomes a competition in which the less fit student is pushed to match the fitter one — which is where technique collapses, injuries happen, and students learn to dislike fitness training.
