# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 10 — Final Applied Game and Pathway Assessment
**Lesson code:** VB-10-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** authentic application and assessment
**Age:** 13–16
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Combine serve, reception, set, coverage, and decisions in safe play.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Serves into the court to start a rally and makes an appropriate choice on the ball in 3 of 4 situations, judged against the criteria stated at the start.
- Contributes to a three-contact rally by receiving, setting, or attacking as the situation requires, in 4 of 5 rallies.
- Recovers to a base position and covers a team-mate who has moved to play the ball, in 3 of 5 rallies.
- Calls clearly and plays safely at the net, with no contact and no reaching over, throughout every game.
- Names one personal strength and one target, each supported by something actually observed rather than a general impression.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-09 — 3v3 play and rotation.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-09 — 3v3 play and rotation.  
**Current lesson:** It teaches nothing new and instead gathers evidence, asking students to combine the serve, reception, set, attack, and coverage of the whole pathway in observed 3v3 play against criteria shared in advance. It withholds no further content here — what it leaves to an advanced unit is 4v4, jump attacking, and complex blocking systems.  
**Next lesson:** Advanced pathway: 4v4 and complex attack.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today I am not counting who wins. I am watching your first contact, your calling, and whether you get back to base — and I will tell you exactly what I saw.”  
**Purpose:** make the assessment criteria transparent before any play begins, so nobody is judged against a standard they did not know.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle with the five criteria displayed or read aloud.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the whole body for continuous play and briefly revisit each pathway skill, so the assessment is fair to students who need a reminder.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then trios.  
**Instructions:** jog with direction changes on a colour call, adding arm circles, wrist rotations, and low shuffles; then in trios spend one minute each on forearm passing, setting, and a controlled send, calling every ball.  
**Teaching points:** this is rehearsal, not yet assessment; ask now if a criterion is unclear.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — soft ball with a catch permitted; advanced — continuous contacts with no catch.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** let every student sharpen the one skill they feel least sure of before being observed.  
**Procedure:** five stations — underhand serve, forearm reception, overhead set, standing attack, and base recovery — with each student choosing one personal focus. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, rotating on the signal.  
**Organisation:** five marked stations; nothing is recorded at this stage, so mistakes are still free.  
**Teaching points:** choose the station that is genuinely weakest, not the most enjoyable; one correction per student.  
**Common errors:** treating the stations as a competition; practising what is already secure; rushing the rotation.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** gather structured evidence in short rounds in which every student is seen in every role.  
**Procedure:** 3v3 rounds observed against the five criteria, with an observer recording behaviour only — never a personal judgement. Roles rotate every minute, so nobody is assessed solely as a receiver or solely as an attacker.  
**Organisation:** groups of six to eight per court with two rotating observers holding a simple criteria card; the teacher watches one student for 45–60 seconds at a time.  
**Teaching points:** play at a pace where the choice is visible; include every team-mate; the observer writes what happened, not whether it was good.  
**Common errors:** the strongest student taking every ball; observers recording opinions; play speeding up until nothing is observable; quiet courts with no calling.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** provide the fairest evidence available — continuous play in which nothing is cued and every student decides for themselves.  
**Rules:** 3v3 with every rally started by a serve; one point for winning the rally, a bonus point for any rally using all three contacts, and a bonus point when every player touched the ball across the set of rallies. The game score is recorded separately and is explicitly not the grade.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a losing team can show the better volleyball, and the evidence will say so; keep calling.  
**Common errors:** chasing the score and abandoning the criteria; excluding a weaker team-mate late in the game; first-contact returns under pressure.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “What I write down about you today is a contact you controlled and a call you made, not a point you won — so tell me your best one, and the thing you want to fix.”  
**Learning summary:** each student records one strength and one target, supported by something they or their observer actually saw.  
**Feedback:** the teacher gives brief individual feedback and links the evidence to the next unit; no public ranking.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Judging success only by winning:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per trio and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** serve and decision-making, three-contact rally, base recovery and coverage, calling and net safety, personal reflection. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** Advanced pathway: 4v4 and complex attack. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is the only point in the pathway where a teacher can see whether the separate contacts have fused into actual play, and it does so against criteria the students knew in advance rather than a private impression.  
**What develops?** Integration of serve, reception, set, attack, and coverage under fatigue, independent decision-making with no cue from the teacher, self-assessment against public standards, and the responsibility to include every team-mate when a result is at stake.  
**Why not skip?** Volleyball skills practised only in pairs often vanish the moment a net and an opponent appear; without an applied game the unit ends with no evidence, and grading on rallies won would simply reward whoever already plays outside school.
