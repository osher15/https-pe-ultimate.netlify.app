# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Fitness 02 — Movement Quality: Squat, Hinge, Push, and Brace
**Lesson code:** FIT-02-EN
**Sport:** Fitness
**Subtopic:** fundamental movement patterns
**Age:** 10–13
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, mats, timer, level cards
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Perform four fundamental patterns with control and a safe personal range.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Performs a squat with the heels down, knees tracking over the toes, and a neutral spine, to a depth they can control, in 4 of 5 repetitions.
- Performs a hip hinge by pushing the hips backwards with a flat back and only slight knee bend, keeping the movement at the hips rather than the lower back.
- Performs a wall or incline push with the body in one straight line from head to heels, lowering under control rather than dropping.
- Holds a brace with steady breathing for the set time, without the hips sagging or lifting, and stops the moment the position breaks.
- Observes a partner, reports one factual cue about their range or alignment, and states a personal goal for the next round.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
FIT-01 — self-regulated strength and endurance circuit.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** FIT-01 — self-regulated strength and endurance circuit.  
**Current lesson:** It teaches the four movement patterns FIT-01 only used: squat, hip hinge, push, and brace are now taught individually, slowly, and with a self-selected range. It withholds all conditioning intensity — nothing here is fast or fatiguing, because the aim is a correct pattern — which FIT-03 begins to load with aerobic intervals.  
**Next lesson:** FIT-03 — aerobic intervals at a personal pace.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four to six stations; cones, mats, timer, and level cards. Setup 5 minutes; pack-down 3.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “These four shapes are the ones your body uses every day — sitting down, picking something up, pushing a door, holding yourself steady. Learn them slowly now and everything else this term gets easier.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — stable stance, keep breathing, control the range, stop for pain — and explain that range is personal, not a class standard.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle where the demonstration can be seen from the side.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise hips, knees, shoulders, and spine through the exact ranges the four patterns will use.  
**Structure:** individual movement in personal space.  
**Instructions:** walk and change direction on a colour call; add ankle rocks, hip circles, arm circles, and gentle trunk rotations; then rehearse a quarter-range version of each of the four patterns, five repetitions each, with no load and no timing.  
**Teaching points:** the warm-up rehearsal is the pattern in miniature — if it is wrong here, it will be wrong under effort.  
**Transitions:** move to stations on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — use a chair or wall for support throughout; advanced — add a brief pause at the bottom of each range.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** learn each pattern in isolation, slowly enough that the shape can be seen and corrected.  
**Procedure:** students work through the four patterns in turn — sit-to-stand squat, hip hinge to a marker, wall or incline push, and a brace — performing each slowly while a partner checks one alignment cue. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback.  
**Organisation:** pairs at four stations with mats and level cards showing a foundation and a standard version of each pattern.  
**Teaching points:** squat with the heels down and knees tracking over the toes; hinge by sending the hips backwards with a flat back; push with the body in one straight line; brace by breathing steadily rather than holding the breath.  
**Common errors:** heels lifting or knees falling inward in the squat; rounding the lower back in the hinge; hips sagging or piking in the push and brace; holding the breath throughout.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** keep the four patterns correct when they follow one another in a circuit.  
**Procedure:** a four-station circuit, one pattern per station, with the partner observing a single named cue and reporting it before roles change every minute. Each student selects the foundation or standard level at each station.  
**Organisation:** four stations with level cards, three to four pairs working side by side at each station (enough space for the whole class), a central timer, and a marked one-way rotation route.  
**Teaching points:** choose the level at which the shape holds for the whole interval; a correct foundation version is worth far more than a broken standard one; stop the interval if the pattern breaks rather than pushing on.  
**Common errors:** choosing the standard level for appearance and losing the shape; speeding up as the timer runs down; the observer counting repetitions instead of watching alignment; skipping the level choice entirely.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** finish with a challenge in which only correct repetitions count at all.  
**Rules:** Quality challenge — at each station a student must produce three repetitions that their partner judges to meet the named cue; a repetition that misses the cue simply does not count, with no penalty. Speed is irrelevant and totals are never compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** three correct repetitions are a genuine achievement and may take several attempts; observers judge the named cue only, not the whole movement.  
**Common errors:** rushing to accumulate attempts; observers passing repetitions out of kindness; choosing a level too hard to produce three clean repetitions; technique degrading at the final station.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A squat you can control is worth more than a deep one you cannot — your range is your own, and it grows by practising the shape, not by forcing it.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their range or alignment, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect mats and cards by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Speed replacing control:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with themselves.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** FIT-03 — aerobic intervals at a personal pace. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** These four patterns cover almost every way the body loads itself in daily life and in sport, so teaching them properly once protects a student far beyond the fitness unit.  
**What develops?** A controlled squat and hip hinge, alignment under load in a push, trunk stability with continuous breathing, and the judgement to work in a range that can be controlled rather than one that looks impressive.  
**Why not skip?** Students who meet loaded training without these patterns learn them by accident, usually with rounded backs and collapsing knees; a fitness unit built on unpractised patterns simply adds repetitions to a faulty shape.
