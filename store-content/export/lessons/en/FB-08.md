# 1. Lesson identity
**Name/code:** Attack-to-Defence Transition After Loss; FB-08-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Football; reaction, recovery, protect the centre
**Age/experience:** 12–15; developing
**Duration/students:** 45 minutes; 18–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 8–12 balls, 28 cones, bibs
**Space/complexity:** field or gym; advanced
# 2. Overall purpose
Develop a quick, safe team response to losing possession: stop, recover position, and protect the central route before trying to regain the ball.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- React within two seconds in 6/8 losses.
- The nearest player delays without contact while the partner recovers centrally.
- Keep the body between ball and goal.
- Use one agreed communication word.
- Restore defensive shape within five seconds.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
2v1, closing lanes, and off-ball movement from Lessons 05–07. Students needing support respond to a signal without opponents.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Football 07—attacking 2v1.
- **Current:** It adds the moment the pathway has so far ignored — the two seconds after the ball is lost — splitting the response into the nearest player delaying and the far player recovering to protect the centre; it withholds continuous play, since every transition is triggered by a signal and then stops, which Football 09 makes uninterrupted.
- **Next:** Football 09—small-sided transition game.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** stop, turn, recover.
- **Social:** responsibility and communication.
- **Cognitive:** recognise possession change.
- **Tactical:** delay and protect the centre.
- **Emotional:** recover from error without blame.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four or five 10×14 metre areas with a central gate and recovery line. **Setup:** 8 minutes; **pack-down:** 5. Groups of 4–6; short games.
# 8. Safety
- No tackling/contact.
- No blind backward sprint.
- Defence up to 70%.
- Stop at the boundary; no entering other areas; ground passes.
- Retrieve after scan; the teacher stops an uncontrolled sequence.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes. “Loss is not blame time; switch to defence within two seconds.” Purpose: nearest delays, far player protects centre.
## Warm-up
7 minutes. Pairs pass; on signal the player without the ball recovers to the central cone while the other advances slowly. 3 minutes walking, 2 turning, 2 with ball. **Variations:** colour/sound.
## Main activity A
10 minutes. A trio passes; on a colour, roles change: the nearest player delays the ball and the far player recovers to the central gate. Rotate after four cues. **Cues:** quick controlled first step, body to goal, call “recover.” Errors: everyone to ball, standing, blind backward run, no communication.
## Main activity B
10 minutes. A pair keeps the ball against two shadows. On a cue/pass to an opponent, possession switches; the losing pair recovers and protects the gate for eight seconds. **Cues:** nearest delays, far covers, no immediate steal. **Errors:** both press, centre open, contact, argument.
## Applied game
10 minutes. 3v3 with two gates. Defensive point when two players get between ball and goal within five seconds after loss; attacking point for dribbling through a gate. **Variations:** 3v2; neutral player; two-touch.
## Closure
5 minutes. **Message:** “Lose it—delay, recover, protect centre.” Teams name one good response; collect.
# 10. Common errors
- **Everyone chases the ball:** assign near/far roles.
- **Standing after loss:** use a code word.
- **Blind backward run:** half-turn and recovery steps.
- **Contact pressure:** delay at distance.
- **No communication:** one agreed word.
- **Blaming a player:** feedback after the sequence only.
# 11. Teaching points
- React in two seconds.
- Nearest delays.
- Far protects centre.
- Body between ball and goal.
- Recover while looking.
- Brief communication.
- No blame.
- Shape before steal.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** signal without opponent. **Intermediate:** 2v2 at 60%. **Advanced:** 3v3/five seconds. **Large group:** five areas. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** 8×10. **Limited equipment:** one ball per area. **Different needs:** closer line, visual/sound cue, walking, fixed central role, partner.
# 13. Assessment
Reaction, role split, central protection, communication, safety. **Success:** at least four of the five objectives achieved, each by its own stated measure. **Partial:** three objectives, or achieved with signal support only. **Not yet:** everyone chases the ball, contact occurs, or no recovery happens. Return to Main A.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were roles understood and time realistic? Student: What did you do immediately after loss? Improve: area and target time.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Football 09—transition game. **If not:** repeat Main A; return to Football 06 for defence.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it teaches that play does not pause for disappointment: the seconds straight after a turnover are when a team is most disorganised, and what happens in them is governed almost entirely by habit.
- **Develops:** recognition of the moment possession changes, the discipline of splitting into a delaying player and a recovering player, communication under time pressure, and the emotional control to respond instead of assigning blame.
- **Why not skip:** without it a lost ball produces either a frozen team or all four players chasing at once; the centre is left open every time, and the lesson students take away is that mistakes are somebody's fault rather than the start of the next action.
