# 1. Lesson identity
**Name/code:** Final Applied Game and Pathway Assessment; FB-10-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Football; integrated control, pass, movement, finish, defence, transition
**Age/experience:** 13–16; developing
**Duration/students:** 45 minutes; 16–32
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 4–6 balls, 32 cones, bibs, small goals, assessment cards
**Space/complexity:** field or gym; advanced
# 2. Overall purpose
Assess whether students combine skill, decisions, and cooperation in safe play without reducing achievement to goals or prior individual ability.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Make an appropriate choice in three of four ball situations.
- Create or use a passing lane at least four times.
- React to a loss within two seconds and complete the change of role within five seconds.
- Defend centrally without contact.
- Participate actively and respectfully in every game.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Components of Football 01–09. Students not ready receive a neutral player, support zone, or active observer role with gradual entry.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Football 09—4v4 transitions.
- **Current:** It adds no new content and instead gathers evidence, asking each student to play 4v4 while observers record decision, lane use, transition, defence, and participation against criteria stated in advance; it withholds nothing further in this pathway — what follows is chosen from that evidence, whether an advanced football unit or the handball pathway.
- **Next:** handball unit or advanced football based on evidence.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** integrated skill.
- **Social:** fairness and cooperation.
- **Cognitive:** decide and explain.
- **Tactical:** width, advantage, transition.
- **Emotional:** self-assessment under pressure.
# 7. Equipment and space
Three or four 16×22 metre courts with two goals per side and an observer card. **Setup:** 10 minutes; **pack-down:** 6. Teams of 4–5; short games and two rotating observers.
# 8. Safety
- No tackle, slide, or contact.
- Stable goals and nobody behind them.
- Low ball.
- Stop out of bounds; defence up to 75%.
- No public ranking; rest for pain/fatigue; the teacher stops unsafe play.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes. “We assess choices, movement, cooperation, and safety—not who scores most.” Purpose: transparent criteria.
## Warm-up
7 minutes. Groups of four pass and move through four cones; each minute changes the condition: two-touch, turn, loss response. **Variations:** walk, soft ball, larger area.
## Main activity A
8 minutes. Three two-minute stations: pass/receive, turn/finish, close lane. Each student chooses one personal focus. Use one feedback cue only.
## Main activity B
10 minutes. Four attack three; roles change after loss. An observer marks five behaviours without an immediate numerical grade. **Cues:** width, free player, response, no contact.
## Applied game
12 minutes. Two five-minute games plus a two-minute transition. The game score remains, but assessment is separate: choice, lane, transition, defence, participation. Game bonus when all four participate. **Variations:** neutral, three-touch, side goals.
## Closure
5 minutes. **Message:** “Your best moment today was a decision, not a goal — name it, name one thing to work on, and that becomes your target for the next unit.” Each student records one strength and one target; the teacher gives brief feedback and links evidence to next planning. Collect and count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Goals-only play:** repeat process criteria.
- **One player dominates:** bonus for all four.
- **Observer judges personality:** mark behaviour only.
- **Assessment anxiety:** adaptation round without recording.
- **Contact rises:** pause and restore the spacing rule.
- **No transition:** code word; reaction begins within two seconds and reorganisation is complete within five seconds.
# 11. Teaching points
- Decision before outcome.
- Include all.
- Width/support.
- Quick transition.
- Defend by position.
- Descriptive feedback.
- No public comparison.
- One personal target.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** 4v3 or neutral. **Intermediate:** 4v4, three touches. **Advanced:** two-touch, smaller goals. **Large group:** four courts. **Small group:** 3v3. **Limited space:** 12×16. **Limited equipment:** cone goals. **Different needs:** support zone, soft ball, extra time, partner, observer-player role; assess an approved personal target.
# 13. Assessment
Choice, lane, transition, defence, participation. **Success:** 4/5 in two games. **Partial:** three, or with support. **Not yet:** fewer than three or a safety risk. Never grade goals alone; return to the missing component.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were criteria fair and time sufficient? Student: What is your strength and target? Improve: select the next unit from evidence.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** advanced unit or handball. **If not:** targeted return to 02 pass, 03 control, 05 movement, 06 defence, 07 advantage, or 08 transition.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it is the only honest test of the pathway: it asks what a student does in live play, rather than what they can demonstrate in a drill or how fast they happen to run.
- **Develops:** self-assessment against public criteria, the ability to explain a choice after the fact, sustained cooperation when a result is at stake, and the habit of setting one's own next target.
- **Why not skip:** grading on goals alone rewards students who already play at a club and tells the teacher nothing about the rest; without multidimensional evidence the next unit is planned on an impression instead of on what the class can actually do.
