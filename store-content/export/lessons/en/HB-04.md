# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 04 — Accurate Overarm Throw to Target
**Lesson code:** HB-04-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** accuracy and standing finish
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student performs a controlled overarm throw to a target with aligned body and safe follow-through.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Takes a side-on stance with the non-throwing shoulder toward the target before releasing, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Releases the ball with the elbow above shoulder height, not from the side.
- Transfers weight from the back foot to the front foot and finishes with the hand forward and down, not across the body.
- Hits the cone-gate target in 3 of 5 throws from their own working distance.
- Hits the open coloured target shown just before release, in 3 of 4 attempts, instead of throwing to a pre-decided zone.
- Chooses the open target zone in 3 of 5 situations, justifying the choice, and gives a partner one factual comment on stance, elbow, or follow-through.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-03 — movement and passing lanes.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-03 — movement and passing lanes.  
**Current lesson:** adds the shot to the passing and movement of HB-03: the same overarm action is now aimed at a target, with a side-on stance, weight transfer, and a safe follow-through. It withholds all movement into the throw — every attempt is made from a balanced standing position — which is exactly what HB-05 adds with the three-step rhythm.  
**Next lesson:** HB-05 — three-step rhythm and finish.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 metres clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Nobody crosses the throwing line; play without a goalkeeper so nobody stands in the firing lane, and nobody stands behind the target.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today the target is accuracy, not power. A ball that hits the zone at half speed is a better throw than one that misses hard.”  
**Purpose:** state the three success cues — side-on, high elbow, finish forward — plus the rule that nobody walks in front of the throwing line.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing the targets, balls held still.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise the shoulder and trunk thoroughly before any throwing at effort, and rehearse the throwing shape without a ball.  
**Structure:** free movement, then shadow throws, then paired passing.  
**Instructions:** jog with arm circles forward and back; step into a side-on stance and mime the throw slowly ten times, feeling the weight move from back foot to front; then pair up and pass at chest height over a short distance.  
**Teaching points:** lead with the elbow, not the hand; the front shoulder points at the target before the arm moves.  
**Transitions:** move to work zones on the colour call.  
**Variations:** foundation — soft or smaller ball; advanced — add a half-turn before the throw.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: build the standing throwing sequence.  
**Procedure:** pairs stand either side of a cone gate and throw through it, starting close. After three consecutive successes the pair steps back one metre; after a miss they step in again. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, in four groups.  
**Organisation:** one ball per pair, a marked throwing line, and a clear retrieval route behind each thrower.  
**Teaching points:** stand side-on with the non-throwing shoulder to the target; keep the elbow above shoulder height; step onto the front foot as the arm comes through; finish the hand forward and down; throw at about 60% effort.  
**Common errors:** square shoulders and a pushed throw from the chest; a low elbow that drops the ball short and to the side; no weight transfer, so only the arm works; whipping the arm across the body so the ball pulls off target.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: select an open target zone.  
**Procedure:** four coloured targets are fixed to the wall or set as cone goals. A partner shows a colour just before release, so the thrower aims at a target chosen for them rather than one already picked. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** trios — thrower, colour giver, retriever — with the colour giver standing beside, never in front of, the thrower.  
**Teaching points:** keep the preparation identical whichever colour appears; turn the front shoulder to the new target rather than steering with the wrist alone; look at the target, not at the ball.  
**Common errors:** starting the throw before the colour is shown; changing direction with the hand only, which costs accuracy; rushing and losing the side-on stance; adding force for the further targets instead of adjusting the angle.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** use the throw as the end of a team sequence rather than an isolated skill, with no goalkeeper so nobody stands in a firing line.  
**Rules:** 3v3 in a marked area; a team scores by hitting a marked wall zone or cone goal, but only after three completed passes. No contact, no stealing from the hands, three seconds maximum while holding, and no throw above chest height.  
**Teaching points:** the last pass should leave the thrower already side-on; choose the open zone rather than the nearest one.  
**Common errors:** shooting on sight before three passes; throwing hard at head height; defenders closing to contact distance.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A throw that hits the target at half power beats one that misses at full power — in handball we aim first and add speed later.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their stance, elbow, or follow-through, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Low elbow and side release:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “high elbow.”
- **Late transition:** seen in standing after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
- Aim for about 60% of maximum effort to protect accuracy before adding speed.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** performance against each of the six learning objectives in section 3 — stance, elbow, weight transfer and follow-through, hitting the target, hitting the coloured late-cue target, and the open-zone choice. **Success:** at least 5 of the 6 listed objectives achieved, each per its own stated measure. **Partial:** 3–4 objectives achieved. **Not yet:** 0–2 objectives, or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-05 — three-step rhythm and finish. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** it builds accuracy and a safe shoulder action before speed, movement, and jumping are layered on — the order that protects a growing arm.  
**Develops:** the overarm throwing pattern with a high elbow, weight transfer from back foot to front, force calibration to distance, and target selection under a late cue.  
**Why not skip:** students left to invent a throw push the ball from the chest with a square body, which is inaccurate, produces no range, and loads the shoulder badly once they begin throwing hard.
