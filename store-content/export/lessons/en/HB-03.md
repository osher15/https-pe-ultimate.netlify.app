# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 03 — Off-Ball Movement and Passing Lanes
**Lesson code:** HB-03-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** support after passing
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** beginner–developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student creates a new passing angle after releasing the ball to sustain safe team possession.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Scans the space and the defender's position before deciding where to pass or move, in 3 of 4 situations.
- Shows two hands as a target before each reception and holds them up until the ball arrives, in 4 of 5 receptions.
- After releasing a pass, reaches an open angle with no defender on the line within two seconds, choosing the unblocked passing lane over a covered one, in 4 of 5 sequences.
- Keeps at least two arm lengths from the nearest team-mate so that two players never occupy the same lane.
- Chooses correctly between a short pass and a deeper supporting position according to the situation, in 3 of 5 opportunities.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-02 — dribbling, stopping, and changing direction.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous:** HB-02 — dribbling, stopping, and changing direction.  
**Current:** adds the off-ball half of possession to the ball control of HB-02: having released a pass, the student now vacates the spot at once and arrives at a new angle where the ball can reach them. It withholds shooting at a goal — every sequence ends in another controlled pass — which HB-04 adds.  
**Next:** HB-04 — accurate overarm throw.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 metres clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “A pass is not the end of your job — it is the start of your next run. The moment the ball leaves your hands, find a place where it can come back to you.”  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle on the sideline, balls held still.  
**Purpose:** state the three success cues — pass, move, show hands — plus the stop signal, the zone boundaries, and the no-contact rule.
## Warm-up
7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** raise the pulse, mobilise shoulders and ankles, and wake up visual scanning before any decision is required.  
**Structure:** free movement in the marked area, then pairs with one ball.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add shoulder circles and side steps; then in pairs pass at chest height while walking, taking three steps to a new position after every pass.  
**Teaching points:** show both hands before the ball arrives; pass to where the partner is going, not where they are.  
**Transitions:** on the colour signal, pairs walk to their numbered work zone.  
**Variations:** foundation — stationary passing; advanced — call the partner's name before releasing.
## Main activity A
10 minutes — goal: leave the passing spot for a new angle.  
**Procedure:** In triangles of three, each student passes and then runs to the open cone, so the empty point is always filled. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback; four groups per zone. Begin at walking pace and move to a jog only once the triangle holds its shape.  
**Organisation:** one ball and three cones per triangle, 4–5 metres apart; a fourth student observes one cue and reports it.  
**Teaching points:** pass, then move immediately — do not admire the pass; run to the empty cone at an angle rather than straight at the receiver; show two hands before arriving; keep the triangle wide so every lane stays open.  
**Common errors:** standing still after releasing the ball; running towards the ball instead of away to a new angle; two students filling the same cone; hands shown only once the ball is already travelling.
## Main activity B
10 minutes — goal: recognise an unblocked passing lane.  
**Procedure:** Three attackers keep the ball against one shadow defender who blocks a lane without attempting a steal. The attackers must find the lane the defender has left open; roles change every minute so that everyone defends.  
**Organisation:** one zone per group of four, defender in a bib, teacher calls the rotation.  
**Teaching points:** scan before receiving so the choice is already made; pass to the team-mate the defender is not covering; when your lane is blocked, move to open a new one rather than forcing the ball through; keep the pass at chest height.  
**Common errors:** forcing the pass into a covered lane; all three attackers drifting to one side; the defender stealing or making contact; deciding only once the ball is already in the hands.
## Applied game
10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold the pass-and-move habit together under game-like pressure, without contact or elimination.  
**Rules:** 3v3 in a marked square. A point is scored for four consecutive passes, but only when every passer relocated after releasing the ball; three seconds maximum while holding; no contact and no stealing from the hands.  
**Teaching points:** talk briefly — a name or a call; the player furthest from the ball is usually the one who must move.  
**Common errors:** counting passes while standing still; crowding the ball carrier; silent play.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “If you can name where you ran after your last pass, you were playing handball — if you can only name where you threw it, you were watching.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence that they moved after passing, and one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion; the teacher comments on spacing and lane choice.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection of balls, cones, and bibs, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Standing after the pass:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “pass–move.”
- **Late transition:** seen in standing after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** performance against each of the five learning objectives in section 3 — scanning, hands-as-target, open-lane timing, spacing, and the pass/support choice. **Success:** at least 4 of the 5 listed objectives achieved, each per its own stated measure. **Partial:** 2–3 objectives achieved. **Not yet:** 0–1 objectives, or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-04 — accurate overarm throw. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** it teaches that involvement continues after the pass — the single idea separating a team that keeps possession from six individuals taking turns to throw.  
**Develops:** off-ball movement, scanning ahead of reception, the judgement to recognise a covered lane, and brief communication under mild pressure.  
**Why not skip:** without it every attack stops the instant the ball is released, the class collapses into a static circle, and the defending team only ever has to cover one player.
