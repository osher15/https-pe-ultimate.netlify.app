# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 08 — Soft-Ball Throw for Distance
**Lesson code:** AT-08-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** force transfer and throwing
**Age:** 11–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Throw a soft ball for distance from side-on stance with a fully cleared sector.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Stands side-on with the non-throwing shoulder towards the sector and the feet split, before every throw.
- Transfers weight from the back foot to the front foot during the throw, so the legs and trunk contribute rather than the arm alone, in 4 of 5 throws.
- Keeps the throwing elbow above shoulder height and releases forward and upward at roughly 45 degrees, in 3 of 4 throws.
- Throws only after the sector is confirmed clear and only on the signal, and never collects until collection is called.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's stance or weight transfer and states a personal goal for the next throw.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-07 — approach run and jump to zone.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-07 — approach run and jump to zone.  
**Current lesson:** It completes the three athletics disciplines by adding throwing: distance is now generated through a side-on stance and a transfer of weight from back foot to front, rather than by arm strength. It withholds any rotational or run-up throw, since all throwing here is from a standing or single-step position with soft implements — AT-09 combines running, jumping, and throwing into one circuit.  
**Next lesson:** AT-09 — combined athletics stations.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Distance comes from the ground, not from your arm. Stand side-on, push off the back foot, and let the throw travel up through your legs and body before it ever reaches your hand.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — side-on, high elbow, transfer weight, release forward — plus the absolute rules: throw only on the signal, and nobody enters the sector until collection is called.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle well behind the throwing line, all facing the sector.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise the shoulder and trunk thoroughly before any throwing at effort, and rehearse the weight transfer without a ball.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then shadow throws.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add arm circles forwards and backwards, trunk rotations, and side bends; then stand side-on and shadow the throw ten times, feeling the weight shift from back foot to front; finish with gentle short throws to a partner.  
**Teaching points:** the back foot pushes before the arm moves; the hips turn towards the target before the shoulder does.  
**Transitions:** move to throwing stations on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — lighter or larger soft ball; advanced — add a single step into the shadow throw.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** establish the release angle, which is what most limits distance in a school throw.  
**Procedure:** from a standing side-on stance, students throw a soft ball over a high gate — a rope or raised marker a few metres ahead — into a wide landing zone, so a flat throw simply fails to clear it. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four stations all throwing the same direction, one thrower per station, and a marked line nobody crosses until collection is called.  
**Teaching points:** stand side-on with the feet split; keep the elbow above the shoulder; release forward and upward, aiming over the gate rather than at the floor; finish with the weight on the front foot.  
**Common errors:** throwing flat and hard, which travels a short distance; standing square to the sector; a low elbow that pushes rather than throws; stepping across the line after release.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** add the step that makes the weight transfer larger and the throw longer without any extra effort.  
**Procedure:** students add a single step into the throw, moving the weight from the back foot through to the front as the arm comes through, and compare the distance with their standing throw. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** stations as before with a personal distance marker for each student; an observer watching only the feet and reporting whether the weight actually moved.  
**Teaching points:** the step goes towards the target, not across the body; the ball leaves the hand after the front foot has landed, never before; the same throw with a step should travel noticeably further with no extra arm effort.  
**Common errors:** stepping and throwing simultaneously, which loses the transfer; over-striding so balance is lost; the back foot never leaving the ground; trying to throw harder rather than better.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** apply the technique in a team task where safety procedure scores as heavily as distance.  
**Rules:** Team distance-zone challenge — teams throw in rotation into scoring zones, and a round counts only when the sector was fully cleared, the signal was obeyed, and collection happened on command. Points are also awarded for a correct side-on stance and a visible weight transfer. No individual distances are compared.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** the safety procedure is part of the score because in throwing events it is part of the event; land the ball in a zone rather than simply as far as possible.  
**Common errors:** throwing before the sector is confirmed clear; wandering forward to watch a throw land; reverting to a flat hard throw when a zone looks far; collecting without waiting for the call.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The longest throw here today came from the best legs, not the strongest arm — and nobody threw until the field was clear.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their stance or weight transfer, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect balls and cones on the signal, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- Throwing before the sector is clear: control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-09 — combined athletics stations. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Throwing for distance is the clearest demonstration in physical education that force travels from the ground upwards through the body, and it is the event where technique most visibly outperforms raw strength.  
**What develops?** A side-on throwing stance, sequential force transfer from legs through trunk to arm, judgement of release angle, and the disciplined safety routine every throwing event requires.  
**Why not skip?** Untaught, students throw flat and entirely with the arm, producing short distances, sore shoulders, and the belief that throwing is simply a matter of strength; the safety routine is also the one part of athletics where an untaught class is genuinely dangerous.
