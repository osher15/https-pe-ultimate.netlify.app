# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Fitness 03 — Aerobic Intervals at a Personal Pace
**Lesson code:** FIT-03-EN
**Sport:** Fitness
**Subtopic:** endurance and effort regulation
**Age:** 11–15
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, mats, timer, level cards
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Complete six intervals at a controlled pace and recover comfortable breathing between rounds.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Completes all six intervals without stopping early, holding a pace that is sustainable rather than maximal, in 4 of 5 rounds.
- Recovers to comfortable, conversational breathing by the end of each recovery period, in most rounds.
- Rates effort on the 1–5 scale after each interval and keeps it in the 3–4 range rather than reaching 5.
- Adjusts the pace of the next interval whenever the previous rating or recovery showed it was too hard.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's pacing or breathing and states a personal goal for the next round.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
FIT-02 — movement quality.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** FIT-02 — movement quality.  
**Current lesson:** It adds a cardiovascular demand to the patterns of FIT-02, using interval work so effort arrives in manageable pieces with recovery built in. It withholds any continuous long effort and all partner-imposed loading — each student sets their own pace and every interval is short — which FIT-04 changes by adding a partner.  
**Next lesson:** FIT-04 — self-regulated partner circuit.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four to six stations; cones, mats, timer, and level cards. Setup 5 minutes; pack-down 3.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Intervals are not sprints with breaks. Pick a pace you could repeat six times, and if round one turns out to be your hardest round, you chose wrong — fix it in round two.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — start conservatively, keep breathing, rate the effort, adjust the next round — and remind the class of the 1–5 scale from FIT-01.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle beside the working area.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** raise the heart rate progressively so the first interval does not begin from cold.  
**Structure:** individual movement in a marked area.  
**Instructions:** walk, then jog, changing direction on a colour call; add arm circles, knee lifts, and ankle rocks; finish with two 15-second efforts at the pace the student intends to hold, with full recovery between them.  
**Teaching points:** those two preview efforts are how you choose your pace — if they felt hard, go slower.  
**Transitions:** move between actions on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk and march intervals; advanced — add a third preview effort.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** experience a generous work-to-recovery ratio across several movement types, so pacing is learned before the demand rises.  
**Procedure:** 20 seconds of movement followed by 40 seconds of recovery, cycling through four modes — marching or jogging on the spot, lateral steps, step-touches, and easy shuttle walking. During each recovery the partner gives one brief comment.  
**Organisation:** four zones with a central timer, students spread two arm lengths apart, and a marked recovery lane nobody works in.  
**Teaching points:** the recovery is part of the training, not a gap in it; walk during recovery rather than standing still; keep the effort identical across all four modes.  
**Common errors:** treating the first interval as a race; standing still or sitting during recovery; holding the breath during effort; going harder in whichever mode the student enjoys most.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** tighten the recovery and add a rating after every interval, so students manage their own load in real time.  
**Procedure:** 30 seconds of work and 30 seconds of recovery, with each student calling an effort rating of 1–5 to their partner at the end of each interval and adjusting the next one accordingly. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** pairs, one working and one recording the ratings on a card, with the teacher watching for anyone whose rating and appearance disagree.  
**Teaching points:** aim to sit at 3 or 4, never 5; a rating that climbs every round means the starting pace was too high; a rating that never moves may mean it is too low.  
**Common errors:** reporting the rating the student thinks is expected rather than felt; reaching 5 by round three; ignoring the rating and repeating the same pace; partners comparing ratings with each other.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** finish with a task in which the most consistent student scores best, not the fastest.  
**Rules:** Group pacing game — each student predicts how much work they will complete in an interval, then tries to repeat exactly that amount in every later interval. Points are awarded for matching the prediction, not for exceeding it, so going hard early costs points. Totals are never compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** an honest, modest prediction repeated four times scores higher than a big first round followed by a collapse; use your effort rating to set the prediction.  
**Common errors:** predicting ambitiously to look strong; sprinting the final interval; comparing counts with neighbours; abandoning the prediction once tired.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Fitness is built by the round you can repeat, not the one that empties you — the whole skill is choosing a pace you could do again.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their pacing or effort rating, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the heart rate, collect cones and cards by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Maximal effort in round one:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with themselves.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** FIT-04 — self-regulated partner circuit. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Interval work is the most practical way to build cardiovascular fitness in a mixed-ability class, because every student works at their own pace inside the same structure and nobody is left behind or held back.  
**What develops?** Pace selection and repeatability, honest effort rating, active recovery between efforts, and the self-management to adjust a load mid-session rather than endure a bad choice.  
**Why not skip?** Without interval structure, cardiovascular work in school becomes a single long run in which the fittest students are barely challenged and the least fit are publicly exposed; the interval format gives both groups a genuine session.
