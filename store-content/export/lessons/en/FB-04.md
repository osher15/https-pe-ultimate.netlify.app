# 1. Lesson identity
**Name/code:** Drive Through and Finish Accurately; FB-04-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Football; forward dribble, preparation touch, accurate strike
**Age/experience:** 10–13; beginner–developing
**Duration/students:** 45 minutes; 20–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 10–15 balls, 30 cones, 4–6 small goals, bibs
**Space/complexity:** flat field or gym; intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Connect dribbling into space with a preparation touch and low accurate finish while controlling speed and shooting-area safety.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Drive through the entry gate with the ball within one step in 6/8.
- Prepare the ball to the striking side with the final touch.
- Hit a two-metre gate in 5/8.
- Slow and scan before striking.
- Leave the shooting lane immediately afterward.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Dribbling, turning, and protecting from Football 01–03. Students needing support finish from a stationary ball at short range.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Football 03—turn and protect.
- **Current:** It adds the end of the attacking sequence, joining the forward drive to a preparation touch and a low accurate strike on goal; it withholds any defender and any team-mate creating the chance — the student arrives at goal alone through an empty lane — which Football 05 begins to change.
- **Next:** Football 05—off-ball movement to create a finishing chance.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** dribble, preparation, strike.
- **Social:** rotation and feeding.
- **Cognitive:** distance and angle.
- **Tactical:** use a lane.
- **Emotional:** accuracy before power and resilience after a miss.
# 7. Equipment and space
Five 5×12 metre lanes with entry gate, preparation zone, and end goal. **Setup:** 8 minutes; **pack-down:** 5. Groups of 4–6, outside return route, no goalkeeper.
# 8. Safety
- One striker per lane.
- Nobody behind the goal.
- Strike only into a clear lane; keep the ball low and avoid power shots.
- Collect only on signal after balls stop; return outside.
- No tackling or contact.
- Check surface, goals, and laces.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes. “A good finish begins with the touch that prepares the ball, not with power.” Purpose: control–prepare–accuracy.
## Warm-up
7 minutes. Dribble between cones, cross a gate, sole-stop in the finish zone. After 4 minutes add a low pass through the goal. **Variations:** walking, wider gate, non-dominant foot.
## Main activity A
10 minutes. Dribble to the line, take a half-metre diagonal touch to the striking side, then stop before a shadow strike. Six each side. **Cues:** scan goal, support foot near ball, balanced body. **Errors:** touch too far, leaning back, no scan, full stop.
## Main activity B
10 minutes. Repeat and strike low through a two-metre goal from 5–7 metres. The striker exits sideways; the next retrieves and feeds. **Cues:** firm ankle, centre contact, follow through toward the target. **Errors:** excess power, toe poke, distant support foot, early entry.
## Applied game
10 minutes. Trios: performer, cue giver, retriever. Two goals; the colour before the preparation zone shows the open target. Score one for a correct choice and one for a hit; no power bonus. **Variations:** stationary; dribble; 40% trailing shadow.
## Closure
5 minutes. **Message:** “The touch before the shot decides the shot — set the ball where you can strike it, look up once, then pass it into the goal instead of blasting it.” Students name what improved accuracy; collect only on signal.
# 10. Common errors
- **Preparation touch too far:** use half a metre.
- **Eyes only on ball:** scan before the touch.
- **Support foot far:** mark a footprint.
- **Power strike:** shorten range and reduce force.
- **Toe poke:** use inside/instep as appropriate.
- **Early collection:** wait for the signal.
# 11. Teaching points
- Ball within a step.
- Touch to striking side.
- Scan.
- Support foot close.
- Firm ankle.
- Low strike.
- Accuracy before power.
- Side exit.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** stationary ball, 3–4 metres, wide gate. **Intermediate:** dribble, 5–7 metres. **Advanced:** late cue and 40% shadow. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** more attempts/two goals. **Limited space:** pass into gate. **Limited equipment:** cone goals and ball per trio. **Different needs:** soft ball, large target, no run, visual/sound cue, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
Control, preparation, scan, accuracy, safety. **Success:** at least four of the five objectives achieved, each by its own stated measure. **Partial:** three objectives, or achieved stationary only. **Not yet:** power shots, poor control, unsafe entry. Return to no-strike preparation.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were range, goals, and waiting time suitable? Student: Which touch prepared the strike and why accuracy first? Improve: distance, target, working foot.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Football 05—off-ball chance creation. **If not:** repeat Main A; return to Football 03 for turning.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it is where control becomes a scoring chance, and where students learn that a finish is an act of accuracy rather than a test of how hard they can kick.
- **Develops:** the preparation touch that sets the ball at a strikeable distance and angle, balance over a close support foot, a firm ankle through the strike, and the scan that locates the target before contact.
- **Why not skip:** untaught, finishing becomes a power contest that mostly misses, buries the ball over the goal, and is unsafe in a shared space; students also never learn that the decisive touch is the one before the shot.
