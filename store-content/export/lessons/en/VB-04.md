# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 04 — Forearm Serve Reception
**Lesson code:** VB-04-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** reception and platform angle
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Move behind an easy serve and direct reception to the setter zone.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Moves behind the flight of the serve and is stationary with the platform already formed before contact, in 4 of 5 receptions.
- Angles the platform towards the setter zone rather than straight back at the server, in 3 of 4 receptions.
- Directs the reception high into the setter zone so that a second contact is genuinely possible, in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Calls “mine” before entering the ball's path and keeps two arm lengths from the nearest team-mate.
- Takes serving, receiving, and setting roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's platform angle.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-03 — underhand serve.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-03 — underhand serve.  
**Current lesson:** It closes the loop the last two lessons opened: the serve of VB-03 is now received with the platform of VB-01 and directed to a target rather than merely returned. It withholds the full three-contact rally and any attack over the net — the sequence stops at the set — which VB-05 completes.  
**Next lesson:** VB-05 — three-contact sequence.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Receiving is not about hitting the ball back — it is about placing it where your setter is standing. Get your feet there first, hold the platform still, and angle it to the middle.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — feet to the ball, flat platform, angle to the middle — plus the calling rule that stops two students going for the same ball.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing a marked setter zone.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the legs for repeated low movement and rehearse reading the flight of a ball before receiving one.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then pairs.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add side steps, low shuffles, and forward–backward steps in the ready position; then in pairs one student lobs a high ball while the partner moves behind it and catches it at platform height with the arms already joined.  
**Teaching points:** judge where the ball will land, not where it is now; be stationary before it arrives.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — catch instead of play; advanced — lob to either side so the receiver must travel further.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** learn to direct, rather than merely return, a predictable ball.  
**Procedure:** a partner tosses a high arcing ball; the receiver moves behind it and plays it into a marked setter zone. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs per zone with a hoop or square of cones marking the setter zone; performer and observer alternate.  
**Teaching points:** move the feet so the ball arrives in front of the body, never at the side; form the platform early and keep it still; angle the shoulders towards the target; lift from the legs with no arm swing.  
**Common errors:** reaching sideways instead of stepping; swinging the arms at the ball, which sends it long; forming the platform at the last instant; standing upright between attempts.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** receive a genuine serve and hand it on, so the reception has a real consequence.  
**Procedure:** in triads, one student serves underhand as taught in VB-03, the second receives into the middle, and the third sets the ball. Roles rotate every minute so everyone serves, receives, and sets.  
**Organisation:** one triad per court, serving only on the signal, with the setter starting inside the marked zone.  
**Teaching points:** start low and ready before the serve is struck — watch the server's hand, not the ball in flight; call early and loudly; a reception that is too low cannot be set, so height matters more than direction at first.  
**Common errors:** standing upright and reacting late; passing flat and fast so the setter cannot reach the ball; nobody calling, so the ball drops between two players; the setter leaving the zone before the reception.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold the quality of the first contact together while a rally is genuinely contested.  
**Rules:** 2v2 over a low net with every rally started by an underhand serve; one point for winning the rally and a bonus point whenever the reception reached the setter zone, whichever team won the point. No spiking.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** the bonus rewards the first contact, so a lost rally with a good reception still scores; call every ball.  
**Common errors:** chasing the rally result and abandoning the target; both players receiving; receptions driven flat over the net instead of up to the setter.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The first contact decides the whole rally — play it high and to the middle, and your team still has two more chances.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their movement or platform angle, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Large arm swing:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per triad and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** posture, contact, direction, position, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-05 — three-contact sequence. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The first contact sets the ceiling on everything that follows: a reception that does not reach the setter removes two of the team's three touches, so the quality of the reception limits every contact that follows it.  
**What develops?** Reading the flight of a served ball early, footwork that gets the body behind it, angling a still platform to redirect rather than return, and the calling that prevents collisions between two receivers.  
**Why not skip?** Students who only ever return the serve straight back keep the ball in play but never build an attack, and the rally stays a two-team exchange of single contacts; two players converging silently on the same served ball is also a genuine collision risk.
