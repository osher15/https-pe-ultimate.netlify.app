# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 08 — Final Applied Game and Pathway Assessment
**Lesson code:** HB-08-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** authentic application and assessment
**Age:** 13–16
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student combines control, support, finishing, defence, and transition in a safe small-sided game.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Makes an appropriate choice — pass, dribble, or finish — in 3 of 4 situations with the ball, judged against the criteria stated at the start of the lesson.
- Creates or uses a passing lane at least four times per game by moving immediately after releasing the ball.
- Changes from attack to defence within three seconds of losing possession, in 4 of 5 turnovers.
- Defends by position without contact, holding the arm's-length distance taught in HB-06.
- Participates in every role across the games and names one personal strength and one target, each supported by something actually observed rather than a general impression.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-07 — fast 3v2 transition.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-07 — fast 3v2 transition.  
**Current lesson:** closes the base sequence by teaching nothing new and instead gathering evidence, asking students to combine control, support, finishing, no-contact defence, and transition in even-sided play observed against five criteria. Width, depth, and line-player support, plus crossing runs, legal screens, and team attacking principles, are left for the pathway that follows.  
**Next lesson:** HB-09 — width, depth, and line support in 4v4.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 m clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today I am not counting goals. I am watching what you choose, how you move after you pass, and how fast you get back — and I will tell you exactly what I saw.”  
**Purpose:** make the assessment criteria transparent before any play begins, so nobody is judged against a standard they did not know.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle with the five criteria displayed or read aloud.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the whole body for even-sided play and briefly revisit each pathway skill, so the assessment is fair to students who need a reminder.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then fours with a ball.  
**Instructions:** jog with direction changes on a colour call and add arm circles and side steps; then in fours pass and move through four cones, changing the condition each minute — first pass and move, then add a three-step finish, then add an immediate role change on a signal.  
**Teaching points:** this is rehearsal, not yet assessment; ask now if a cue is unclear.  
**Transitions:** move to game courts by colour.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk and use a soft ball; advanced — add a shadow defender.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: refresh the five pathway skills.  
**Procedure:** five brief stations — dribble and stop, support after passing, overarm throw to target, defensive stance and steering, and transition on a signal. Each student picks one personal focus to sharpen before the assessed games. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, in four groups.  
**Organisation:** five marked stations with one rotation on the teacher's signal; nothing is recorded at this stage, so students can still make mistakes freely.  
**Teaching points:** choose the station that is genuinely weakest rather than the most comfortable; one correction per student, not a list.  
**Common errors:** treating the stations as a competition; practising what is already secure; rushing the rotation and arriving without having worked.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: apply advantage and support in 4v3.  
**Procedure:** four attackers against three defenders in 75-second rounds, observed against the five criteria. An observer records what is seen — behaviour only, never a personal judgement — and roles change every minute so everyone is observed both attacking and defending.  
**Organisation:** groups of seven or eight with two rotating observers holding a simple criteria card; the teacher moves between courts, observing one student for 45–60 seconds at a time.  
**Teaching points:** use the extra player rather than forcing the ball; defenders protect the centre and talk; play at a pace at which the choice is visible.  
**Common errors:** the strongest student taking every attack; observers writing a verdict instead of a behaviour; defenders reverting to contact once the score matters; quiet play with no communication.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** provide the fairest evidence available — even-sided continuous play in which nothing is cued and every student decides independently.  
**Rules:** 4v4, no contact, no goalkeeper. One point for a goal, one bonus point when every team member touched the ball during the attack, and one bonus point for restoring defensive shape within three seconds of losing possession. Three seconds maximum while holding.  
**Teaching points:** the assessment is separate from the score, so a losing team can still show the better play; include the quieter players deliberately.  
**Common errors:** chasing goals and abandoning the criteria; excluding a weaker team-mate late in the game; contact returning under pressure.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “What I write down about you today is a decision you made, not a goal you scored — so tell me the best decision you made, and the one thing you want to fix.”  
**Learning summary:** each student records one strength and one target, supported by something they or their observer actually saw.  
**Feedback:** the teacher gives brief individual feedback and links the evidence to the next unit; no public ranking of any kind.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection and a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Judging success only by goals:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “five criteria.”
- **Late transition:** seen when learners stand after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** five criteria — transition to defence within three seconds of losing possession, an appropriate decision in 3 of 4 situations, combining at least 3 of the 5 pathway skills in live play, contact-free defence at arm's-length distance, and active, effective participation across every role, evidenced by what was actually observed rather than the score. **Success:** at least 4 of the 5 criteria achieved, each judged by its own measure. **Partial:** 2–3 criteria achieved. **Not yet:** 0–1 criterion, or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-09 — width, depth, and line support in 4v4. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** it is the only lesson that shows which skills actually transfer from closed practice into live play, and it does so against criteria students were told in advance rather than a private impression.  
**Develops:** independent decision-making without cues, integration of all five pathway skills under fatigue, self-assessment against public standards, and the responsibility to include every team-mate when a result is at stake.  
**Why not skip:** a pathway that ends at the last drill leaves both teacher and student guessing; grading on goals alone rewards whoever already plays at a club, and the students who progressed most are precisely the ones who would go unrecognised.
