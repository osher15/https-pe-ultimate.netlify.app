# 1. Lesson identity
**Name:** Lay-Up from an Open Lane
**Code:** BB-07-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Basketball; step rhythm, balanced take-off, close finish
**Age:** 11–13
**Experience:** beginner–developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Students:** 18–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 6–10 balls, 20 cones, floor spots, 2–4 baskets
**Space:** gym or basketball court
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Develop a safe, timed gather–two-step–finish sequence that converts recognition of an open attacking lane into a controlled close shot.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Complete gather–two steps–take-off in correct order in 6/8 attempts.
- Take off from the foot opposite the finishing hand in 5/7.
- Direct a controlled finish toward board or rim without excessive force.
- Enter only a clear lane and leave through the marked side.
- Explain that a lay-up follows recognition of a safe open lane.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Controlled dribble, moving reception, and open-lane decision from Basketball 06. Students struggling with the gather begin without dribble or basket.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Basketball 06—receive and choose pass or dribble.
- **Current:** It adds the finish Basketball 06 stopped short of: a recognised open lane is now converted into a gather, two timed steps, and a soft close-range release. It withholds all defensive pressure — every entry is unopposed and signalled in advance — so rhythm is learned before contest, which Basketball 08 introduces from the defender's side.
- **Next:** Basketball 08—defensive stance, lateral movement, and closing a lane without contact.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** rhythm, hand–foot coordination, jump and landing.
- **Social:** safe waiting, feeding the station, peer feedback.
- **Cognitive:** sequence recall and side recognition.
- **Tactical:** use of an open lane and finishing-hand choice.
- **Emotional:** confidence near the basket and persistence after misses.
# 7. Equipment and space
One ball per 2–3 students; a gather mark and two step spots per lane; entry and exit cones. **Setup:** 8 minutes before; **pack-down:** 5 after. Prepare two lanes per basket but activate one at a time; 4–6 students per station. With one basket, half the class rehearses rhythm toward a high wall target without release.
# 8. Safety
- One ball in flight per basket.
- Enter only after the previous shooter clears the landing area.
- No active defender or contact.
- Land softly under control and leave to the side.
- Rebound only after board/rim contact and the shooter's exit.
- Do not cross right and left lanes.
- Retrieve loose balls after stopping and scanning.
- Stop for slippery flooring or pain.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Time:** 3 minutes  
**Teacher says:** “A lay-up is controlled rhythm, not a fast charge at the basket.”  
**Purpose:** introduce rhythm, side, and safety.
## Warm-up
**Time:** 7 minutes  
**Purpose:** warm joints and rehearse rhythm without a ball.  
**Structure:** four lanes with two step spots and a landing line.  
**Instructions:** walk, use the two spots, lift the knee, make a small rise, land, and exit sideways.  
**Transitions:** 3 minutes no arm action; 2 with finishing hand; 2 at light-running rhythm.  
**Variations:** no jump; closer spots; “one–two–up” sound cue.
## Main activity A
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** connect ball gather to two steps.  
**Process:** receive a soft pass or carry the ball, approach, gather, step on two spots, and place the ball against a high target without release.  
**Organisation:** pairs in lanes; six repetitions each side.  
**Teaching points:** gather first, flowing steps, knee lift, eyes on target.  
**Errors:** extra step, reversed order, ball away from body, pause before lift.
## Main activity B
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** add a soft release using the board.  
**Process:** unopposed entry from 3–4 metres; gather, two steps, rise, soft release, exit sideways; the next player rebounds and feeds.  
**Organisation:** maximum six in line; right side 5 minutes, left side 5.  
**Teaching points:** opposite take-off foot, hand near basket, target point, quality over makes.  
**Errors:** too much force, eyes down, landing in lane, next player enters early.
## Applied game
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Purpose:** link choice to entry.  
**Rules:** trios—feeder, performer, signal giver. Green—lay-up entry; red—stop and return pass. Signal before reception. No defender or speed race.  
**Scoring:** one for correct decision, one for correct step sequence; a made basket is not required.  
**Variations:** no dribble; one bounce; two bounces and varied angle.
## Closure
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Message:** “A lay-up is won by rhythm, not by speed — gather, two steps, lift, and land where you can walk away safely.”  
**Review:** demonstrate from both sides.  
**Feedback:** partner names one success and one correction.  
**Exit:** assigned collection after ball count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Three steps after gather:** extra foot contact; slow down and restore two floor spots.
- **Same foot and hand:** the body closes; rehearse “opposite foot–knee up” without ball.
- **Ball away from body:** unprotected gather; bring it to two hands before the first step.
- **Hard release:** the ball strikes the board sharply; move closer and request soft touch.
- **Landing in lane:** blocks the next student; mark landing and side exit.
- **Eyes on feet:** the target disappears; use a coloured board marker.
# 11. Teaching points
- Gather before two steps.
- Opposite foot to hand.
- Knee up.
- Eyes on target.
- Soft release.
- Controlled landing.
- Exit sideways.
- Sequence before scoring.
- **Rules note:** current FIBA rules allow a "zero step" (gather step) before the two steps. In school PE, teach the simple rule — gather, then two steps — and mention the zero step only to advanced players.
# 12. Adaptations
- **Foundation:** no basket/dribble, large step spots.
- **Intermediate:** one bounce and board finish.
- **Advanced:** two bounces, varied angle, non-dominant hand.
- **Large group:** parallel no-basket rhythm stations.
- **Small group:** more repetitions and approved school video feedback.
- **Limited space:** two-metre approach, no run.
- **Limited equipment:** one ball per trio and wall target.
- **Different needs:** no jump, lower/large target, short lane, visual/sound cue, individual plan.
# 13. Assessment
Check gather, step order, take-off foot, control, safe finish. **Success:** 4/5 in 6/8. **Partial:** three criteria or success without release only. **Not yet:** repeated extra step, loss of balance, or unsafe entry. Return to floor spots without basket.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were spots suitable, queues short, and no-basket work needed? Students: What cue held rhythm? Which foot lifted you? Improve: side, distance, and target height.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Basketball 08—no-contact lane defence. **If not:** repeat Main A; return to Basketball 06 if decision is the barrier.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** the lay-up is one of the highest-percentage shots available to a school player, and it is the first time a student joins travelling, deciding, and scoring into one continuous action.
- **Develops:** hand–foot coordination in a fixed rhythm, the gather that prevents travelling, single-leg take-off with controlled landing, and the spatial discipline of exiting to the side.
- **Why not skip:** without a taught step sequence students invent their own, producing travelling violations, unbalanced landings, and congestion under the basket — all far harder to correct once the pattern is automatic.
