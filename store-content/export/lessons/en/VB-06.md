# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 06 — Movement and Coverage after Contact
**Lesson code:** VB-06-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** base positions and coverage
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Move from base to the ball and cover open court after contact.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Returns to the assigned base position within two seconds of every contact, in 4 of 5 rallies.
- Moves to the ball with the feet and arrives stationary before playing it, in 3 of 4 receptions.
- Covers the open area left by a team-mate who has moved to play the ball, in 3 of 5 situations.
- Keeps the trio in a triangle rather than a straight line, holding two arm lengths from the nearest team-mate.
- Takes receiving, covering, and observing roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's recovery to base.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-05 — three-contact sequence.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-05 — three-contact sequence.  
**Current lesson:** It adds what happens between the contacts of VB-05: students learn a base position, the movement out of it to play a ball, and — crucially — the recovery back into it, plus covering the gap a moving team-mate leaves behind. It withholds attacking the ball downward and defending such an attack, which VB-07 and VB-08 add.  
**Next lesson:** VB-07 — controlled standing attack.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “The rally is not decided by the contact — it is decided by where you were standing before it. Leave your base to play the ball, then get straight back to it.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — base, call, move, recover to cover — and show the class what an uncovered court looks like.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing a court with three base positions marked on the floor.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the legs for repeated short sprints and recoveries, the main physical demand of this lesson.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then shadow coverage.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add side steps, forward and backward shuffles, and low half-squats; then stand on a marked base spot, sprint to a pointed cone, touch it, and recover to base, repeating for 45 seconds.  
**Teaching points:** face the net throughout — never turn your back to the ball; the recovery should be as fast as the run out.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk over a short distance; advanced — two cones pointed in sequence.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** make the move-and-recover pattern automatic before a ball is involved.  
**Procedure:** a partner points to one of four zones; the student moves there, plays or shadows a contact, and recovers to base before the next point is given. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs per court, one pointing and one moving, alternating roles; base positions clearly marked on the floor.  
**Teaching points:** push off hard, arrive balanced, and be still before contact; recover by the shortest route while facing the net; stay low between repetitions.  
**Common errors:** standing and watching after the contact; turning the back to the net while recovering; drifting to a new base instead of the marked one; reaching sideways rather than moving the feet.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** learn that coverage is a team responsibility, not an individual one.  
**Procedure:** in trios, one student receives while a second immediately covers the short space in front and the third holds the deep space behind. A feeder varies short and deep balls so the trio constantly readjusts. Roles rotate every minute.  
**Organisation:** one trio per court, feeder at the net, the trio starting in a marked triangle.  
**Teaching points:** when one player moves forward, somebody must fill behind; keep the triangle shape rather than forming a line; call both what you are taking and what you are leaving.  
**Common errors:** all three standing in a line across the court; nobody covering the short ball; two players covering the same space; coverage arriving only after the ball has landed.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold court shape together in live play.  
**Rules:** 3v3 over a low net; one point for winning the rally, plus a bonus point whenever the defending trio was in a clear triangle at the moment the ball crossed the net, called by an observer or the teacher. No spiking.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** look at your team-mates between rallies, not only at the ball; the bonus rewards shape, so it can be earned even in a lost rally.  
**Common errors:** the trio flattening into a line as the rally speeds up; everyone converging on the ball; no recovery after the first contact.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The empty part of the court is the part nobody went back to — every time you leave your base, somebody has to fill it.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their recovery or coverage, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- Three players standing in one line: loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per trio and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** recovery to base, movement to the ball, coverage of open space, triangle shape, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-07 — controlled standing attack. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Volleyball is played mostly without touching the ball, and the difference between a team that covers the court and one that does not is positional habit rather than skill or speed.  
**What develops?** A defined base position and the discipline of recovering to it, movement that arrives early and balanced, spatial awareness of the gap a moving team-mate leaves, and the communication that assigns space rather than just claiming a ball.  
**Why not skip?** Without taught coverage, three students stand in a line watching each other, easy balls land in gaps nobody owned, and the class concludes it is bad at volleyball when in fact it was simply standing in the wrong places.
