# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 06 — Standing Long Jump and Landing
**Lesson code:** AT-06-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** take-off and landing
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Push from two feet and land on both feet with control in a safe zone.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Swings both arms back and then forward in time with the push, so the arms lead the take-off, in 4 of 5 jumps.
- Takes off from two feet simultaneously and lands on two feet, absorbing through bent knees, in 4 of 5 jumps.
- Holds the landing still for two full seconds without stepping, hopping, or putting a hand down, in 3 of 4 jumps.
- Jumps into the marked safe zone and waits for the signal before leaving it or before the next student jumps.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's arm swing or landing and states a personal goal for the next jump.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-05 — personal endurance pacing.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-05 — personal endurance pacing.  
**Current lesson:** It opens the jumping half of the pathway, moving from running to a two-footed take-off and, above all, a controlled two-footed landing. It withholds the approach run entirely — every jump is made from standing, so the take-off can be learned without speed — which AT-07 adds.  
**Next lesson:** AT-07 — approach run and jump to zone.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Your arms jump before your legs do. Swing them back, throw them forward as you push, and then land like you are landing on ice — quiet, bent, and still.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — arms back, push, knees forward, soft landing — plus the rule that only one student is in a landing zone at a time.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing the landing zone.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare ankles, knees, and hips for repeated take-offs and landings, which is essential before any jumping.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then landing rehearsal.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add ankle bounces, half-squats, and leg swings; then practise landing alone — step off a low line, land on two feet, bend the knees, and freeze for two seconds, repeated ten times.  
**Teaching points:** the landing is rehearsed before the jump, never after; a quiet landing is a safe landing.  
**Transitions:** move to jumping lanes on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — landing practice only, with no jump for distance; advanced — land and hold with the eyes closed.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** connect the arm swing to the leg drive, which is what actually produces distance.  
**Procedure:** students jump from standing towards their own personal marker, placed at their previous best rather than at any class standard. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four lanes with mats or a soft landing area, one jumper per lane, a partner marking the landing point and resetting the personal marker.  
**Teaching points:** swing both arms back as the knees bend, then drive them forward and up as the feet push; push through the whole foot rather than the toes alone; land on two feet with the knees bending to absorb; each student competes only against their own marker.  
**Common errors:** arms hanging still so only the legs work; taking off from one foot; landing stiff-legged; sitting back onto the hands on landing.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** make the landing itself the task, so control is valued above distance.  
**Procedure:** coloured zones are marked at increasing distances; a partner calls a colour and the student jumps to land in that zone and freeze for two full seconds. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** groups of three — jumper, colour caller, landing judge — with soft landing surfaces and a clear waiting line.  
**Teaching points:** jumping the correct distance matters more than jumping the maximum; adjust the effort, not the technique, for a nearer zone; the freeze proves the landing was controlled rather than merely survived.  
**Common errors:** jumping maximally regardless of the colour called; stepping or hopping after landing; putting a hand down to save an over-reached jump; the next jumper starting before the zone is clear.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** finish with a challenge in which a stable landing outscores a long one.  
**Rules:** Each student completes a set of jumps scoring one point for distance beyond their own personal marker and two points for a landing held still for two seconds, so a jump that travels far but is not held scores less than a shorter controlled one. No distances are compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** the scoring deliberately rewards control, because control is what makes distance safe to pursue later; reset the personal marker only after two clean jumps beyond it.  
**Common errors:** over-reaching for distance and losing the landing; skipping the two-second hold; comparing markers with neighbours; fatigue degrading the landings late in the set.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Anybody can jump far once. Landing it every time, on two feet and still, is what lets you keep jumping far for the rest of your life without getting hurt.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their arm swing or landing, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect mats and cones by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Landing on one foot:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-07 — approach run and jump to zone. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The two-footed take-off and landing underpins every jumping and rebounding action in sport, and a stiff, uncontrolled landing is a recognised knee-injury risk when it is never taught.  
**What develops?** Coordinated arm and leg drive, a symmetrical two-footed take-off, eccentric strength through the landing, and the body awareness to hold a position still rather than simply arrive in it.  
**Why not skip?** Students jump constantly in every other sport, and if a controlled landing has never been taught they absorb it with straight legs; teaching it here, standing and unhurried, is far easier than correcting it later at speed.
