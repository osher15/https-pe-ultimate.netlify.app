# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 09 — Running, Jumping, and Throwing Circuit
**Lesson code:** AT-09-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** multi-skill circuit
**Age:** 12–15
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Complete running, jumping, and throwing stations with regulated effort and independent transitions.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Performs the key cue of each station — tall running posture, two-footed landing, side-on throwing — correctly at every station, in 4 of 5 rounds.
- Regulates effort across the whole circuit, so the final station is performed as well as the first.
- Moves between stations only on the transition signal, leaving equipment safe and ready for the next group.
- Records their own result honestly at each station and compares it only with their own previous score.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's technique at any station and states a personal goal for the next round.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-08 — soft-ball distance throw.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-08 — soft-ball distance throw.  
**Current lesson:** It brings the three disciplines together for the first time, asking students to move independently between running, jumping, and throwing stations while holding technique as fatigue accumulates. It withholds formal assessment — the teacher still coaches at each station and nothing is recorded for a grade — which AT-10 replaces with observation against criteria.  
**Next lesson:** AT-10 — final multi-event assessment.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today you meet all three events in one lesson, and the hard part is not any single station — it is still doing the last one properly when you are tired. Quality before speed, every time.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — quality before speed, scan, perform, record, move — plus the transition signal and the rule that nobody moves early.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle at the centre of the station layout.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the body for three different demands in one session, since the circuit asks for sprinting, jumping, and throwing within minutes of each other.  
**Structure:** individual movement covering all three patterns.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add arm circles and trunk rotations for throwing, ankle bounces and half-squats for jumping, and two build-up runs for sprinting; then walk the circuit route once to see every station and its cue card.  
**Teaching points:** each station's cue card carries one cue only — read it before you start.  
**Transitions:** walk the route in the direction of travel.  
**Variations:** foundation — shorter walk-through with a partner; advanced — rehearse one repetition at each station.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** refresh the single key cue of each discipline before they are combined under fatigue.  
**Procedure:** short technique stations, each with a cue card carrying one cue — tall posture and arm action for running, arms back and soft landing for jumping, side-on with weight transfer for throwing. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, moving on the signal.  
**Organisation:** stations spaced so throwing is well clear of running and jumping, each with a cue card and equipment already laid out.  
**Teaching points:** read the card before starting, not while performing; one cue per station is enough; this round is for quality only, with nothing recorded.  
**Common errors:** starting before reading the cue; treating the technique round as a race; crowding a popular station; carrying throwing equipment through a running lane.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold technique together as fatigue builds and transitions become self-managed.  
**Procedure:** ninety-second rounds at each station with transitions on the signal; students record their own result and note whether the cue was held. Roles change so everybody performs, records, and observes.  
**Organisation:** groups of four to six rotating in a fixed direction, a recording card per student, and the teacher positioned to see the throwing station at all times.  
**Teaching points:** pace the circuit as a whole rather than emptying yourself at the first station; the recording is for you, so it is only useful if it is honest; leave the station ready for the next group.  
**Common errors:** going maximally at station one and fading; moving before the signal; inflating a recorded score; abandoning the cue once tired.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** finish with a team task in which technique under fatigue is the only currency.  
**Rules:** Team challenge in which each member earns their team a point at every station where the key cue was visibly held, judged by a rotating observer. Results at the stations do not score at all — only technique and safe transitions.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a team scores best by slowing down slightly and keeping form, which is precisely the lesson; observers name the cue they did or did not see.  
**Common errors:** rushing to complete more repetitions; observers being generous to team-mates; technique collapsing at the final station; transitions turning into races.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Athletics is not three separate sports — it is one set of habits: push the ground, stay tall, land softly. Today you used all three inside ten minutes.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about holding a cue under fatigue, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, dismantle stations in the assigned order, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Moving before the transition signal:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-10 — final multi-event assessment. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is where the separate events of the unit become a single set of transferable movement habits, and where students discover which discipline suits them — often not the one they expected.  
**What develops?** Effort regulation across mixed demands, independent transitions without teacher direction, honest self-recording, and the ability to hold technique when tired, which is when it matters most.  
**Why not skip?** Skills practised only in isolation tend to disappear the moment they are combined; the circuit also gives every student a route into athletics through the event they are best at, rather than judging the whole unit on sprinting alone.
