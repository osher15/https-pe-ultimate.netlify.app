# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 01 — Standing Start and Short Acceleration
**Lesson code:** AT-01-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** standing-start position, first steps, and controlled deceleration
**Age:** 10–13
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 30 cones, 8–12 batons or beanbags, and lane markers
**Space:** court, field, or straight track with 15–20 metre lanes and run-off area
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Teach a safe standing start, progressive acceleration, and controlled finish as the foundation for school sprinting.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Sets a stable split stance with a consistent lead foot before the signal.
- Starts on the signal and takes three short, quick first steps in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Holds a forward lean as one line through the first ten metres.
- Runs through the finish line and slows gradually inside the run-off zone, with no sudden stop.
- Stays in their own lane and gives a partner one factual comment.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No athletics lesson required; students need safe lane and stop-signal routines.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** None — this is the opening lesson of the athletics sequence.  
**Current lesson:** As the opening athletics lesson it establishes the three things every sprint depends on: a stable split stance, short quick first steps under a forward lean, and a deceleration that happens gradually inside a run-off zone. It withholds refined running technique at full speed and the systematic teaching of reaction to changing signals — the core task uses one predictable command, and the late-signal option is only an optional extension for advanced learners — which AT-02 and AT-03 develop properly.  
**Next lesson:** AT-02 — running posture, arm action, and acceleration.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- One runner per lane, clear run-off beyond the finish, walking return outside lanes, and no sudden stopping at the line.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use controlled effort and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn standing-start position, first steps, and controlled deceleration. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Progress from walking to marching, ankling, arm action, and three relaxed build-ups over ten metres, walking back outside lanes.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** From a standing split stance, lean slightly, push from the rear foot, and take three quick controlled steps. Practise on command without racing.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** One foot forward; lean as one line; push the ground back; quick first steps; run through then slow gradually.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** Run 10–15 metres at progressive speed, pass a finish cone, then decelerate through a marked zone. Partner observes lean, arm action, and lane discipline.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** hold the forward lean through the first steps, drive the arms forward and back, run through the finish cone, and slow down gradually inside the run-off zone.  
**Common errors:** standing up after the first step, braking before the finish cone, drifting out of lane, and starting before the signal.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Acceleration Match: cones stand every 2 metres from 6 to 16 metres; before each run the student names the cone they expect to pass when the whistle sounds three seconds after the start, runs at controlled effort, and a partner confirms the cone actually passed. Points are earned for a prediction within one cone, lane safety, and smooth deceleration—not fastest time. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—shorter distance and an auditory signal only; intermediate—full distance with a marked run-off zone; advanced—a late visual signal.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Push the ground back for three quick steps to start, and when you finish, keep running through the line and slow down gradually inside the zone — never stop on the line.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- Standing up immediately at the start: there is no acceleration; correct by holding the body lean as one line through the first three steps.
- **First step too long:** the student loses speed instead of building it; correct with short, frequent first steps.
- **Starting before the signal:** an early and unsafe start; correct with a full stop in position and a wait for the signal.
- Stopping sharply at the finish line: knee loading and collision risk; correct by running through the line and decelerating along a marked zone.
- **Arms crossing the midline:** the body twists and energy is lost; correct with a forward–back arm action beside the body.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** a shorter lane, a walking start, and an auditory signal only.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance with a marked run-off zone.  
**Advanced:** a late visual signal or a changing cue, without increasing speed.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt start position, lane length, distance, pace, signal type, role, and communication; allow a walking start or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** stable split stance, three quick first steps, forward lean held as one line, run-through finish with gradual deceleration, and staying in lane.  
**Success:** all five measures are shown consistently and the student explains one cue.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for preparation or decision.  
**Not yet successful:** spacing or force remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before AT-02 — running posture, arm action, and acceleration.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to AT-02 — running posture, arm action, and acceleration.  
**If not:** return to a walking start on a short lane, with one success cue and an auditory signal only.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Sprinting underlies nearly every sport a student will meet, and the start and the finish are the two moments where technique and safety are decided rather than inherited.  
**What develops?** A stable, repeatable start position, the short quick steps that actually build speed, a forward lean held as one line, and above all the habit of decelerating gradually instead of stopping dead.  
**Why not skip?** Untaught, students stand straight up at the signal and lose the acceleration entirely, then stop sharply on the finish line — which loads the knees and raises the risk of a collision with the next runner.
