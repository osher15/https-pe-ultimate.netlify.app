# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 02 — Dribbling, Stop, and Change of Direction
**Lesson code:** HB-02-EN
**Sport:** handball
**Subtopic:** ball control while moving and changing lanes
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** beginner–developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; groupings and adaptations are based on ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 handballs or soft balls, 28–36 cones, 4 sets of bibs
**Space:** gym or court at least half a handball court
**Complexity:** basic
# 2. Overall purpose
The student controls the ball while moving, stopping, and changing direction so they can safely progress to combining dribbling with passing and team movement.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- The student dribbles with one hand for 8–10 metres, keeping the ball at or below waist height in 4 of 5 attempts.
- The student stops under control within two steps of a signal without collision or loss in 3 of 4 attempts.
- The student changes direction around a cone using a lowered centre of mass and ball protection in 3 of 5 attempts.
- The student looks up at least twice on each route and responds to open space.
- The student keeps two arm lengths of space and completes both roles in each pair task.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Basic grip, short passing and catching, and moving after a pass from HB-01. Continuous dribbling experience is not required; the teacher models it before practice.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-01 — basic passing, catching, and paired movement.  
**Current lesson:** adds individual ball control to the paired passing of HB-01: the student now travels with the ball, brakes within two steps of a signal, and changes lane while protecting it. It withholds combining that dribble with a pass to a moving team-mate — every route here ends in a controlled stop rather than a release — which is what HB-03 adds.  
**Next lesson:** HB-03 — off-ball movement and creating a passing lane after a dribble.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** hand–eye coordination, force control, braking, direction change.  
**Social:** turn-taking, giving space, brief supportive feedback.  
**Cognitive:** processing a signal, scanning, and choosing a route.  
**Tactical:** protecting the ball, escaping pressure, recognising open space.  
**Emotional:** confidence with the ball, persistence, and recovery after a loss.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel lanes 3 metres wide, a change-of-direction zone, and four squares for the final game. Provide one ball per student or pair; unused balls stay in a basket or marked area.  
**Setup time:** 5 minutes.  
**Pack-down time:** 3 minutes.  
**Recommended organisation:** 5–8 students per lane; staggered starts; one-way travel with return outside the lane.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, ball pressure, and distance from walls.
- Keep two arm lengths between students and start only when the lane is clear.
- No stealing, pushing, body blocking, or striking another student’s ball.
- Keep the dribble at or below the waist and slow down before turning.
- Retrieve a loose ball only after scanning and through the return lane.
- On a stop signal, set both feet, hold the ball close, and face forward.
- Stop immediately for pain, dizziness, or a collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today speed is not the target. Success is arriving, stopping, and changing direction while the ball stays controlled and the space stays safe.”  
**Purpose:** introduce three success cues: low ball, lifted eyes, balanced stop.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Goal:** warm the joints and develop signal response while learning the ball’s bounce.  
**Structure:** free movement in personal space, first without a ball and then with one per student or pair.  
**Instructions:** walk and jog, circle shoulders, change body height; stationary bounce with the preferred hand; switch if appropriate; on signal, stop and secure the ball.  
**Transitions:** on a colour signal, groups enter pre-marked lanes.  
**Variations:** roll and collect instead of dribbling; non-dominant hand for advanced learners.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Goal:** control dribble pace and stopping.  
**Procedure:** dribble straight for 8–10 metres; on a visual or sound signal, slow down, stop within two steps, and secure the ball. Return outside the lane.  
**Organisation:** four lanes, 5–8 students per lane, starts 3–4 seconds apart.  
**Teaching cues:** soft fingers above the ball, push rather than slap, bent knees, eyes up, body between the ball and outside space.  
**Common errors:** high bounce, fixed downward gaze, upright stop, and losing the ball forward.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Goal:** change direction while keeping the ball on the protected side.  
**Procedure:** dribble to a cone, slow, plant the outside foot, lean, and leave through a new lane; later, the teacher shows a colour for left or right.  
**Organisation:** two cones per lane; practise both sides; waiting students remain behind the line.  
**Teaching cues:** arrive under control, lower the centre of mass, place the shoulder between ball and “defender”, and push the ball into the new lane.  
**Common errors:** overly wide turn, late hand change, and accelerating before scanning.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Goal:** choose an open route under moderate time pressure without contact.  
**Rules:** “Changing Gates” — in each square, 3v3 with two cone gates. A ball carrier dribbles through an open gate and stops under control; an opponent may close space but cannot steal. After success, pass the ball to the next player. Rotate every 60–75 seconds.  
**Scoring:** 1 point for a controlled crossing and stop; 1 bonus point if the student looked up and changed target before the gate. No individual ranking.  
**Variations:** neutral player points to a gate; wider square; non-dominant hand only for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “When the ball is low and quiet, your eyes come up — and only then can you see the gate, choose it, and stop exactly where you meant to.”  
**Learning summary:** each student shows the correct bounce height and names when they looked up.  
**Feedback:** a partner gives one control example and one next-step suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** each group collects only its colour of equipment after the signal.
# 10. Common errors
- Ball rises above the waist. Seen in long recovery time and repeated loss. Correct with a shorter, softer push close to the body.
- Slapping with a rigid palm. Heard as a loud bounce and seen in poor accuracy. Correct with spread fingers and a relaxed elbow.
- Eyes remain down. Seen in missed signals or crowding. Correct by asking the learner to identify fingers shown by the teacher.
- Stop is upright and narrow. Seen in an extra step or forward stumble. Correct with a wide base, bent knees, and ball close.
- Body turns around the ball. Seen in an exposed ball and wide path. Correct by placing the body between ball and marker and leaving on a short angle.
- Acceleration into congestion. Seen when entering an occupied lane. Correct with the rule “scan—choose—go.”
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate from the side and front, first at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach control before speed and increase pace only after success.
- Pair visual and sound signals at first.
- Practise turns to both sides without immediately demanding equal weak-hand use.
- **Give brief feedback:** one observed action and one correction.
- Protect movement time by explaining between rounds.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, use a softer or larger ball, widen the gate, and use a predictable stop.  
**Intermediate:** light jog, directional signal, and changing pace.  
**Advanced:** non-dominant hand, narrow gate, and late target change.  
**Large group:** six short lanes with wave starts.  
**Small group:** two lanes and 2v2 play.  
**Limited space:** stationary dribble, side step and stop; walking game.  
**Partial equipment:** one ball per pair; one performs while the other signals and monitors spacing.  
**Students with varied needs:** light or audible ball, high-contrast floor marks, visual cue, extra time, consistent partner, and adapted movement range.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** ball height and control, lifted gaze, balanced stop, direction change, and safe spacing.  
**Success:** 4–5 criteria appear in most attempts and in the game.  
**Partial success:** 2–3 criteria appear at low pace or after a prompt.  
**Not yet successful:** 0–1 criterion, repeated loss, or unsafe behaviour; return to walking lanes and an adapted ball.
# 14. Reflection
**For the teacher:** Who controls without a fixed downward gaze? Did spacing and starts prevent waiting and collision? Which adaptation improved success?  
**For students:** What helped you stop? When did you change route? What will you try next time?  
**Improve next lesson:** decide whether gaze, stop, or turn needs a short review before adding the pass.
# 15. Continuity
**If objectives are met:** move to HB-03 — off-ball movement and creating a passing lane after dribbling.  
**If objectives are not met:** return to stationary and walking dribbles, a predictable stop signal, and a wide turn around a marker.

# 18. Teacher quick read
- Name control, gaze, and stop as the three success measures.
- Warm up through signal response and individual bouncing.
- Practise straight dribbling and stopping in four lanes.
- Add direction change and open-gate choice without contact.
- Close with brief feedback and preparation for movement and passing.
# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why this lesson matters:** it creates a safe base for travelling with the ball without disconnecting vision from play.  
**What it develops:** force control, braking, scanning, and space selection.  
**Why not skip it:** without control and stopping, passing, decisions, and team movement cannot be combined safely.
