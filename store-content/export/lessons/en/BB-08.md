# 1. Lesson identity
**Name:** No-Contact Defence and Lane Control
**Code:** BB-08-EN
**Sport/subtopic:** Basketball; stance, lateral movement, spacing
**Age:** 11–14
**Experience:** beginner–developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Students:** 20–30
**Group:** Girls, boys, or mixed; adapt by ability
**Equipment:** 16–24 cones, 8–12 balls, bibs
**Space:** gym or court
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Teach students to slow an attacker and protect a route through position and safe lateral movement, without contact or hazardous reaching.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Hold a balanced defensive stance for six seconds in 5/6 attempts.
- Complete 4–6 lateral steps without crossing feet in 6/8.
- Maintain about one-and-a-half arm lengths from a shadow attacker.
- Keep the body between attacker and end gate in 5/7 situations.
- Stop immediately on signal without contact.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Balanced stopping, changing direction, and boundaries. Dribbling is required only for the attacker in the later task.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
- **Previous:** Basketball 07—use an open lane for a lay-up.
- **Current:** It turns students to the defending side for the first time: having attacked open lanes in Basketball 07, they now learn the stance, lateral steps, and safe distance that close a lane by position alone. It withholds any second defender and all help defence — every task is one defender against one attacker in a marked corridor — which the 2v1 of Basketball 09 introduces.
- **Next:** Basketball 09—2v1 numerical advantage and pass-or-drive choice.
# 6. Lesson contribution
- **Motor:** low base and lateral steps.
- **Social:** self-control and respect for space.
- **Cognitive:** read hips and direction.
- **Tactical:** body between attacker and target.
- **Emotional:** defend confidently without aggression.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark 5–6 corridors of 4×8 metres with end gates; add one ball per group in Main B. **Setup:** 6 minutes; **pack-down:** 4. Groups of 4–5; outside return route.
# 8. Safety
- No contact, pushing, screening, or taking from hands.
- The defender watches the body and does not extend a foot into the path.
- The attacker works at no more than 60% in pairs.
- Only one active pair per corridor.
- Stop on signal or boundary exit.
- Retrieve loose ball after scanning.
- Do not move backward without looking.
- Check surface, laces, and cones.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
3 minutes  
**Teacher says:** “Defence is not hitting the ball or body; it is position that slows and closes a route.”  
**Purpose:** body–gate–distance rule.
## Warm-up
7 minutes  
**Purpose:** warm-up and lateral movement.  
**Structure:** mirror pairs in corridors.  
**Instructions:** the leader moves right/left with short steps; the mirror maintains distance.  
**Transitions:** 3 minutes walking; 2 in stance; 2 with stop cue.  
**Variations:** short route; visual or sound cue.
## Main activity A
10 minutes  
**Purpose:** stance and uncrossed movement.  
**Process:** the defender starts centrally and responds to a partner pointing at the right/left gate; moves, sets between cones, holds two seconds.  
**Organisation:** trios—defender, cue giver, observer; rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** wide feet, bent knees, short steps, chest facing attacker.  
**Errors:** crossed feet, hopping, tall stance, eyes on ball.
## Main activity B
10 minutes  
**Purpose:** stay between attacker and gate.  
**Process:** the attacker without ball moves at 50% toward the gate; the defender maintains distance and guides to the sideline. Add slow dribble without steals.  
**Organisation:** active pair and observer; switch every two attempts.  
**Teaching points:** slight angle, do not chase from behind, hands at chest height, stop at boundary.  
**Errors:** too close, turning fully sideways, contact, attacker too fast.
## Applied game
10 minutes  
**Purpose:** controlled 1v1.  
**Rules:** the attacker has five seconds and two bounces to reach the gate. The defender scores by staying between attacker and gate; no steal/contact.  
**Scoring:** attacker one for controlled crossing; defender one for closing the route; both gain a safety point if no contact.  
**Variations:** no ball; two bounces; three bounces and two gates.
## Closure
5 minutes  
**Message:** “Distance and position defend before the hands.”  
**Review:** demonstrate safe and unsafe examples.  
**Feedback:** each student names one cue.  
**Exit:** group collection and teacher release.
# 10. Common errors
- **Crossing feet:** feet meet; slow and use parallel floor marks.
- **Tall stance:** late response; hold a low base for four seconds.
- **Too close:** less than one arm's length; mark a distance ring.
- **Chasing behind:** the defender trails; start half a step nearer the gate.
- **Eyes on ball:** the attacker gets past; focus on hips/centre.
- **Reaching to steal:** contact occurs; visible chest-height hands, no steal.
# 11. Teaching points
- Wide base.
- Short steps.
- No crossing.
- Body between attacker and gate.
- One-and-a-half arm lengths.
- Eyes on centre.
- Contain before steal.
- Stop on cue.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** no ball at 40%. **Intermediate:** slow dribble. **Advanced:** two gates at 70%. **Large group:** six corridors plus observer. **Small group:** rotating 1v1. **Limited space:** 3×6. **Limited equipment:** no balls. **Different needs:** wide path, walking, visual line, fixed partner, or static positioning role.
# 13. Assessment
Stance, steps, distance, position, safety. **Success:** 4/5 in 6/8. **Partial:** three criteria, or success only without ball. **Not yet:** repeated contact, crossing, or lost route. Return to mirror and unopposed gate.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Were speeds and widths safe? Student: What did you watch and how did you keep distance? Improve: speed and width before 2v1.
# 15. Continuity
**If achieved:** Basketball 09—2v1. **If not:** repeat Main A; return to Basketball 02 for unstable stopping.

# 19. Pedagogical value
- **Why important:** it reframes defence as spatial responsibility rather than aggression — the single change that makes competitive small-sided play safe enough to run in a school class.
- **Develops:** a low balanced stance, lateral footwork without crossing, judgement of safe distance, and the self-control to contain an attacker instead of lunging for the ball.
- **Why not skip:** a class that reaches 2v1 and 3v3 without a taught no-contact defence defaults to grabbing and pushing; the contact then has to be policed as misbehaviour rather than corrected as technique.
