# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 05 — Receive, Set, and Send Sequence
**Lesson code:** VB-05-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** three-contact sequence
**Age:** 11–13
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Complete receive–set–send with clear roles and communication.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Successfully completes the full three-touch sequence — reception, set, and send — with correct technique at each touch, in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Calls for the ball before every contact and moves into position before it arrives, in 3 of 4 sequences.
- Sets the second contact high enough, and close enough to the net, for the third player to send it over, in 3 of 5 sequences.
- Stays in the assigned role for the whole sequence instead of chasing the first ball, keeping two arm lengths from team-mates.
- Takes receiver, setter, and sender roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's contact or position.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-04 — serve reception.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-04 — serve reception.  
**Current lesson:** It assembles three separate contacts into one sequence for the first time: the reception of VB-04, the set of VB-02, and a controlled send over the net, each performed by a different player. It withholds attacking and any defence of the third ball — the send is a placement, not a hit, and nobody plays it back — which VB-06 and VB-07 add.  
**Next lesson:** VB-06 — movement and court coverage.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Volleyball gives you three touches, and today we use all three. One receives, one sets, one sends — and the only way that works is if you call your ball out loud every single time.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — call, middle target, support, send — plus the rule that only the player who called may play the ball.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing one demonstration court.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare wrists, shoulders, and legs for all three contact types and rehearse calling before contact.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then triads.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add wrist circles, arm swings, and low shuffles; then in triads pass a ball around a triangle, every student calling their own name before touching it.  
**Teaching points:** the call comes before the contact, not with it; hold the ready position between touches.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — catch and throw with a call; advanced — alternate forearm and overhead contacts.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** build the full sequence under conditions where nothing is contested.  
**Procedure:** in triads a feeder tosses, the first player forearm-passes to the setter, the setter sets, and the third player sends the ball over a low rope with a controlled contact. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times, rotating roles each round.  
**Organisation:** one triad per court with a marked setter zone; balls collected only on the signal.  
**Teaching points:** the reception goes high and to the middle; the setter faces where the ball will go; the sender aims at open floor, never at a person; everyone calls.  
**Common errors:** two players going for the reception; a flat set that cannot be played; the third contact hit hard instead of placed; silent sequences that break down halfway.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** keep the three-contact structure when the ball arrives less predictably.  
**Procedure:** teams of three must use all three contacts before sending the ball over a low rope to a receiving trio, who do the same. Roles rotate every minute.  
**Organisation:** two trios per court either side of the rope; a dropped ball is simply restarted with a toss, with no scoring.  
**Teaching points:** return to your base position after each contact so you are ready for the next; the setter should be whoever did not receive; height on contacts one and two buys the team time.  
**Common errors:** sending the ball over on the first contact because it is easier; the same student taking two touches in a row; the trio collapsing into one spot; rushing so the sequence never reaches three.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold the three-contact habit together when a rally is contested.  
**Rules:** 3v3 over a low net; one point for winning the rally plus a bonus point whenever a team used all three contacts before returning the ball, whichever side won the point. No spiking; the ball may be caught once per side at foundation level.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** three touches are almost always better than one, even when one would work; call every ball.  
**Common errors:** returning the ball first touch under pressure; two students colliding on the first contact; abandoning roles once the score matters.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “One touch gives the ball straight back to them; three touches give it back on your terms. Call it, lift it, then place it.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their role or calling, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Everyone chasing the first ball:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Standing after contact:** court uncovered; move to support.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per triad and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** posture, contact, direction, position, communication. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-06 — movement and court coverage. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** This is the lesson where volleyball becomes volleyball: three contacts by three players is the structure the whole sport is built on, and it cannot emerge from practising contacts separately.  
**What develops?** Role clarity under time pressure, the calling that prevents two players taking the same ball, judging the height and placement each contact needs in order to make the next one possible, and patience to build rather than return.  
**Why not skip?** Classes that never rehearse the sequence return every ball on the first touch, which hands the advantage straight back; rallies stay one contact long, and every later tactical lesson has nothing to attach to.
