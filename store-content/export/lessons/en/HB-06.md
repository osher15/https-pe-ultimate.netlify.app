# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 06 — No-Contact Defence and Lane Control
**Lesson code:** HB-06-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** stance, lateral movement, steering
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student delays an attacker and closes a lane using stance and lateral movement without contact.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Moves with continuous side steps, feet never crossing, for at least six steps in a row.
- Holds a low defensive stance at one arm's length from the attacker, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Keeps the body positioned in the passing lane between the attacker and the goal, in 3 of 4 one-against-one situations.
- Delays an attacker for five seconds without any contact, steering them towards the sideline rather than the centre.
- Takes attacker, defender, and observer roles in turn and names one observable cue about a partner's stance or distance.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-05 — three-step finish.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-05 — three-step finish.  
**Current lesson:** turns the pathway around to the defending side: after five attacking lessons the student now learns the stance, the side step, and the safe distance that delay an attacker and steer them away from the centre, entirely without contact. It withholds any second defender and all help defence — every task is one against one in a marked corridor — which the 3v2 of HB-07 opens up.  
**Next lesson:** HB-07 — fast transition and 3v2 advantage.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 m clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Defending in handball is not grabbing, and it is not waiting. It is standing in the place the attacker wants to go, and getting there first.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — low, hands visible, body in the lane — plus the absolute no-contact rule and a demonstration of what an arm's-length distance looks like.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle, balls held still.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare ankles, knees, and hips for repeated lateral work, the most demanding movement in this lesson.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then mirror pairs.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add side steps, low half-squats, and ankle circles; then face a partner and mirror their side-to-side movement without a ball, holding a low stance throughout.  
**Teaching points:** short steps, feet never crossing, hands up where the attacker can see them.  
**Transitions:** move to work zones by colour.  
**Variations:** foundation — walking mirror over a short width; advanced — leader changes direction on a late cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: maintain stance against a moving attacker.  
**Procedure:** mirror in pairs without a ball. The attacker moves side to side and forward at a controlled pace; the defender holds an arm-length distance and stays square to them. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, in four groups, swapping roles each round.  
**Organisation:** pairs spread along a marked line with two metres between pairs; no ball at this stage, so attention stays on the feet.  
**Teaching points:** keep the centre of gravity low with bent knees; push off the outside foot and never cross the feet; keep hands visible at chest height and never reach in; watch the attacker's hips rather than the shoulders or the eyes.  
**Common errors:** standing tall, so the first change of direction beats them; crossing the feet and losing balance; drifting in to within touching distance; following every shoulder feint.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: steer an attacker away from danger.  
**Procedure:** one against one in a marked corridor. The attacker tries to reach the end line; the defender scores by delaying them for five seconds or steering them out to the sideline. No steal and no contact — the defender wins by position alone. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** one active pair per corridor, the rest waiting behind the line as observers, each watching one cue.  
**Teaching points:** take a position half a step towards the side you most want to protect, so the route you leave open is the harmless one; slow the attacker before trying to stop them; stay on the balls of the feet and move backwards under control.  
**Common errors:** reaching for the ball and making contact; turning fully sideways and being beaten back inside; retreating in a straight line so the attacker keeps the centre; giving up after being beaten instead of recovering.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold positional defending together when three attackers move the ball quickly.  
**Rules:** 3v3 in a marked area with cone goals. The defence scores a point for stopping a penetration without contact and the attack scores for a completed finish; any contact cancels the defensive point immediately. Three seconds maximum while holding the ball.  
**Teaching points:** talk — call out who is taking the ball carrier; the two defenders away from the ball protect the centre.  
**Common errors:** all three defenders following the ball; silent defence with two players marking the same attacker; contact creeping back in as the game speeds up.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The best defender here today was not the one who grabbed the ball — it was the one who stood in the right place so often that the attacker ran out of ideas.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their stance, distance, or steering, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection and a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Crossing feet or reaching:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “side steps–hands visible.”
- **Late transition:** seen when learners stand after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, gaze, decision, spacing, safety. **Success:** 4–5 criteria in most rounds. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompting. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-07 — fast transition and 3v2 advantage. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** handball tolerates contact at senior level, so a school class needs a positional model of defending taught explicitly — it is what keeps competitive play safe and fair rather than physical.  
**Develops:** a low balanced stance, lateral footwork without crossing, judgement of a distance that delays without inviting contact, and the communication that stops two defenders marking one attacker.  
**Why not skip:** without it students defend by grabbing shirts and blocking with the body, producing the injuries and arguments that lead some teachers to drop handball from the programme altogether.
