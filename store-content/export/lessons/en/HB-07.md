# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 07 — Fast Transition and 3v2 Advantage
**Lesson code:** HB-07-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** decision-making in numerical advantage
**Age:** 12–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 soft balls/handballs, 30 cones, bibs, and four cone goals
**Space:** gym or flat court at least half-court size
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
The student uses width, timely passing, and immediate role change to exploit a 3v2 advantage.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Occupies one of three separate lanes during a transition, so no two attackers run in the same lane, in 4 of 5 waves.
- Scans the two defenders before reaching the decision line rather than after arriving at the goal area.
- Releases the pass before the defender closes the lane, early enough for the receiver to catch it in stride, in 3 of 4 advantages.
- Changes from attack to defence within three seconds of a wave finishing, without waiting to watch the outcome of the shot.
- Takes attacking and defending roles in each rotation and names one observable cue about a partner's width or pass timing.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
HB-06 — no-contact defence.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-06 — no-contact defence.  
**Current lesson:** puts attack and defence together for the first time: the student holds width in a three-lane break, reads two defenders, releases the pass before the lane closes, and then switches role immediately for the next wave. It withholds equal numbers, since the attack always outnumbers the defence — which HB-08 removes in the final even-sided game.  
**Next lesson:** HB-08 — final game and pathway assessment.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, braking, direction change, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turn-taking. **Cognitive:** scanning and decisions. **Tactical:** spacing, support, transition. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four parallel work zones, secure unused balls, and keep 2 m clear from walls. **Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes. **Organisation:** groups of 5–8, staggered starts, rotations every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, footwear, balls, and goals.
- Keep two arm lengths between participants.
- No pushing, pulling, stealing from behind, or body blocking.
- Start only on the signal and with a clear lane.
- Retrieve loose balls only after scanning.
- Slow near walls, goals, and other students.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “An advantage only lasts a few seconds. Spread out so two defenders cannot cover three of you, and pass before the gap closes — not after.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — spread, scan, pass before closure — plus the wave rotation and the no-contact rule.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle at the halfway line.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** raise the pulse towards the running demand of repeated waves and rehearse passing while moving forward at pace.  
**Structure:** individual running, then threes in lanes.  
**Instructions:** jog the width of the court with changes of pace on a colour call; add heel flicks and gentle strides; then in threes run the length in three marked lanes, passing across without anyone stopping.  
**Teaching points:** pass across the body into the runner's path; hold the lanes wide even when they feel too far apart.  
**Transitions:** move to wave positions by colour.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk the pattern; advanced — limit to two passes per length.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: move in three lanes during transition.  
**Procedure:** a trio advances unopposed from the halfway line and finishes after at least two passes, each player staying in their own lane the whole way. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, in four groups.  
**Organisation:** three marked lanes per group, one ball, and a retriever behind the goal; the next trio starts only once the previous one has cleared.  
**Teaching points:** hold the width even when the ball is on the far side; run forward rather than diagonally towards the ball; pass across into the runner's path so nobody has to stop; the finisher uses the three-step rhythm from HB-05.  
**Common errors:** all three drifting towards the ball so the lanes collapse; slowing down to receive; passing behind the runner; the finish breaking down because the run was too fast to stay within the three-step rule.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes — goal: read the final defender.  
**Procedure:** three attackers advance against two defenders working at controlled pace. The ball carrier drives at a defender: if that defender commits, the pass goes to the free player; if the lane stays open, the carrier continues and finishes. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** waves of three against two, the next group waiting on the sideline; defenders work at about 60% and never make contact.  
**Teaching points:** attack the defender's inside shoulder to force a choice; scan before the decision line, not at it; release at the moment of commitment, because a pass even half a second late arrives into a closed lane.  
**Common errors:** deciding before the defenders have done anything; passing so early that the defender simply slides across; carrying into the defender and losing the ball; the free player standing still instead of running onto the pass. Keep each wave brisk: aim to release the finishing shot within about eight seconds of starting the break.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** make the role change automatic by removing every pause between waves.  
**Rules:** continuous 3v2 waves — as soon as a wave finishes, two of the attacking trio stay to defend the next wave coming the other way while a fresh trio attacks. One point for a completed finish, one bonus point when the team switched to defence within three seconds. No contact and no stealing from the hands.  
**Teaching points:** turn and run back the moment the shot leaves the hand, not after watching it; call to your partner which of you takes the ball.  
**Common errors:** watching the outcome of the shot; jogging back instead of sprinting the first three steps; both new defenders covering the same attacker.  
**Scoring:** one point for successful execution and a bonus for the correct decision; no individual ranking.  
**Variation:** wider space for foundation; time limit for advanced learners.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “In a fast break the ball should always travel faster than any defender can run — your job is to be wide enough and early enough to let it.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their width, pass timing, or role change, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** colour-coded collection and a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Action before scanning:** seen when entering congestion; correct with “scan–choose–act.”
- **Narrow spacing:** seen when two learners share one lane; correct with a marked width spot.
- **Three attackers in one lane:** seen as lost control; correct by slowing and using “three lanes.”
- **Late transition:** seen when learners stand after possession changes; correct with three immediate role-change steps.
- **Power over accuracy:** seen in repeated loss or misses; correct with a larger target and 60% force.
- **Vague feedback:** heard as “good/bad”; correct with one observable fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate at normal speed and then slowly.
- Teach accuracy and control before speed.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Give one observed fact and one correction.
- Rotate roles and protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** walk, soft ball, wide target, no pressure. **Intermediate:** light pace and one decision. **Advanced:** narrow target, short time, or weak hand. **Large group:** six zones. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** walking play. **Partial equipment:** one ball per pair. **Varied needs:** visual cue, high-contrast mark, extra time, consistent partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** performance against each of the five learning objectives in section 3 — lane width, scanning the defenders, pass timing, transition to defence, and role rotation with feedback. **Success:** at least 4 of the 5 listed objectives achieved, each per its own stated measure. **Partial:** 2–3 objectives achieved. **Not yet:** 0–1 objective, or unsafe action; return to slow, unopposed practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Which cue worked? Was movement time equal? Students: What did you see before acting? What will you change? Next lesson: select one group criterion to revisit.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** HB-08 — final game and pathway assessment. **If not:** return to the unopposed version, enlarge the target, and practise at walking pace.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important:** the fast break is one of the most productive attacking situations in handball, and it is the clearest situation in which a student must decide rather than execute: two defenders cannot cover three lanes, but only if the lanes actually exist.  
**Develops:** running width under fatigue, scanning while moving at speed, the timing of a pass released at the moment of commitment, and the discipline of changing role instantly.  
**Why not skip:** without it students treat every turnover as a rest, the advantage evaporates before it is used, and they learn to attack only against a set defence — the hardest version of the game.
