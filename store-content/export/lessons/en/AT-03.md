# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 03 — Reaction and Start to Changing Signals
**Lesson code:** AT-03-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** reaction and start
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Respond to a visual or sound signal and accelerate in balance without anticipating.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Holds the set position completely still until the signal is given, with no false start, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Reacts to the signal and takes the first step within roughly half a second, without rocking or pre-moving.
- Reads a colour or sound cue at the start and accelerates in the matching direction in 3 of 4 attempts.
- Accelerates in balance after reacting, holding the posture and arm action from AT-02 rather than stumbling out of the start.
- Observes a partner, reports one factual cue about their stillness or reaction, and states a personal goal for the next attempt.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-02 — running posture and acceleration.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-02 — running posture and acceleration.  
**Current lesson:** It removes the predictability the pathway has relied on: the start command may now be visual or auditory, may come at any moment, and in the second task also determines which direction to run. It withholds any partner or baton, so the reaction remains entirely the individual's own — which AT-04 changes with the relay exchange.  
**Next lesson:** AT-04 — relay baton exchange.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “A good start is a still start. If you are rocking, you are guessing — and a guess that comes early is a false start, not a fast one. Be still, see the signal, then push the ground away.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — ready, scan, push the ground, rise gradually — plus the rule that a false start simply means repeating the attempt, with no penalty.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing the lanes.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the legs for repeated explosive starts and wake up the reaction system before it is tested.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then partner reaction games.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add leg swings, ankling, and two build-up runs; then in pairs stand still and react to a partner's hand signal with three quick steps, alternating roles.  
**Teaching points:** stillness before the signal is part of the skill, not a delay; react to what you see, not to what you expect.  
**Transitions:** move to lanes on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — sound signal only, from standing; advanced — mix visual and sound signals.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** separate the reaction itself from the complication of choosing, by keeping the signal predictable while the body position varies.  
**Procedure:** students start from a range of positions — standing, seated, kneeling, facing sideways — on a single predictable signal, accelerating ten metres. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four lanes, one runner per lane, staggered starts, walking return outside the lanes.  
**Teaching points:** settle completely into the position before the signal; the first movement is a push against the ground, not a lift of the body; rise gradually over the first steps rather than standing straight up.  
**Common errors:** rocking or creeping before the signal; standing up immediately and losing the acceleration; a first step that is long and slow rather than short and quick; looking around instead of focusing forward.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** add a decision to the reaction, so the student must perceive before moving rather than release a stored action.  
**Procedure:** a partner shows one of two colours at the moment of the start, each colour sending the runner to a different marked lane or cone. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** trios — runner, signal giver, observer — with the signal giver standing in front at a safe distance and the two exit routes clearly separated.  
**Teaching points:** keep the set position identical whichever colour appears; look at the signal, not at the lane you are hoping for; commit fully once the colour is seen rather than half-starting both ways.  
**Common errors:** pre-committing to a favourite direction and correcting late; leaning towards the expected lane before the signal; slowing down while deciding; two runners crossing routes because the exits were not respected.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** compete against one's own previous reaction rather than against a faster classmate.  
**Rules:** Paired reaction starts in which each student's target is their own best clean start of the day. Points are earned for stillness before the signal, a correct direction, and a balanced acceleration; a false start simply cancels that attempt. No times are compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a clean start that loses the pair is still the better start; students of any speed can be paired, since each competes with themselves.  
**Common errors:** anticipating in order to “win” the pair; frustration after a false start; abandoning posture once the pressure rises.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The quickest start belongs to whoever was most still — guessing feels fast, but all it ever does is make you start again.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their stillness or reaction, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect equipment by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- Guessing the signal and false starting: control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-04 — relay baton exchange. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Reaction is the element of sprinting that transfers most directly into every game the students play — responding to a ball, an opponent, or a whistle — and unlike top speed it improves quickly with practice.  
**What develops?** The stillness that makes a genuine reaction possible, perception of a cue under time pressure, inhibition of the anticipated action, and balanced acceleration from unfamiliar body positions.  
**Why not skip?** Students never taught to wait learn instead to guess, which produces false starts in athletics and mistimed movements in every invasion game; guessing also succeeds often enough to become permanent unless it is addressed directly.
