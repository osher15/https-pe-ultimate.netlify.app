# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 01 — Passing and Receiving on the Move
**Lesson code:** HB-01-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** target passing, ready reception, and movement into a new lane
**Age:** 10–13
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 10–15 handballs or soft balls, 30 cones, and bibs
**Space:** gym or court with 4–6 passing lanes and safety zones
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Establish safe passing and receiving while moving so students can keep possession and offer support.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Delivers an overarm pass into the receiver’s hands while moving in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Shows two hands as a target before receiving the ball.
- Catches and moves into a new lane without stopping the run.
- Keeps to the three-step rule without an unnecessary dribble.
- Alternates passer and receiver roles on every rotation and names one factual cue observed in the partner's pass or catch, rather than a verdict.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No handball lesson required; students must move safely and stop on signal.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** No handball lesson required; students must move safely and stop on signal.  
**Current lesson:** As the opening handball lesson it establishes the two actions the sport is built on — the overarm pass to a target and the catch taken in stride — together with the three-step rule that governs them. It withholds the dribble entirely, so possession moves only by passing, which is what HB-02 adds.  
**Next lesson:** HB-02 — dribble, stop, and change of direction.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- Use soft balls for beginners, pass below face height, keep an arm’s length in defence, and prohibit contact or screening.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use a controllable force and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn target passing, ready reception, and movement into a new lane. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Pairs walk in lanes, show target hands, pass softly at chest height, receive in front of the body, and change side at a cone.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** In pairs, pass while walking through a lane. The receiver moves into a side gate, shows a target, controls the ball, and becomes the next passer.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** See the receiver; step and pass; show both hands; cushion the ball; move after passing.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** In triangles, pass and follow to the empty cone. Add a shadow defender who blocks one lane without attempting a steal.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** scan before acting, select free space, and reset after each attempt.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied area, late decision, and repeating without feedback.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Moving Support 3v3: five passes score one point when each passer moves to a new lane. No contact, no stealing from the hands, and three seconds maximum while holding. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—larger target and no defender; intermediate—smaller target; advanced—one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Catch the ball already moving and pass it where your team-mate is going — in handball the ball should always travel faster than any of us can run.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Passing from the elbow:** no range and no accuracy; correct by leading with the elbow, bringing the hand behind the head, and following through to the finish.
- **Catching with rigid fingers:** the ball is dropped or jams the fingertips; correct with soft hands that meet the ball and draw it to the body.
- **Stopping after the catch:** the movement breaks down; correct by catching in stride and exiting into a free lane.
- **More than three steps:** a travelling violation; correct by counting three steps aloud during practice.
- Passing too hard at short range: the receiver cannot control it and risks finger injury; correct with a shorter distance and a chest-height pass.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Have students pause all equipment before you speak.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without increasing contact.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, ball size, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** performance against each of the five learning objectives in section 3 — pass accuracy, target hands, catch-and-move into a new lane, the three-step rule, and role alternation with feedback.  
**Success:** at least 4 of the 5 listed objectives achieved, each per its own stated measure.  
**Partial:** 2–3 objectives achieved.  
**Not yet:** 0–1 objective, or unsafe action; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before HB-02 — dribble, stop, and change of direction.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to HB-02 — dribble, stop, and change of direction.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Handball is played almost entirely by passing, so a class that cannot catch on the move and release accurately cannot play the game at all, however fit or willing its students are.  
**What develops?** The overarm throwing pattern led by the elbow, with the hand behind the head, soft hands that absorb the ball rather than block it, running and catching as one action, and early awareness of the three-step limit.  
**Why not skip?** Students who cannot catch safely at speed protect themselves by stopping, and the whole game slows to a static circle; hard passes over short distances are also a common cause of jammed fingers, and the soft-hands catch taught here reduces that risk.
