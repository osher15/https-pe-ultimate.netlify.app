# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Volleyball 03 — Underhand Serve to a Target Zone
**Lesson code:** VB-03-EN
**Sport:** Volleyball
**Subtopic:** underhand serve and accuracy
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 balls, net or rope, 30 cones, bibs
**Space:** gym or flat court with four small courts
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Send a safe underhand serve over a low net into a target zone.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Strikes the ball with a firm, straight arm below waist height, off a held or lightly dropped ball rather than a toss, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Stands with the opposite foot forward and steps towards the target as the arm swings, transferring weight onto the front foot, in 3 of 4 serves.
- Places the serve inside the marked target zone in 3 of 5 attempts.
- Sends the serve over the rope or low net so it travels roughly 4–6 metres, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Checks that the service and target zones are clear and the retriever is ready before every serve, and serves only on the teacher's signal, keeping two arm lengths from the next server, in 5 of 5 serves.
- Takes serving, retrieving, and observing roles in turn and names one factual cue about a partner's stance or swing.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
VB-02 — overhead set and support.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** VB-02 — overhead set and support.  
**Current lesson:** It adds the contact that begins every rally: instead of receiving a feed from a partner, the student now puts the ball into play alone, over a net, to a chosen zone. It withholds receiving that serve — the ball lands in a target area and nobody plays it back — which VB-04 adds.  
**Next lesson:** VB-04 — forearm serve reception.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, timing, accuracy. **Social:** communication and turns. **Cognitive:** reading flight and deciding. **Tactical:** support and coverage. **Emotional:** confidence and recovery.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four 8×4 m courts with adapted net/rope. Spare balls secured. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4; groups of 5–8 rotate every 60–90 seconds.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, net anchors, and balls.
- Never hang on or cross under the net.
- Keep two arm lengths and stay out of adjacent courts.
- Enter the ball path only after calling.
- Retrieve loose balls after scanning.
- Use a soft ball and low net when needed.
- Stop for pain, dizziness, or collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “A serve is the one contact nobody can rush you on. Hold the ball still in front of your hip, swing a straight arm like a pendulum, and step towards where you want it to land.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — ball in front of the hip, straight arm, step to the target — plus the absolute rule that nobody serves until the signal and nobody crosses to collect until it is called.  
**Organisation:** class behind a single serving line, balls held still.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** mobilise the shoulder for repeated swinging and rehearse the serving action without sending a ball anywhere.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then shadow swings, then held-ball contacts.  
**Instructions:** jog with direction changes on a colour call; add full arm circles forwards and backwards; then stand with the opposite foot forward and shadow-swing the straight arm ten times, stepping through each time; finally hold the ball on the non-hitting palm and tap it gently off that hand.  
**Teaching points:** the holding hand stays still and low — it is a tee, not a toss.  
**Transitions:** move to courts on the colour signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — larger softer ball held on a cone; advanced — add a small drop before contact.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** groove the swing and contact point with the ball held still, so nothing depends on a toss.  
**Procedure:** students strike a ball held on the flat non-hitting palm towards a floor target, with no net at this stage. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs spread along a line all facing the same direction, one ball per pair, a marked floor target 4–5 metres ahead.  
**Teaching points:** opposite foot forward; hold the ball at waist height in front of the hip; swing the straight arm back and through like a pendulum; contact the bottom of the ball with a firm hand; step onto the front foot as you strike.  
**Common errors:** tossing the ball up instead of holding it; bending the elbow so the ball is pushed rather than struck; contacting the side of the ball, which sends it sideways; standing square with no step.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** add height and distance while accuracy remains the measure.  
**Procedure:** students serve over a rope or low net from three marked distances, starting closest. Three successes into the target zone earn a step back; a miss means stepping in again. Roles rotate every minute.  
**Organisation:** one server per court at a time, a retriever standing to the side of the target zone, and an observer watching one cue; all serving happens on the teacher's signal.  
**Teaching points:** contact lower on the ball to gain height rather than swinging harder; aim at a zone, not at the whole court; keep the same swing at every distance.  
**Common errors:** adding force instead of adjusting the contact point, so the serve flies long; dropping the head at contact; serving before the signal; the retriever standing inside the target zone.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** serve under the mild pressure of a scoring context while accuracy still decides the points.  
**Rules:** Target game — teams serve in rotation into marked zones of different values, the smallest zone furthest away. Every rally starts with a serve; a serve into the net simply passes to the next server with no penalty. A bonus point is earned for calling the intended zone before serving and then hitting it.  
**Scoring:** one point for execution plus a decision bonus; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** choose the zone you can actually reach; the same swing scores more than a harder one.  
**Common errors:** aiming at the highest-value zone every time; speeding up the swing under pressure; forgetting to call the zone.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The serve is the one contact in volleyball that nobody can interfere with — no block, no opponent — so a slow one that lands in your zone is worth far more than a fast one that hits the net.”  
**Learning summary:** each student names one piece of evidence about their stance, swing, or contact point, plus one next step.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** balls collected by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Contact before position:** ball behind body; use “feet before hands.”
- **Eyes only on ball:** collision risk; call and scan.
- **Tossing the ball too high:** loss of control; slow down and reset cues.
- **Power over direction:** ball travels long; near target and 60% force.
- **Serving without checking the zone:** risk to the retriever; wait for the signal and confirm the target zone is clear.
- **Vague feedback:** give one fact and one suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate front and side, normal then slow.
- Teach body path before contact.
- Pair visual and sound cues.
- Increase distance after three successes.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Protect equal movement time.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** catch–throw, soft ball, low net. **Intermediate:** continuous contact. **Advanced:** small target or short time. **Large group:** six courts. **Small group:** 2v2. **Limited space:** short court. **Partial equipment:** one ball per triad and rope. **Varied needs:** visual cue, strong markings, extra time, fixed partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** stance and the ball-in-front-of-hip release, the straight-arm contact point, the step and weight transfer, net clearance and target-zone accuracy, and the safety check. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to soft-ball catch–throw.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Did everyone contact the ball? Students: What did you see and where did you move? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** VB-04 — forearm serve reception. **If not:** return to catch, shorten distance, and lower net.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The serve is the only closed skill in volleyball — nobody interferes with it — so it is the fairest chance in the sport for any student to succeed, and no rally can begin without one.  
**What develops?** A repeatable pendulum swing from a stable stance, weight transfer towards a chosen target, calibration of force to distance, and the self-discipline of serving only on a signal.  
**Why not skip?** A class that cannot serve cannot start a rally, so games stall before they begin and the lesson becomes a queue; students who invent a serve also tend to throw the ball up and swing hard, which is both inaccurate and the least safe action on a shared court.
