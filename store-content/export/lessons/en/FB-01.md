# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Football 01 — Ball Control and Dribbling into Space
**Lesson code:** FB-01-EN
**Sport:** Football
**Subtopic:** close touches, scanning, and dribbling toward free space
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 12–16 footballs or soft balls, 30 cones, and bibs
**Space:** field or gym divided into 4 grids with safety channels
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Develop close ball control while walking and jogging so students can recognise and enter free space safely.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Dribbles with close touches and keeps the ball within one step's distance in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Looks up at least twice per route and selects free space.
- Stops the ball with the sole on the signal within two seconds.
- Changes direction without entering another student’s area.
- Takes both performer and observer roles in every rotation and names one factual cue seen in the partner's touches, rather than a verdict.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
No football lesson is required; students must stop on signal and move safely.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** No football lesson is required; students must stop on signal and move safely.  
**Current lesson:** As the opening football lesson it adds the first relationship with the ball at the feet: close inside- and outside-foot touches, the sole stop, and the forward look that finds free space. It withholds passing to a team-mate entirely — the ball stays with one student for every task — which is what FB-02 introduces.  
**Next lesson:** FB-02 — pass, receive, and support movement.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- No tackling in this lesson; loose balls are retrieved by walking; students keep one-way routes.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use controlled force and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn close touches, scanning, and dribbling toward free space. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Students move without balls through gates, stop the sole on a marker, turn, and scan; then repeat while rolling a ball slowly.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** In lanes, use inside and outside touches to move the ball over ten metres, stop it under the sole, look up, and return outside the lane.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** Small touches; ball within one step; look up between touches; use both sides of the foot; slow before traffic.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** In a gate grid, dribble to a free colour, stop before the gate, scan, and choose another free colour. Add a passive shadow partner at advanced level.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** scan before acting, select free space, and reset after each attempt.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied area, late decision, and repeating without feedback.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Open Gate Challenge: points for entering a free gate under control, looking up, and stopping the ball inside the zone. Speed and total gates entered do not rank students. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—larger target and no defender; intermediate—smaller target; advanced—one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Keep the ball close enough to touch again in one step, and your eyes stay free to find the space — that is what control means.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Touches too strong:** the ball escapes ahead of the player; correct with short inside-foot touches at walking pace.
- **Eyes down:** the space is never scanned; correct with a brief forward look every two touches.
- **Dribbling into occupied space:** collisions or loss of the ball; correct by choosing the direction before the next touch.
- **Stopping with the toe:** the ball bobbles away; correct by stopping with the sole and a firm ankle.
- **Upright posture on the turn:** the change of direction is slow; correct with soft knees and a lower centre of gravity.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without increasing contact.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, ball size, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** close ball control while dribbling, scanning before choosing a direction, the sole stop on signal, changing direction without entering another student's space, and taking both the performer and observer roles with factual feedback.  
**Success:** at least four of the five measures are shown consistently.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for scanning or the observer role.  
**Not yet successful:** ball control or safe spacing remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before FB-02 — pass, receive, and support movement.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to FB-02 — pass, receive, and support movement.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Every later football skill assumes the ball can be moved without being watched; close control is what frees the eyes, and free eyes are what make passing, turning, and finishing possible.  
**What develops?** Fine foot–ball coordination on both sides of the foot, the sole stop as a reliable brake, visual scanning while moving, and early judgement about which space is safe to enter.  
**Why not skip?** Students who begin with games instead of control kick the ball ahead and chase it; that pattern rewards the fastest runner rather than the best player, and it excludes everyone who is not quick.
