# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 04 — Safe Relay Baton Exchange
**Lesson code:** AT-04-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** relay exchange
**Age:** 10–13
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Exchange a baton inside a marked zone with matched speed and clear communication.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Receives the baton inside the marked exchange zone, with no exchange happening before or beyond it, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Holds the receiving hand still and steady behind the hip, palm open and fixed, until the baton is placed in it.
- Calls the agreed word at the right moment, so the outgoing runner starts neither too early nor too late, in 3 of 4 exchanges.
- Keeps running forward through and after the exchange, without stopping, slowing sharply, or turning to look back.
- Observes a partner, reports one factual cue about their hand position or call, and states a personal goal for the next exchange.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-03 — reaction and start.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-03 — reaction and start.  
**Current lesson:** It turns the individual sprinting of AT-01 to AT-03 into a shared task: two runners must now match speeds, communicate, and move a baton between them inside a marked zone. It withholds competitive timing entirely — exchanges are scored for safety and accuracy, never for how fast a team finishes — and it withholds the endurance pacing AT-05 introduces.  
**Next lesson:** AT-05 — personal endurance pacing.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “The baton travels faster than any of us, so nobody should ever slow down to hand it over. Hold your hand still, listen for the call, and keep running forward.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — call, stable hand, pass forward, continue in your lane — plus the rule that nobody turns around to look for the baton.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle beside one marked exchange zone.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare shoulders and legs for running while reaching behind, and rehearse the hand position before any baton moves at speed.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then pairs walking.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add arm swings and leg swings; then walk in pairs, one behind the other, practising the receiving hand held still behind the hip while the partner places the baton into it.  
**Teaching points:** the receiving hand is a stationary target, not something that reaches back and grabs.  
**Transitions:** move to lanes on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — stationary exchange from standing; advanced — exchange at a jog.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** build the exchange pattern at a speed where nothing can go wrong.  
**Procedure:** pairs complete exchanges inside a marked zone, first walking and then jogging, the incoming runner calling and the outgoing runner holding a still hand. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four lanes, one pair active per lane, exchange zone marked at both ends, walking return outside the lanes.  
**Teaching points:** the outgoing runner looks forward, never back; the receiving hand stays open and still behind the hip; the incoming runner places the baton rather than throwing it; both keep moving forward through the exchange.  
**Common errors:** turning the head to look for the baton; the receiving hand waving or reaching; the incoming runner slowing to make the pass easier; the exchange happening outside the marked zone.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** solve the real problem of a relay — that two runners travelling at different speeds must briefly travel at the same one.  
**Procedure:** on a straight lane the outgoing runner starts when the incoming runner reaches a marked check point, and the pair adjusts that mark until the exchange happens comfortably in the middle of the zone. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** pairs per lane with a movable check-point cone they reposition themselves; an observer records where the exchange actually occurred.  
**Teaching points:** if the exchange happens early, move the check mark closer; if it happens late or outside the zone, move it further away; the outgoing runner accelerates properly rather than jogging and waiting.  
**Common errors:** the outgoing runner starting too late and being caught immediately; starting too early and running away from the baton; never adjusting the mark after a failed exchange; both runners watching each other instead of looking forward.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** run a genuine relay in which the reward is a clean exchange rather than a fast finish.  
**Rules:** Cooperative relay in teams of four; a team earns a point for every exchange completed inside the zone with a still hand, a clear call, and no turning of the head. A dropped baton is simply picked up and the relay continues. No times are recorded and no team is declared the winner.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a team with three clean exchanges has done the lesson, in whatever order they finish; call loudly enough to be heard over the other lanes.  
**Common errors:** speed creeping up until exchanges fall apart; the call disappearing once it starts to feel like a race; runners stopping immediately after handing over, in the path of the next lane.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A relay is not four sprints — it is three exchanges with running in between, and the exchanges are what you actually win or lose.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their hand position or call, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect batons and cones by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Stopping suddenly during exchange:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-05 — personal endurance pacing. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** The relay is the only genuinely cooperative event in athletics, and the exchange rather than the running is what decides it — it is also where students who are not the fastest discover they can be the most valuable.  
**What develops?** Matching speed with another person, a stable receiving hand while moving, timing a start from a visual check point, and the trust required to run forward while something arrives from behind.  
**Why not skip?** Untaught exchanges mean students turn around, stop, or snatch at the baton, which is slow and is also the most common cause of collisions and falls in school relays.
