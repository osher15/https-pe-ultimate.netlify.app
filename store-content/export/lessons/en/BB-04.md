# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Basketball 04 — Close-Range Shooting and Safe Rebounding
**Lesson code:** BB-04-EN
**Sport:** Basketball
**Subtopic:** balanced set shot, target focus, and safe rebound timing
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 10–15 basketballs or soft balls, 20 cones, bibs, and 2–4 baskets or wall targets
**Space:** court with marked shooting lanes and rebound waiting zones
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Build a repeatable close-range shot and safe rebound routine before introducing shots under pressure.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Shoots from close range with the shooting hand, the guide hand only supporting the ball, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Fixes the eyes on the backboard or rim before releasing the ball.
- Lands balanced on two feet in the same spot after the shot.
- Waits for the rebound outside the shooter’s landing area.
- Alternates shooter and retriever roles on every attempt and gives one factual comment on balance, target focus, or landing.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
BB-03 — pass, receive, and move to support.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** BB-03 — pass, receive, and move to support.  
**Current lesson:** It adds the first attempt on the basket: BB-03 ended each sequence with a controlled catch, and this lesson turns that catch into a balanced close-range shot plus the rebound routine that follows it. It withholds any defender and any shot on the move — students shoot stationary from three fixed close spots — which later lessons build on.  
**Next lesson:** BB-05 — off-ball movement and passing lanes.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- One ball moves in each shooting lane; nobody stands under the basket; do not chase a rebound into another lane.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use a controllable force and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn balanced set shot, target focus, and safe rebound timing. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Students rehearse balanced stops, ball-to-target movement, wrist follow-through, and two-foot landing without shooting, then add gentle self-tosses.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** From three close spots, shoot after a balanced catch. Hold the follow-through until the ball reaches the target; retrieve only after the shooter leaves the lane.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** Feet ready; eyes on target; extend smoothly; land balanced; rebound after contact and exit sideways.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** In pairs, one passes and one shoots; the passer becomes a rebounder only after the ball touches the rim, backboard, or wall. Secure with two hands, chin the ball, and exit to the side.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** wait outside the shooter’s landing space until the ball touches the rim or board; take the ball with two hands and pull it to the chin, elbows out; leave to the side, never through the shooting lane.  
**Common errors:** moving in before the ball touches the rim, grabbing with one hand, and turning back into the shooter’s path.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** Quality Shooting Ladder: one point for balance, one for a safe release lane, and one for a two-hand rebound and side exit. Makes are recorded separately and do not decide success. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—larger target and no defender; intermediate—smaller target; advanced—one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Shoot the same way every time and land where you started — a shot you can repeat is worth more than one that happens to go in.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Two-handed push:** the ball is thrown rather than released; correct with a shooting hand and a guide hand that only supports.
- **Eyes on the ball:** the target is never scanned; correct by fixing on a point on the backboard before release.
- **Unbalanced landing:** the student falls forward under the basket; correct by jumping and landing on two feet in the same spot.
- **Entering the rebound early:** collisions under the basket; correct by waiting outside the zone until the ball touches the rim.
- **Excessive force:** the ball clears the backboard; correct with a shorter distance and power generated from the legs.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without increasing contact.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, ball size, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** preparation, execution, target adjustment, spacing, and explanation.  
**Success:** all five measures are shown consistently and the student explains one cue.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for preparation or decision.  
**Not yet successful:** spacing or force remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before BB-05 — off-ball movement and passing lanes.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to BB-05 — off-ball movement and passing lanes.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Close-range shooting is where effort becomes a score, and the rebound routine around it is the main safety control under a basket — the most congested area of any court.  
**What develops?** A repeatable release with one shooting hand, visual focus on a fixed target, balanced take-off and landing, and the timing discipline to wait outside the shooter's landing space.  
**Why not skip?** Students who never build a stable shape shoot two-handed from the chest and keep that pattern for years; and a class that has not rehearsed rebound timing collides under the basket as soon as shots are contested.
