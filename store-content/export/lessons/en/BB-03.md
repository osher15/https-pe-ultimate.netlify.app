# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Basketball 03 — Pass, Receive, and Move to Support
**Lesson code:** BB-03-EN
**Sport:** Basketball
**Subtopic:** accurate passing, ready reception, and movement to a new support angle
**Age:** 9–11
**Experience:** beginner
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–30 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 10–15 basketballs or soft balls, 30 cones, and bibs
**Space:** gym or court with 4–6 passing lanes
**Complexity:** foundation
# 2. Overall purpose
Connect passing and receiving to immediate support movement so possession does not stop after the ball leaves the hands.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Delivers a chest pass into the receiver’s hands in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Shows two hands as a target before the ball is released.
- Moves to a new support angle within two seconds of passing.
- Catches with soft hands and controls the ball before the next pass.
- Calls the receiver’s name before releasing the ball and gives one factual comment naming a cue that was seen, not a verdict.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
BB-02 — controlled stop and change of direction.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** BB-02 — controlled stop and change of direction.  
**Current lesson:** It moves the ball between players for the first time: BB-01 and BB-02 kept the ball in one student's hands, and this lesson adds the chest pass, the ready catch, and the run to a new support angle straight after release. It withholds shooting entirely — every sequence ends in a controlled catch, not a score — which is what BB-04 adds.  
**Next lesson:** BB-04 — close-range shooting and safe rebounding.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, balance, control, and accuracy.  
**Social:** communication, turn-taking, and support.  
**Cognitive:** observation, decision, and self-regulation.  
**Tactical:** use of space, support, and transition.  
**Emotional:** confidence, persistence, and recovery after error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Prepare 4–6 parallel work zones with spare equipment outside the active area. Mark a waiting line, a one-way return route, and a teacher demonstration zone.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios, no more than 5–6 students per zone, one task card and a central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, footwear, and fixed goals or benches before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and use only the marked travel direction.
- Start, stop, and rotate only on the teacher signal.
- Keep fingers away from faces, use chest-height passes, and increase distance only after control.
- Retrieve loose equipment by walking after checking the route.
- Use a controllable force and select the foundation option when accuracy falls.
- Stop and report sharp pain, dizziness, collision, or unusual breathlessness.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we learn accurate passing, ready reception, and movement to a new support angle. Success means control, preparation, and safe use of space—not speed.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, boundaries, stop signal, and success marker.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare the relevant joints, movement pattern, and visual attention.  
**Structure:** pairs or individual movement inside marked zones.  
**Instructions:** Pairs move between cones, show target hands, make a soft chest pass, follow the pass, and stop in balance.  
**Transitions:** rotate by walking on signal; equipment is held still during explanation.  
**Variations:** reduce distance and pace; advanced learners add a late visual cue.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central technique under stable conditions.  
**Procedure:** In triangular groups, pass to a target, call the receiver’s name, and move to the empty cone. Begin without a defender, then add a shadow defender who closes one lane.  
**Organisation:** pairs or trios; performer and observer rotate every minute.  
**Teaching points:** Show a target; step toward the pass; receive with soft hands; move after release; create a triangle.  
**Common errors:** rushing, poor preparation, and using excessive force.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill with a simple decision.  
**Procedure:** Play 3v1 keep-ball in a marked square. A point is earned only when the passer moves to a new support angle and the receiver controls the ball before the next pass.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 students per zone; one active attempt at a time.  
**Teaching points:** scan before acting, select free space, and reset after each attempt.  
**Common errors:** entering an occupied area, late decision, and repeating without feedback.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** connect technique, decision, and cooperation without elimination.  
**Rules:** 3v3 end-zone possession: five controlled passes, or a pass received in the end zone, scores one. No stealing from hands and no body contact. The full-stop signal overrides scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 process points for control, decision, and safety; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—larger target and no defender; intermediate—smaller target; advanced—one extra decision cue.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The pass is not finished when the ball leaves your hands — it is finished when you have moved somewhere new and useful.”  
**Summary:** each student names one successful cue and one next adjustment.  
**Feedback:** teacher comments on control, spacing, and decisions.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower effort by walking, collect by zone, and complete a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Passing without a target:** the ball arrives at the feet or above the head; correct by showing two hands at chest height before the pass.
- **Standing still after passing:** the passer freezes and the attack stops; correct with three steps to a free angle immediately after release.
- **Catching with stiff hands:** the ball bounces out of the hands; correct by catching with soft elbows and drawing the ball to the body.
- **Excessive force on the pass:** the receiver cannot control it; correct with a shorter distance and a bounce pass.
- **Passing into a blocked lane:** a defender stands in the line; correct by scanning before release and choosing the free receiver.
- **Vague feedback:** observer says only “good”; correct with “I saw…; next try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation version from two angles.
- Coach one observable cue at a time.
- Reward control and decisions rather than speed or power.
- Keep practice turns short and equitable.
- Pause all equipment before speaking.
- Ask for evidence before giving the answer.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** shorter distance, slower pace, larger target, and softer equipment.  
**Intermediate:** standard distance and one decision cue.  
**Advanced:** smaller target, weaker side, or later cue without increasing contact.  
**Large group:** duplicate simple zones and use performer–observer–recorder trios.  
**Small group:** fewer zones and more feedback cycles.  
**Limited space:** stationary practice and short one-way routes.  
**Partial equipment:** one item per pair; observer rehearses movement without equipment.  
**Different needs:** adapt position, range, distance, pace, ball size, role, and communication; allow seated or fixed-support participation.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** preparation, execution, target adjustment, spacing, and explanation.  
**Success:** all five measures are shown consistently and the student explains one cue.  
**Partial success:** safe execution but a prompt is needed for preparation or decision.  
**Not yet successful:** spacing or force remains unsafe after adaptation; return to isolated foundation practice.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who prepared early? Did the organisation create enough practice? Which adaptation improved control?  
**Student questions:** What cue helped? What changed after feedback? What will you repeat next time?  
**Improvement:** adjust one distance, target, group ratio, or cue before BB-04 — close-range shooting and safe rebounding.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to BB-04 — close-range shooting and safe rebounding.  
**If not:** repeat the foundation action with no decision pressure, one success cue, and a larger target.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Passing is the first genuinely cooperative act in the sport: it is where a class stops being a set of individuals with balls and starts playing as a team.  
**What develops?** Accuracy and force calibration over distance, soft-hand catching, the scan that finds a free receiver, and the habit of moving immediately after release instead of admiring the pass.  
**Why not skip?** Without the move-after-pass habit, students freeze where they stand, and every later small-sided game collapses into a static circle of throwing rather than an attack.
