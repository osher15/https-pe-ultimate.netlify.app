# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Handball 09 — Width, Depth, and Line Support in 4v4 Handball
**Lesson code:** HB-09-EN
**Sport:** Handball
**Subtopic:** creating passing lanes and non-contact line-player support
**Age:** 13–16
**Experience:** intermediate–advanced
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** 8–12 handballs or soft balls, 30 cones, bibs, and two goals or four cone goals
**Space:** gym or court divided into two short courts with safety zones
**Complexity:** advanced
# 2. Overall purpose
Extend team play after the foundation assessment by using width, depth, and line-player support in 4v4.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Occupies a different channel from the nearest team-mate, so the attack holds at least two of the three channels, in 4 of 5 possessions.
- Offers an angle rather than a straight line to the ball carrier, and shoulder-checks before every reception.
- Provides depth as the fourth attacker, supporting behind the ball rather than level with it, in 3 of 4 possessions.
- Plays the line-support role without screening, changing angle into free space while keeping an arm's length from every defender.
- Recovers behind the marked line within three seconds of losing possession, and explains one team decision using something actually seen in the game.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Basic pass, catch, off-ball movement, non-contact defence, and transition. **Previous lesson:** HB-08 — final applied game and foundation pathway assessment.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** HB-08 — final applied game and foundation pathway assessment; the foundation pathway assessment was completed.  
**Current lesson:** It reopens the pathway after the HB-08 assessment and adds team shape: width across three channels, depth behind the ball, and a line player who supports by changing angle rather than screening. It withholds the students' own tactical planning and officiating — the teacher still sets the principle and the rules — which HB-10 hands over to them.  
**Next lesson:** HB-10 — tactical game management and extended final assessment.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** passing, catching, changing direction, and stopping.  
**Social:** communication, role sharing, and fair officiating.  
**Cognitive:** scanning, planning, risk selection, and reflection.  
**Tactical:** width, depth, passing lanes, and attack–defence transition.  
**Emotional:** responsibility, confidence, and recovery from error.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark two short courts, three channels on each, a recovery line, and a waiting area outside the sideline. Keep spare balls in a container.  
**Setup:** 6 minutes. **Pack-down:** 4 minutes.  
**Organisation:** teams of four, one bib colour per team, one game on each half, and observer or official roles for the waiting team.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, goals, balls, and footwear; anchor regulation goals.
- No pushing, holding, body screening, or contact with the throwing arm.
- Keep an arm’s length in defence and stay outside the goal area.
- Throw only when the lane and goal are clear; use a soft ball in restricted space.
- Start, stop, and change courts only on the teacher signal.
- Retrieve an out-of-bounds ball by walking after checking the area.
- Stop and report pain, dizziness, or a collision.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we examine creating passing lanes and non-contact line-player support. The goal is a safe team decision, not speed or power.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, non-contact rule, and stop signal.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** prepare shoulders, hands, stopping, and scanning.  
**Structure:** pairs with one ball in a channel.  
**Instructions:** pass while moving, balanced stop, change angle, and recover on signal.  
**Transitions:** change role and direction every 40 seconds.  
**Variations:** soft ball and shorter range; advanced students add a shadow defender.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** isolate the tactical principle.  
**Procedure:** In groups of three with a line player near a marked line, two outside players pass and move while the line player changes angle without screening. Rotate after three passes. Begin with a shadow defender, then add an active non-contact defender.  
**Organisation:** groups of three or four in a 10×12 metre zone.  
**Teaching points:** stretch the court; offer an angle, not a straight line; scan before receiving; the line player moves into free space; recover immediately after loss.  
**Common errors:** crowding the ball, receiving while stationary, and defending with hands on the attacker.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the principle with advantage and transition.  
**Procedure:** Play 4v3 across three channels. Attack must occupy at least two channels while one player provides depth. After a turnover, all players recover behind a marked line before defending.  
**Organisation:** short games; teacher feeds a new ball after it leaves.  
**Teaching points:** scan before receiving, pass to safe space, and recover immediately.  
**Common errors:** all players on one line, chasing the ball, and delayed transition.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** make independent choices in a small-sided game.  
**Rules:** 4v4 to goals or cone goals. A goal scores one point; add a process point when the possession shows width, depth support, and a pass to a third player. Body screens and contact are not allowed. Every student changes role at least once.  
**Scoring:** outcome and process points are recorded separately; no individual ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation — 3v3 and one pass before shooting; intermediate — 4v4; advanced — three-second decision limit.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “If two of you are standing in the same channel, one of you is not playing — stretch the court and the passing lane appears without anyone having to force it.”  
**Summary:** each team states one item of evidence and one principle to retain.  
**Feedback:** movement, decision, non-contact play, and fairness.  
**Orderly exit:** balls to container, bibs by colour, cones by zone, and head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Crowding:** everyone moves near the ball; no passing lane is visible; correct by occupying at least two channels.
- **Receiving without scanning:** eyes remain on the ball; correct with a shoulder check before reception.
- **Passing to a marked player:** the pass is intercepted; correct with a feint, new angle, or third player.
- **Contact defence:** hands touch the attacker; correct with arm’s-length space and lane closure.
- **Slow transition:** players argue or stand after loss; correct with a team cue and immediate recovery.
- **Unsafe shot:** ball travels through a group; correct by stopping, scanning, and choosing another goal.
# 11. Teaching points
- Coach one principle at each stoppage.
- Freeze a situation briefly to reveal passing lanes.
- Reward movement and decisions, not only goals.
- Keep substitutions short and equitable.
- **Ask:** “Where is the width?”, “Who provides depth?”, “What happens after loss?”
- Stop contact immediately and restart after a clear correction.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** 3v2, wider area, soft ball, and no dribble.  
**Intermediate:** 4v3 or 4v4 with a process rule.  
**Advanced:** shorter decision time or a team-selected tactical target.  
**Large group:** two courts and active officiating/observation roles.  
**Small group:** 3v3 with a neutral support player.  
**Limited space:** cone goals, no powerful shots, and walking transitions.  
**Partial equipment:** one ball per court and bibs as markers.  
**Different needs:** adapt ball size, distance, speed, role, and pass method; provide a fixed support player or safe zone.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** passing lane, decision, transition, non-contact defence, and explanation.  
**Success:** shows four measures consistently and explains one item of evidence.  
**Partial success:** succeeds in practice but needs a prompt in the game.  
**Not yet:** repeated contact, poor spacing, or unsafe choice; return to numerical advantage and one rule.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Did the space create decisions? Did every student touch the ball and change role? Where did contact occur?  
**Students:** Which movement opened a lane? What happened after loss? Which piece of evidence will you act on?  
**Improvement:** adjust court size, process rule, or game time before HB-10 — tactical game management and extended final assessment.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to HB-10 — tactical game management and extended final assessment.  
**If not:** return to 3v2, channel movement, and shadow defence before full play.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is where individual technique becomes team shape: the same four players occupying three channels rather than one create passing lanes without any increase in skill or speed.  
**What develops?** Reading and occupying space across a full court, supporting with depth as well as width, the non-contact line-player role, and the responsibility to recover instantly when possession is lost.  
**Why not skip?** Without taught shape the play collapses around the ball, the strongest two students take every possession, and everyone else learns that handball is a game they watch from inside a crowd.
