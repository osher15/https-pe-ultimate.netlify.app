# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 10 — Final Multi-Event Assessment
**Lesson code:** AT-10-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** application and pathway assessment
**Age:** 13–16
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Show observable improvement in run, jump, and throw and explain the next personal goal.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Demonstrates the key technical cue of each event — running posture, jumping take-off and landing, throwing stance and transfer — in both measured attempts, in 4 of 5 cases.
- Uses the feedback given between the two attempts to change something specific in the second attempt.
- Records their own results accurately and compares them only against their own earlier scores in the unit.
- Follows every safety routine unprompted — lane discipline, throwing signal, landing-zone clearance — throughout the assessment.
- States one personal strength and one next goal, each supported by evidence from the lesson rather than a general impression.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-09 — combined athletics stations.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-09 — combined athletics stations.  
**Current lesson:** It teaches nothing new and instead gathers evidence across all three disciplines, giving each student two measured attempts per event with coaching in between, so that improvement rather than raw result is what gets recorded. It withholds no further content in this pathway — what it leaves to an advanced unit is competitive events, standardised measurement, and event specialisation.  
**Next lesson:** Advanced pathway or personal improvement unit.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “You get two attempts at everything today, and the second one is the one I care about — because what I am measuring is whether you can take feedback and change something.”  
**Purpose:** make the assessment criteria transparent before any attempt, and explain the prepare–perform–inspect–adjust cycle.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle at the centre of the station layout, criteria displayed or read aloud.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the body for all three events and give every student a final rehearsal before anything is recorded.  
**Structure:** individual movement covering all three patterns.  
**Instructions:** jog with direction changes on a colour call; add arm circles and trunk rotations, ankle bounces and half-squats, and two build-up runs; then take one unrecorded practice attempt at each of the three events.  
**Teaching points:** this is the moment to ask about a cue you are unsure of — nothing here is being recorded.  
**Transitions:** move between events on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — shorter distances and lighter implements, with the same criteria; advanced — full distances.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** put one clear cue back in each student's mind before the measured attempts begin.  
**Procedure:** three review stations — running posture and arm action, jumping take-off and landing, throwing stance and weight transfer — with each student choosing the event they feel least confident in and working there longest. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback.  
**Organisation:** three stations with cue cards, the throwing station well separated from the others, nothing recorded at this stage.  
**Teaching points:** choose the weakest event, not the favourite; one cue per event is enough to carry into the assessment.  
**Common errors:** practising only the strongest event; rushing the review to reach the measured attempts; ignoring the cue card.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** measure improvement rather than ability, by giving specific feedback between two attempts at each event.  
**Procedure:** each student takes a first measured attempt at each event, receives one specific piece of feedback from a partner or the teacher, then takes a second attempt having tried to change that one thing. Both attempts are recorded. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** three event areas each with a recorder, personal record cards, and the teacher circulating to give feedback where a partner cannot.  
**Teaching points:** the feedback must name one change, not a list; the second attempt is judged on whether the change was attempted, not only on the result; record honestly, since the card belongs to the student.  
**Common errors:** vague feedback such as “do it better”; changing three things at once in attempt two; recording an optimistic result; treating a lower second result as failure when the technique actually improved.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** close the unit with a festival atmosphere in which every student can score well regardless of how fast or far they go.  
**Rules:** Personal festival across the three events, scoring one point for a held technical cue, one for an unprompted safety routine, and one for any improvement on the student's own earlier score in the unit. Results are never compared between students and nobody is ranked.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** a student who improves on their own earlier score scores exactly as well as anybody else who does; celebrate improvement out loud.  
**Common errors:** drifting back into comparison with friends; safety routines slipping as the atmosphere lifts; students who improved least becoming discouraged, which the scoring exists to prevent.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “What I am writing down is that you changed something after being told one thing — and that is the skill that stays useful long after you stop measuring how far you can throw.”  
**Learning summary:** each student records one strength and one next goal, supported by evidence from their own card.  
**Feedback:** the teacher gives brief individual feedback and links the evidence to the next unit; no public ranking of any kind.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, dismantle stations in the assigned order, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Judging success only by result:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** Advanced pathway or personal improvement unit. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It is the only lesson in which the teacher can see whether the unit's habits survive without coaching, and its two-attempt design measures coachability — the ability to take feedback and change — rather than inherited athleticism.  
**What develops?** Independent application of all three disciplines, constructive response to specific feedback, honest self-recording, and goal-setting grounded in evidence rather than impression.  
**Why not skip?** Athletics assessed only on times and distances rewards early physical maturity and tells students nothing they did not already know; this design records what actually changed across the unit, which is the only fair measure of learning in a mixed-ability class.
