# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Fitness 09 — Personal Improvement Plan and Peer Coaching
**Lesson code:** FIT-09-EN
**Sport:** Physical fitness
**Subtopic:** setting a goal, selecting an action, and evidence-based feedback
**Age:** 12–16
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, 6–10 mats, stable benches or a wall, timer, and task cards
**Space:** gym, shaded court, or flat marked area
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Build a short measurable fitness goal and practise it through precise, safe peer feedback.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Writes one measurable fitness goal with a named measure and an observable success criterion, rather than a general intention.
- Selects one familiar station and one action that directly serves that goal, and explains the link between them.
- Observes a partner against their chosen measure and gives feedback naming what was seen, in the form “I saw… so try…”, while holding spacing and the stop signal.
- Changes exactly one variable per coaching cycle and records whether the result actually moved.
- Compares results only with their own earlier attempt, never with a partner's.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
Students need the stop signal, the 1–5 effort scale, and the basic exercises taught earlier in the pathway. **Previous lesson:** FIT-08 — cardiorespiratory endurance and the talk test.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** FIT-08 — cardiorespiratory endurance and the talk test; students practised load selection and movement quality.  
**Current lesson:** It turns the self-regulation of the whole pathway into a plan the student owns: they now set one measurable goal, choose an action that serves it, and coach a partner against their measure across three cycles. It withholds the integrated assessment itself — the goal is practised here rather than examined — which FIT-10 completes.  
**Next lesson:** FIT-10 — integrated challenge and pathway assessment.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** posture, coordination, strength, and endurance.  
**Social:** turn-taking, listening, and respectful feedback.  
**Cognitive:** planning, observation, recording, and revision.  
**Tactical:** effort distribution, station order, and space management.  
**Emotional:** independence, persistence, and decisions without peer comparison.
# 7. Equipment and space
Mark four to six one-way work zones, leaving a transition lane and a recovery area. Keep spare equipment outside the active space.  
**Setup:** 5 minutes. **Pack-down:** 3 minutes.  
**Recommended organisation:** pairs or trios, 4–6 students per station, one card per station, and one central timer.
# 8. Safety
- Check the surface, mats, benches, and footwear before activity.
- Keep two arm lengths and move only in the marked direction.
- Start and rotate only on the teacher’s signal.
- Choose a version that permits control; do not work to failure.
- Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, unusual breathlessness, or loss of balance.
- Do not jump over equipment or collect it while another group is active.
- Water and rest are available without justification; never force pace or make public comparisons.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today we will practise setting a goal, selecting an action, and evidence-based feedback. Success means a sound choice, controlled performance, and an explanation based on evidence.”  
**Purpose:** establish the goal, effort scale, stop signal, and travel lanes.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes. **Purpose:** raise heart rate gradually and prepare the joints.  
**Structure:** free movement between cones, 30 seconds per action.  
**Instructions:** brisk walk, lateral steps, partial squat, wall push, knee lift, and single-leg balance.  
**Transitions:** walk to the next action on the audio signal.  
**Variations:** reduce range or pace; advanced students add a controlled direction change.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes. **Purpose:** learn the central quality marker and choice.  
**Procedure:** The teacher presents three possible goals: movement quality, consistent pace, or recovery. Each student chooses one goal, one measure, and one familiar station, then writes a success criterion that can be observed.  
**Organisation:** pairs; performer and observer switch every minute.  
**Teaching points:** One goal; one measure; factual feedback; one small change; compare only with yourself.  
**Common errors:** starting too fast, holding breath, and feedback that does not name evidence.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 12 minutes. **Purpose:** apply the skill across rounds and make an independent adjustment.  
**Procedure:** Complete three three-minute coaching cycles in pairs: perform, observe one measure, give factual feedback, and revise. In each cycle the student changes only one variable and records the outcome.  
**Organisation:** 4–6 per station; 45 seconds work and 30 seconds transition or recovery as required.  
**Teaching points:** change one variable only, keep movement clean, and enter the recovery lane immediately.  
**Common errors:** selecting excessive difficulty, shortening recovery, and valuing quantity over quality.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 8 minutes. **Purpose:** make decisions under time without turning the lesson into a fitness contest.  
**Rules:** Improvement Lab: one point for a measurable goal, one for feedback that names evidence, and one for a revision that improves the result. Starting fitness level is not scored. The full-stop signal overrides all scoring.  
**Scoring:** up to 3 points per round for planning, quality, and regulation; no points for speed or student ranking.  
**Variations:** foundation—two stations; intermediate—four; advanced—add an explanation or recovery plan.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “A goal you cannot measure is only a wish — name the number, name what your partner should watch for, then change one thing at a time.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one choice and one piece of evidence that prompted an adjustment.  
**Feedback:** the teacher comments on decisions, safety, and quality.  
**Orderly exit:** stop, lower heart rate by walking, collect by zone, and leave after head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Excessive load:** identify loss of alignment or abrupt stopping; correct with an easier version or shorter interval.
- **Fast opening pace:** identify a sharp decline in round one; correct by beginning at effort 3 of 5.
- **Breath holding:** identify facial tension or inability to speak; correct by exhaling during effort.
- **Uncontrolled range:** identify momentum or knees collapsing inward; correct by reducing range and adding a clear end point.
- **Unsafe transition:** identify crossing an active station; correct with a one-way lane and signal-only rotation.
- **Unhelpful feedback:** identify “good” without evidence; correct with “I saw… so try…”.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate the foundation option before every progression.
- Use language of quality, choice, and regulation rather than winning.
- Check technique before increasing duration or difficulty.
- Give clear ten-second, stop, and rotate signals.
- Permit water and rest without penalty.
- Ask “What evidence led to that choice?” before giving the solution.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** 20–25 seconds work, smaller range, and wall support.  
**Intermediate:** 30–40 seconds and a controlled full version.  
**Advanced:** independent planning, evidence-based explanation, or longer work without quality loss.  
**Large group:** trios with performer, observer, recorder; duplicate simple stations.  
**Small group:** fewer stations and more feedback rounds.  
**Limited space:** stationary actions and a short one-way walking route.  
**Partial equipment:** use a wall, court lines, and body weight; a mat is not essential at every station.  
**Students with different needs:** adapt position, range, time, pace, role, and communication; allow seated performance or a consistent partner.
# 13. Assessment
**Teacher checks:** choice, technique, regulation, safety, and evidence-based explanation.  
**Success:** plans, performs safely, adjusts one variable, and explains why.  
**Partial success:** performs safely but needs a prompt to choose or explain.  
**Not yet successful:** continues despite risk cues, loses spacing, or cannot perform the foundation version after adaptation; stop and return to individual instruction.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher questions:** Who selected a suitable load? Who changed in response to evidence? Were transitions and work–recovery ratios safe?  
**Student questions:** What did you plan? What happened? Which adjustment will you retain?  
**Next improvement:** change one station, one timing ratio, or one feedback prompt before FIT-10 — integrated challenge and pathway assessment.
# 15. Continuity
**If outcomes are met:** proceed to FIT-10 — integrated challenge and pathway assessment.  
**If not:** return to the foundation option, a short round with one measure, and guided choice before repeating the lesson.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why is it important?** It is where fitness stops being something done to students and becomes something they direct: a goal they chose, a measure they named, and a partner coaching them against it.  
**What does it develop?** Writing an observable success criterion, coaching language grounded in evidence rather than encouragement, systematic one-variable revision, and the habit of measuring progress against oneself.  
**Why not skip it?** Without a plan of their own, students finish a fitness unit fitter but no more able to train independently; the goal-setting and coaching cycle is the part that transfers beyond the ten lessons.
