# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 02 — Running Posture, Arm Action, and Acceleration
**Lesson code:** AT-02-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** running technique and acceleration
**Age:** 10–12
**Experience:** developing
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Maintain tall posture and forward–back arm action during a 20-metre acceleration.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Holds a tall running posture with the hips underneath the shoulders through the full 20 metres, in 4 of 5 runs.
- Drives the elbows backwards in a forward–back action beside the body, with the hands never crossing the midline, in most runs.
- Lengthens the stride gradually rather than over-striding from the first step, matching effort to the 20-metre distance — shown by being able to breathe and talk without excessive difficulty by the end of the acceleration — in 3 of 4 runs.
- Runs in the assigned lane, starts only on the signal, and keeps two arm lengths from the runner ahead.
- Observes a partner, reports one factual cue about their posture or arm action, and states a personal goal for the next run.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-01 — standing start and short acceleration.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-01 — standing start and short acceleration.  
**Current lesson:** It refines what AT-01 began: the start position is now assumed rather than taught, and attention moves to what the body does during the run — a tall posture, an efficient forward–back arm action, and a stride that lengthens progressively over 20 metres. It withholds any unpredictable start signal, since every run begins on a single known command, which AT-03 changes.  
**Next lesson:** AT-03 — reaction and start to changing signals.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Fast running is tall running. Keep your hips under your shoulders, drive your elbows back rather than across, and let your steps get longer on their own — do not reach for them.”  
**Purpose:** state the three cues — tall body, elbows back, steps lengthen gradually — plus the one-runner-per-lane rule and the walking return route.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle facing the lanes.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** raise the pulse gradually and rehearse the running shape at low speed before any effort is asked for.  
**Structure:** individual movement in lanes.  
**Instructions:** walk, then march with high knees, then ankling over short marks, then practise the arm action standing still with the elbows fixed at a right angle; finish with two relaxed build-ups over ten metres at roughly half effort, walking back outside the lanes.  
**Teaching points:** the arm action is rehearsed standing before it is used running; nothing in this warm-up is a race.  
**Transitions:** move between drills on the signal, walking.  
**Variations:** foundation — walking only with arm action; advanced — add a third build-up at three-quarter effort.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** fix posture and stride rhythm using floor marks that make the pattern external and visible.  
**Procedure:** students run over evenly spaced floor marks that set a consistent stride rhythm, holding a tall posture throughout. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four lanes with marks at a comfortable stride length, one runner per lane, a partner observing one cue from the side.  
**Teaching points:** run tall with the hips under the shoulders rather than leaning back; hit the marks without reaching or chopping; eyes on a target at the far end, never on the feet; shoulders relaxed and hands loose.  
**Common errors:** looking down, which drops the chest and drifts out of lane; over-striding to reach the marks; tensing the shoulders and clenching the fists; leaning back as speed builds.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** hold the technique together while the speed rises towards a real acceleration.  
**Procedure:** runners complete 15–20 metre accelerations at building effort while a partner observes one nominated cue and reports it immediately afterwards. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** pairs per lane — runner and observer — with a marked run-off zone beyond the finish and a walking return outside the lanes.  
**Teaching points:** build speed across the distance rather than exploding and fading; the arms set the leg rhythm, so drive them back deliberately; stay tall as the effort rises.  
**Common errors:** sprinting flat out from the first step and losing all form; arms crossing the body once tired; stopping sharply at the end instead of running through; observers watching everything rather than the one agreed cue.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** keep the technique intact under the mild excitement of a team task, with quality rather than time as the measure.  
**Rules:** Technique relay in teams of four; each runner completes a 20-metre leg and the team earns points for posture, arm action, and a controlled finish, judged by a rotating observer. No times are taken and no team is declared fastest.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** the relay rewards the team whose fourth runner still looks like the first; slow down slightly if form is breaking.  
**Common errors:** racing the neighbouring lane and abandoning the cues; the last runners rushing; celebrating by stopping abruptly at the line.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The fastest runner in this class is not the one who strains hardest — it is the one who stays tall and relaxed when everyone else is straining.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their posture or arm action, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect equipment by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Arms crossing the body:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-03 — reaction and start to changing signals. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Running is the most common movement in physical education and the least often taught; a tall posture and an efficient arm action make every student faster and less tired without any gain in fitness.  
**What develops?** Postural control at speed, a forward–back arm action that sets the leg rhythm, the patience to let stride length build rather than reaching for it, and the ability to observe technique in a partner.  
**Why not skip?** Students who never learn running mechanics simply strain harder, which tightens the shoulders, shortens the stride, and persuades slower students that speed is fixed at birth rather than something they can improve.
