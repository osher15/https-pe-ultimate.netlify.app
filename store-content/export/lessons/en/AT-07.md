# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 07 — Approach Run and Jump to a Zone
**Lesson code:** AT-07-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** approach rhythm and take-off
**Age:** 11–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Link a short approach to one-foot take-off and safe two-foot landing on a mat or zone.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Runs a consistent 5–7 stride approach that arrives at the take-off mark without stuttering or stretching, in 4 of 5 attempts.
- Takes off from one foot at the marked board or line, without over-running it or braking before it.
- Lands on two feet inside the marked zone with bent knees, holding the landing under control, in 4 of 5 jumps.
- Keeps the eyes forward through the approach and take-off rather than looking down to find the take-off mark.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's approach rhythm or landing and states a personal goal for the next jump.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-06 — standing long jump.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-06 — standing long jump.  
**Current lesson:** It adds the run-up AT-06 withheld: the student now carries speed into a one-footed take-off and must still land on two feet under control. It withholds any measured competition for distance and any hard landing surface — jumps go into a wide zone or onto mats, and accuracy of take-off is what scores — while AT-08 turns to throwing.  
**Next lesson:** AT-08 — soft-ball distance throw.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “The run-up is the jump. If your last three steps are even and your eyes stay up, the take-off happens by itself — if you look down to find the line, you stop jumping and start stepping.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — even rhythm, eyes forward, firm take-off, soft landing — plus the rule that only one jumper approaches at a time.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle beside one approach lane and landing zone.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** prepare the legs for a one-footed take-off at speed and rehearse the approach rhythm without jumping.  
**Structure:** individual movement, then approach walk-throughs.  
**Instructions:** jog and change direction on a colour call; add ankle bounces, leg swings, and two build-up runs; then walk and jog the 5–7 stride approach repeatedly without taking off, counting the strides aloud.  
**Teaching points:** count the approach so it becomes repeatable; the last two steps are quick, not long.  
**Transitions:** move to approach lanes on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — three-stride approach at walking pace; advanced — seven strides at running pace.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** establish a repeatable approach before any jump is attempted, since that is what makes the take-off consistent.  
**Procedure:** students run their 5–7 stride approach and step over the take-off mark without jumping, repeating until the same foot lands on the mark every time and adjusting the start point until it does. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of paired feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** four approach lanes, one runner at a time, a partner watching where the take-off foot actually lands and moving the start cone accordingly.  
**Teaching points:** adjust the start of the run, never the last stride; count the strides every time; run through the mark rather than at it; eyes on a target ahead, not on the ground.  
**Common errors:** stuttering or chopping the last steps to hit the mark; stretching for the mark and losing speed; changing the count between runs; looking down and losing posture.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** add the jump now the approach is reliable, with a wide zone so distance never pressures the landing.  
**Procedure:** students run the rehearsed approach, take off from one foot at the mark, and land on two feet inside a wide marked zone or mat, holding the landing. Roles change every minute.  
**Organisation:** two approach lanes onto mats or a soft zone, a landing judge at the side, and a clear waiting line; only one jumper in the air at a time.  
**Teaching points:** drive the free knee up at take-off; keep the chest up rather than diving forward; bring both feet together before landing; bend the knees to absorb.  
**Common errors:** landing on one foot; over-running the mark because the approach was rushed; leaning forward and landing on the hands; the next jumper starting before the zone is clear.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** finish with accuracy of take-off, rather than distance, as the thing being measured.  
**Rules:** Each student jumps in their own lane, scoring points for taking off within the marked take-off area, for an even approach rhythm, and for a held two-footed landing. A long jump that took off before or beyond the area scores nothing. No distances are compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** an accurate take-off is worth more here than a big jump, because in athletics a foul jump does not count at all; keep the same approach every time.  
**Common errors:** lengthening the approach to gain distance and missing the mark; abandoning the stride count under pressure; landing poorly while reaching for extra distance.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “The jump is decided before you leave the ground — same run-up, same count, eyes up, and the take-off looks after itself.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their approach rhythm or landing, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk to lower the pulse, collect mats and cones by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Chopping steps before take-off:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-08 — soft-ball distance throw. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** It joins the two halves of jumping — running speed and a controlled take-off — and teaches that consistency of approach, not effort at the board, is what produces a good jump.  
**What develops?** A repeatable stride pattern under acceleration, converting horizontal speed into height, one-footed take-off strength, and a two-footed landing that stays safe even when arriving fast.  
**Why not skip?** Students jumping from a free run-up stutter, foul, or take off from the wrong foot, which is ineffective and increases the risk of a twisted ankle at take-off; a counted approach addresses all three at once.
