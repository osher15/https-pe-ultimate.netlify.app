# 1. Lesson identity
**Full title:** Athletics 05 — Personal Endurance Pacing
**Lesson code:** AT-05-EN
**Sport:** Athletics
**Subtopic:** pace and effort regulation
**Age:** 11–14
**Experience:** intermediate
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Group:** 20–32 students of all genders; adapt by ability and need
**Equipment:** cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, tape
**Space:** gym, court, or flat track
**Complexity:** intermediate
# 2. Overall purpose
Hold a steady personal pace for 6–8 minutes and rate effort honestly.
# 3. Observable learning objectives
- Holds a steady pace for 6–8 minutes without stopping, finishing the final lap within a few seconds of the first, in 4 of 5 rounds.
- Maintains a pace at which a short sentence can still be spoken, and checks this honestly when asked.
- Rates their own effort on the agreed scale in a way that matches what the teacher observes, in 3 of 4 checks.
- Starts at a sustainable pace rather than sprinting from the line, keeping a reserve for the final minutes.
- Reports one factual cue about a partner's pacing or breathing and states a personal goal for the next round.
# 4. Required prior knowledge
AT-04 — relay exchange.
# 5. Position in the learning pathway
**Previous lesson:** AT-04 — relay exchange.  
**Current lesson:** It changes the question from how fast to how long: after four lessons built on short maximal efforts, students now choose a pace they can hold for 6–8 minutes and learn to judge their own effort honestly. It withholds all jumping and throwing events, and all competition between students, since every target here is personal — AT-06 turns to take-off and landing.  
**Next lesson:** AT-06 — standing long jump.
# 6. Lesson contribution
**Motor:** coordination, posture, power, endurance. **Social:** turns and feedback. **Cognitive:** regulation and decisions. **Tactical:** pace and space planning. **Emotional:** persistence and confidence.
# 7. Equipment and space
Four lanes and stations; cones, floor marks, batons, soft balls, mats, and tape as needed. Setup 6 minutes; pack-down 4.
# 8. Safety
- Check surface, equipment, and footwear.
- Keep fixed lanes and travel direction.
- Start only on signal and with clear space.
- Collect equipment after a full stop.
- Keep two arm lengths.
- Match load to ability; stop for pain or dizziness.
- No public comparison or forced pace.
# 9. Lesson sequence
## Opening
**Duration:** 3 minutes.  
**Teacher says:** “Today nobody is racing anybody. The only person you are running against is the version of you that goes off too fast in the first minute and walks in the last one. Start easy and keep something back.”  
**Purpose:** state the cues — start easy, rhythmic breathing, check your effort, keep a reserve — and explain the talk test and the effort scale.  
**Organisation:** class in a half-circle beside the running route.
## Warm-up
**Duration:** 7 minutes.  
**Purpose:** raise the pulse gradually so the main run can begin at a sustainable pace rather than from cold.  
**Structure:** individual movement around the route.  
**Instructions:** walk one lap, then jog one lap easily, adding arm swings, leg swings, and ankle circles; finish with 30 seconds at the pace the student intends to hold, to feel it before committing to it.  
**Teaching points:** the warm-up pace should already feel too easy — that is precisely the point; breathe in rhythm with the steps.  
**Transitions:** move between laps on the signal.  
**Variations:** foundation — walk and jog intervals; advanced — add a second pace-preview effort.
## Main activity A
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** find, by direct experiment, the pace at which each student can still speak.  
**Procedure:** students run 60-second rounds at a pace they believe they can sustain; at the end of each round a partner asks a simple question and the runner must answer in a full short sentence. If they cannot, the next round is slower. Work 40 seconds, then 20 seconds of feedback, repeated four times.  
**Organisation:** pairs alternating runner and questioner, on a marked loop with a clear one-way direction.  
**Teaching points:** the talk test is the most reliable pacing tool a student owns and needs no equipment; a pace that feels easy in minute one is usually about right; breathing should be rhythmic, not ragged.  
**Common errors:** running at a partner's pace rather than one's own; gasping a one-word answer and claiming to have passed; treating a slower pace as failure; starting each round faster than the last.
## Main activity B
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** turn a felt pace into an objective, repeatable one the student can actually hear.  
**Procedure:** a continuous 6–8 minute run in which a partner calls the time at the end of each lap, so the runner can hear whether their laps are even. Roles change after the run.  
**Organisation:** a marked loop with the teacher or a partner calling split times; runners spread out so nobody is tempted to chase.  
**Teaching points:** aim for laps that sound the same, not laps that get faster or slower; adjust by small amounts, since a large correction usually overshoots; a slightly slow first lap is far easier to fix than a fast one.  
**Common errors:** setting off with the fastest group and fading; speeding up every time a split is called; stopping when the pace slips instead of easing slightly; lapping partners and then racing them.
## Applied game
**Duration:** 10 minutes.  
**Purpose:** test pacing directly, with evenness rather than speed as the measure of success.  
**Rules:** Pacing challenge — each student runs a set number of laps and scores points for how closely the final lap matches the first. A student who runs every lap slowly and evenly scores higher than one who runs fast and then fades. Times are never compared between students.  
**Scoring:** quality and decision points only; no individual ranking.  
**Teaching points:** this is the one challenge in the unit where running slower can score more; use the talk test throughout.  
**Common errors:** sprinting the last lap to feel strong, which loses the challenge; comparing lap times with neighbours; abandoning the plan the moment someone goes past.
## Closure
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Key message:** “Anyone can start fast. Finishing at the pace you started is the actual skill — and it is what makes running feel possible instead of punishing.”  
**Learning summary:** each student states one piece of evidence about their pacing or effort rating, plus their next personal goal.  
**Feedback:** partners exchange one observed fact and one suggestion.  
**Orderly exit:** walk a full lap to lower the pulse, collect equipment by zone, then a head count.
# 10. Common errors
- **Speed before technique:** posture is lost; slow and restore one cue.
- **Eyes down:** lane drift; use a forward target.
- **Starting too fast:** control drops; break the action down and rebuild.
- **Unmatched effort:** speech is difficult or recovery slow; reduce pace.
- **Entering another lane:** near collision; restore one-way travel.
- **Vague feedback:** replace with one fact and suggestion.
# 11. Teaching points
- Demonstrate normally and then slowly.
- Teach quality before speed or result.
- Pair visual and sound signals.
- Allow a repeat after feedback.
- Observe one learner for 45–60 seconds.
- Compare each learner only with self.
# 12. Adaptations
**Foundation:** short distance, walking pace, wide target. **Intermediate:** full pace and one decision. **Advanced:** time, precision, or complex sequence. **Large group:** six lanes. **Small group:** pairs. **Limited space:** short stations. **Partial equipment:** share in pairs. **Varied needs:** visual mark, extra time, fixed partner, adapted range.
# 13. Assessment
**Checks:** technique, control, regulation, safety, independence. **Success:** 4–5 criteria. **Partial:** 2–3 with prompt. **Not yet:** 0–1 or unsafe; return to foundation version.
# 14. Reflection
**Teacher:** Who acted independently? Was load appropriate? Students: What evidence shows improvement and what is next? Next: revisit one criterion.
# 15. Continuity
**If met:** AT-06 — standing long jump. **If not:** return to foundation version, reduce distance or load, and model again.

# 19. Pedagogical value
**Why important?** Pacing is the difference between endurance running becoming a lifelong activity and remaining a punishment; it is also the single factor that most determines whether a student decides they are “bad at running”.  
**What develops?** The ability to read one's own effort and breathing accurately, the self-discipline to start slower than the group, honest self-rating, and the experience of finishing a run strongly rather than merely surviving it.  
**Why not skip?** Students never taught to pace set off with the fastest child in the class, fade within two minutes, and conclude they lack fitness; the real deficit is a pacing strategy, and it can be taught in a single lesson.
